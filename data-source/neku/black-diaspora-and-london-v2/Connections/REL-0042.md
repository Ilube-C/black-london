# Ladipo Solanke and Amy Ashwood Garvey

*Ladipo Solanke and Amy Ashwood Garvey founded the Nigerian Progress Union in London in July 1924.*

In 1924 [Ladipo Solanke](../People/PER-0011.md), a Nigerian law student, wrote letters to the press protesting at reports about Nigeria. Hakim Adi writes that [Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md) saw them and wrote to congratulate him. "They became friends and, largely due to Amy Ashwood Garvey's enthusiasm, went on to form the Nigerian Progress Union."[^1] The [Union](../Organisations/ORG-0007.md) was founded by thirteen Nigerian students in London in July 1924, with Solanke as its leading figure.[^1] Adi and Sherwood write that he founded it "with the assistance of" Ashwood Garvey.[^2]

## Significance

Adi writes that the philosophy behind the Union "owed as much to the ideas of Marcus Garvey as it did to those of his estranged wife". From then on, he adds, Solanke was "destined to play a major role in West African student politics until his death in 1958", above all through the [West African Students' Union](../Organisations/ORG-0018.md) he founded the next year.[^3]
