# C. L. R. James and Paul Robeson

*Paul Robeson played the title role in C. L. R. James's play Toussaint Louverture in March 1936, and James later sought his view of The Black Jacobins.*

[Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md) took the title role in [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md)'s play [*Toussaint Louverture*](../Works/WRK-0014.md). The Stage Society staged it at the [Westminster Theatre](../Places/PLC-0049.md) on 15 and 16 March 1936 (see [the production](../Events/EVT-0018.md)).[^1] Adi and Sherwood write that the play was staged in 1936 "with Paul Robeson leading the cast".[^2] Høgsbjerg notes that it was the only time Robeson starred in a play by a writer of African heritage.[^3] Two years later James asked Robeson, as he asked [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md), what he thought of [*The Black Jacobins*](../Works/WRK-0015.md). James recalled that both told him: "James, I always knew the history was there, that we had it."[^4]

## Significance

Høgsbjerg writes that the production "was the first time black professional actors had ever performed on the British stage in a play written by a black playwright".[^5]
