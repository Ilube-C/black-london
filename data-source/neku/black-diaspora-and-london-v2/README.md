# The Black Diaspora and London

*Second edition, November 2026*

An atlas of twenty-five people of African descent and the London places, organisations, events and writings that connect them, from the baptism of Olaudah Equiano at St Margaret's, Westminster, in 1759 to the radical Black book fairs of the 1980s.

## Contents

- [People](People/README.md) — 25 entries. Twenty-five people of African descent whose lives connect with London, from the eighteenth century to the late twentieth.
- [Places](Places/README.md) — 68 entries. The buildings, streets and districts where the people in the atlas lived, worked, met and campaigned. Full entries tell the story of the major meeting places; shorter entries record the rest.
- [Organisations](Organisations/README.md) — 26 entries. The societies, unions, committees, colleges and publishers through which the people in the atlas organised.
- [Events](Events/README.md) — 26 entries. Conferences, congresses, trials, performances, demonstrations and visits, from a baptism in 1759 to a book fair in 1982.
- [Works](Works/README.md) — 25 entries. Books, plays, music, journals, speeches and broadcasts by the people in the atlas, with links to texts that can be read online.
- [Connections](Connections/README.md) — 91 entries. Every documented link between a person and another person, place, organisation, event or work. Each page says how the link is documented and why it matters.
- [Source library](Sources/README.md) — 110 sources on four shelves.
- [How this atlas was made](METHODOLOGY.md)
- [Corrections since the first edition](CORRECTIONS.md)
- [Data](data/SCHEMA.md) — the records behind the atlas, as JSON and CSV.
- [Website](website/index.html) — the same atlas as a single web page that works offline.
