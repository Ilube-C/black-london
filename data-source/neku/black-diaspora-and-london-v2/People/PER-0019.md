## At a glance
Paul Leroy Robeson (9 April 1898 – 23 January 1976) was an African American bass-baritone, actor, and anti-imperialist activist who made his home in London in the late 1920s and 1930s, returned to live in Britain in 1958, and began a long semi-retirement in the United States in 1963.[^1] Residing at [The Chestnuts](../Places/PLC-0064.md) on Branch Hill, Hampstead, he starred in [*Othello*](../Events/EVT-0020.md) in London in 1930 and studied Swahili and phonetics at the [School of Oriental Studies](../Organisations/ORG-0026.md).[^2][^1][^3]

## Life and significance
Born in Princeton, New Jersey, in 1898, Robeson first appeared on the British stage in 1922, returned in 1925 to star in *Emperor Jones*, and in 1928 sang 'Ol' Man River' in the London production of *Show Boat*; in the late 1920s and 1930s he and his family made their home in London.[^1] Duberman places *Show Boat* in "the vast [Drury Lane](../Places/PLC-0062.md) theater", where Robeson also gave a celebrated matinee concert on 3 July 1928.[^4] In 1929–30 the Robesons lived at The Chestnuts, 1 Branch Hill, Hampstead.[^2] In 1930 he gave his first, much acclaimed performance as Othello.[^1] Duberman describes rehearsals from early April and a gala opening night, with Peggy Ashcroft as Desdemona; his notes cite London reviews dated 20 May 1930.[^4] Morrison's study of the production shows that, after plans to open at the Globe had been dropped, it opened at the [Savoy Theatre](../Places/PLC-0063.md) on 19 May 1930.[^5]

In 1934 he enrolled at the School of Oriental Studies, then at Finsbury Circus,[^6] to study Swahili and phonetics;[^3] Adi and Sherwood describe the course as linguistics and African languages at London University.[^1] He was active in London organisations including the League of Coloured Peoples and the International African Service Bureau, and the [West African Students' Union](../Organisations/ORG-0018.md) made him one of its patrons.[^1] In 1936 he took the lead in C. L. R. James's play [*Toussaint Louverture*](../Works/WRK-0014.md).[^1] The Stage Society staged it at the [Westminster Theatre](../Places/PLC-0049.md) on 15 and 16 March 1936, with Robeson in the title role.[^7]

In 1957, while still without a passport, he broadcast live by telephone link to a large gathering in [St Pancras Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0059.md);[^1] Duberman puts the crowd at about a thousand.[^4] When his passport was returned in 1958 he came back to Britain, and his performances included St Paul's Cathedral, the Eisteddfod at Ebbw Vale and *Othello* at Stratford; by the early 1960s the strain of his life and the persecution he had suffered were taking their toll, and in 1963 he began a long period of semi-retirement in the United States.[^1] Soon after his return he gave a sold-out formal concert at the [Albert Hall](../Places/PLC-0066.md), with Larry Brown as his accompanist.[^4]

## Connections

- **[Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md)** — Jomo Kenyatta and Paul Robeson met during the filming of Sanders of the River in 1934–35 and became better acquainted, visiting each other's flats.[^8]
- **[C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md)** — Paul Robeson played the title role in C. L. R. James's play Toussaint Louverture in March 1936, and James later sought his view of The Black Jacobins.[^9]
- **[Claudia Jones](../People/PER-0018.md)** — Claudia Jones and Paul Robeson were friends and colleagues; in London her West Indian Gazette held receptions for Robeson in 1959 and featured him at its anniversary concert in 1960.[^10]
- **[The Chestnuts, Branch Hill](../Places/PLC-0064.md)** — Paul Robeson lived at The Chestnuts, 1 Branch Hill, Hampstead, in 1929–30.[^11]
- **[Savoy Theatre](../Places/PLC-0063.md)** — Paul Robeson played Othello at the Savoy Theatre, opening on 19 May 1930.[^12]
- **[School of Oriental Studies (SOAS)](../Organisations/ORG-0026.md)** — Paul Robeson enrolled at the School of Oriental Studies in 1934 to study Swahili and phonetics.[^13]
- **[Staging of Toussaint Louverture](../Events/EVT-0018.md)** — Paul Robeson played the title role in the Stage Society's production of C. L. R. James's Toussaint Louverture in March 1936.[^14]

## Evidence notes
1. **Where SOAS was in 1934:** When Robeson enrolled, SOAS was housed at Finsbury Circus, in the former London Institution building that it had occupied since 1917; it later moved temporarily to Vandon House in Victoria before settling in Bloomsbury.[^6]
2. **The 1930 *Othello*:** Duberman names neither the theatre nor the date of the opening night, though his notes cite reviews dated 20 May 1930.[^4] Morrison places the first night at the Savoy Theatre on 19 May 1930, and both are given here on his authority.[^5]
