## At a glance
Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela (1918–2013) was a South African anti-apartheid revolutionary, [African National Congress](../Organisations/ORG-0002.md) leader, and first democratically elected President of South Africa (1994–1999).[^1] His London history runs from his clandestine visit of June 1962 to his address in [Westminster Hall](../Places/PLC-0004.md) and his visit to [Brixton](../Places/PLC-0006.md) in 1996; he also spoke at [South Africa House](../Places/PLC-0005.md) on an occasion that has not been dated.[^2][^3][^4]

## Life and significance
Nelson Mandela made his first [visit to London in June 1962](../Events/EVT-0005.md), which Gordon Brown recalled in the Commons as "his first visit to London".[^3] The Nelson Mandela Foundation records that, having left South Africa secretly in January 1962, he travelled around Africa and "visited England to gain support for the armed struggle", returning home in July 1962, when he was arrested at a police roadblock.[^1]

Mandela visited Parliament in July 1996.[^5] On [11 July 1996](../Events/EVT-0006.md), Mandela delivered an [official address](../Works/WRK-0002.md) to both Houses of Parliament assembled in Westminster Hall.[^2] Reflecting on two centuries of British involvement in Southern Africa, Mandela told Parliament: *"Perhaps the fact that we are here today might serve to close a circle which is two hundred years old."*[^2]

Mandela [visited Brixton](../Events/EVT-0007.md) in 1996; in the Commons tributes of December 2013 a local MP spoke of constituents "who celebrated and still remember Mandela's visit in 1996".[^4] In the same debate the MP Nigel Evans recalled hearing Mandela speak in South Africa House, on Trafalgar Square, on an occasion he did not date.[^3]

## Connections

- **[Oliver Tambo](../People/PER-0020.md)** — Nelson Mandela and Oliver Tambo met in London in June 1962, when Mandela visited the city during his months abroad.[^6]
- **[Nelson Mandela's Clandestine Visit](../Events/EVT-0005.md)** — Nelson Mandela spent about ten days in London in June 1962, his first visit to the city, weeks before his arrest in South Africa.[^7]
- **[Address in Westminster Hall](../Events/EVT-0006.md)** — Nelson Mandela, as President of South Africa, addressed both Houses of Parliament in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996.[^8]
- **[Westminster Hall](../Places/PLC-0004.md)** — Nelson Mandela spoke in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996, when he addressed both Houses of Parliament.[^9]
- **[Address in Westminster Hall](../Works/WRK-0002.md)** — Nelson Mandela delivered the Address in Westminster Hall to both Houses of Parliament on 11 July 1996.[^10]
- **[Brixton](../Places/PLC-0006.md)** — Nelson Mandela visited Brixton in 1996.[^11]
- **[South Africa House](../Places/PLC-0005.md)** — Nelson Mandela spoke at South Africa House on Trafalgar Square, according to an MP's recollection; the date is not recorded.[^12]

## Evidence notes
1. **The South Africa House speech:** Nigel Evans's recollection places Mandela inside South Africa House but gives no date.[^3] In her speech of thanks in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996, the Speaker told Mandela that the next day he would "walk across Trafalgar Square to South Africa House", which suggests, but does not prove, that Evans heard him there during the state visit.[^13]
2. **Brixton:** The 1996 visit is attested in the Commons tributes of 2013;[^4] no contemporary report of the visit has been examined, so its day and itinerary are not given.
