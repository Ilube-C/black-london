## At a glance
Claudia Jones (21 February 1915 – 24 December 1964) was a Trinidadian-born Communist theorist, Black feminist, and journalist who spent the final nine years of her life in political exile in London (1955–1964).[^1][^2] Operating from [250 Brixton Road](../Places/PLC-0058.md), she founded the *[West Indian Gazette](../Works/WRK-0022.md)* (1958), and in January 1959 launched the indoor Caribbean Carnival at [St Pancras Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0059.md).[^1]

## Life and significance
Born Claudia Cumberbatch in Port of Spain, Trinidad, on 21 February 1915, Jones was deported from the United States to Britain in December 1955, having never been granted US citizenship because of her communist affiliation; she arrived in London seriously ill.[^2] In her farewell statement she named the Smith Act and the McCarran Act as the laws under which she was deported.[^3] In 1958, working from 250 Brixton Road in Lambeth, she launched the *West Indian Gazette*,[^1] a monthly paper that she published and edited.[^2]

After the anti-Black riots of 1958 and the killing of Kelso Cochrane on a London street in 1959, Jones organised resistance outside the Communist Party, forming groups and coalitions and organising meetings, marches, demonstrations and petitions; the *Gazette* office served as an advice bureau and a forum for visiting Black political figures.[^2] In Notting Hill she was general secretary of the Association for the Advancement of Coloured People, founded by [Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md) and in existence by September 1958, when a meeting on the riots was held at Ashwood Garvey's home at 1 Bassett Road.[^3] She then formed the [Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations](../Organisations/ORG-0025.md) (CAACO), which campaigned against racial discrimination and immigration restrictions;[^2] Sherwood's records of its work begin in June 1963.[^3] She organised the first indoor London Caribbean Carnival at St Pancras Town Hall on 30 January 1959, partly televised.[^1] She died in her sleep on 24 December 1964,[^2] and her ashes were interred at [Highgate Cemetery](../Places/PLC-0061.md).[^3]

## Connections

- **[Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md)** — Claudia Jones and Paul Robeson were friends and colleagues; in London her West Indian Gazette held receptions for Robeson in 1959 and featured him at its anniversary concert in 1960.[^4]
- **[Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey and Claudia Jones were friends and colleagues in London; Jones was general secretary of Ashwood Garvey's Association for the Advancement of Coloured People, founded in 1958.[^5]
- **[West Indian Gazette](../Works/WRK-0022.md)** — Claudia Jones founded the West Indian Gazette in London in 1958 and published and edited it.[^6]
- **[Caribbean Carnival](../Events/EVT-0019.md)** — Claudia Jones organised the indoor Caribbean Carnival held at St Pancras Town Hall on 30 January 1959, sponsored by her West Indian Gazette.[^7]
- **[West Indian Gazette Office](../Places/PLC-0058.md)** — Claudia Jones's newspaper, the West Indian Gazette, had its offices at 250 Brixton Road for most of its life.[^8]

## Evidence notes
1. **The indoor carnival and Notting Hill:** Jones's carnivals were held indoors, every year from 1959 until her death,[^2] and the place recorded here is the venue of the first. Sherwood traces the outdoor Notting Hill festival to a Notting Hill Fair parade organised by Rhaune Laslett in 1965, after Jones's death, which drew on the carnival network Jones had built, and calls Jones the festival's founding spirit.[^3]
2. **January or February:** English Heritage and Sherwood's 1999 biography both date the first carnival to Friday 30 January 1959;[^1][^3] Adi and Sherwood (2003) say that the carnivals were held in February.[^2] The venue changed each year: St Pancras Town Hall in 1959, Seymour Hall in 1960 and the Lyceum Ballroom in 1961.[^3]
3. **CAACO's name:** Adi and Sherwood (2003) and Sherwood's 1999 biography both call the body the Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations, and that name is used here.[^2][^3]
4. **1955 or 1956:** Sherwood (1999) and Adi and Sherwood (2003) say that she arrived in London in December 1955;[^3][^2] Busby's 1992 headnote says that she took up residence in Britain in 1956 and calls her the coordinator of the first Caribbean Carnival in London.[^9]
5. **The *Gazette*'s first issue:** English Heritage gives 1958 as the year of the paper's founding;[^1] the sources examined do not give the month of the first issue.
