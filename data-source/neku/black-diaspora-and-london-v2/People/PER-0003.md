## At a glance
Henry Sylvester Williams (born 1867 or 1869[^1][^2] – died on 26 March 1911[^2] in Port of Spain[^3]) was a barrister, writer and local councillor in London; he published the journal *The Pan-African* (1901) and *The British Negro: A Factor in the Empire* (1902).[^2] He formed the [African Association](../Organisations/ORG-0001.md) and was its secretary,[^1][^3] and was principal organiser of the [First Pan-African Conference](../Events/EVT-0001.md) at Westminster Town Hall in July 1900.[^4][^3] The *Afropean* article dates the Association's formation to 1897;[^3] Adi and Sherwood date it to 1898.[^2]

## Life and significance
Henry Sylvester Williams's early life is the subject of scholarly dispute. Earlier biographers gave his birth as 19 February 1869 in Arouca, Trinidad; Marika Sherwood reports that Ronald Noel found the registration of his birth, dated 24 March 1867, in Barbados.[^1] His parents, Henry Bishop Williams, a wheelwright, and Elizabeth Williams, had emigrated from Barbados to Trinidad.[^1] A 2014 *Afropean* article also gives Barbados, 1867, and says his family moved to Arouca in Trinidad shortly afterwards; he always identified as Trinidadian.[^3] After teaching in Trinidad and studying in the United States and Canada, he came to London in the 1890s to study law at King's College, and later qualified and practised at Gray's Inn.[^3] Adi and Sherwood give more detail: he studied law at Dalhousie University in Halifax, Nova Scotia, in 1893–4; arrived in London in 1896 and enrolled at King's College as an evening student taking Latin; was admitted to Gray's Inn the following year; and was called to the Bar in June 1902.[^2]

Williams interrupted his training as a barrister to form the African Association;[^1] it was formed in September 1897 with him as secretary, and its stated aims included direct appeals to the imperial and local governments, and from 1898 it was encouraged to put forward MPs to advocate in Parliament for people in the colonies.[^3] His landmark achievement was organising the First Pan-African Conference, held at [Westminster Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0001.md) on 23–25 July 1900.[^4][^3]

Williams later entered municipal politics. In November 1906 he was elected to [St Marylebone Borough Council](../Organisations/ORG-0006.md) as a Progressive for the Church Street ward, polling 701 votes;[^5][^6] Sherwood counts him as one of the first two Black borough councillors elected in London.[^1] Adi and Sherwood note that his attendance at the council was irregular.[^2] Westminster City Council commemorates him with a green plaque at [38 Church Street](../Places/PLC-0009.md), which it describes as his former home.[^7]

## Evidence notes
1. **Date and place of birth:** Sherwood (2011) rejects the date of 19 February 1869 in Arouca given by earlier biographers, in favour of a Barbados birth registration of 24 March 1867.[^1] The *Afropean* article also gives Barbados and 1867, and its statement that he died in 1911 "aged only 44" fits an 1867 birth.[^3] Fryer gives 19 February 1869, as does Sherwood's own 2003 entry in Adi and Sherwood.[^2][^6]
2. **38 Church Street:** The council calls the house his former home,[^7] but no source examined gives the years he lived there.
3. **King's College and Gray's Inn:** The *Afropean* article says that he studied law at King's College in the 1890s and qualified and practised at Gray's Inn;[^3] Adi and Sherwood say that he enrolled at King's College in 1896 as an evening student taking Latin and was admitted to Gray's Inn the following year.[^2] The Inn's own admission records have not been checked.
