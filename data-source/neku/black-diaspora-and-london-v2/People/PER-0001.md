# W. E. B. Du Bois

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Dates | 23 February 1868 – 27 August 1963 |
| Origins | USA, Ghana |
| Roles | Sociologist, Pan-Africanist |

## At a glance
William Edward Burghardt Du Bois (1868–1963) was an African American sociologist, historian, and Pan-African organiser.[^1] He participated in the 1900 First Pan-African Conference at Westminster Town Hall[^2] and the 1945 Fifth Pan-African Congress in Manchester.[^1] For the 1911 First Universal Races Congress in London he was Hon. Secretary for the United States and contributed a paper;[^3] Adi and Sherwood say he returned to London for the congress and presented the paper there, and that he took the initiative to hold the 1921 Second Pan-African Congress in Paris, London and Brussels.[^4]

## Life and significance
W. E. B. Du Bois was born in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, in 1868.[^1] He attended the First Pan-African Conference in London in 1900.[^1] The University of Edinburgh dates the conference to 23–25 July 1900 and identifies its organiser as the West Indian barrister [Henry Sylvester Williams](../People/PER-0003.md).[^5] The venue was [Westminster Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0001.md),[^2] now Caxton Hall.[^6] Du Bois chaired the committee that composed the conference's address, *"[To the Nations of the World](../Works/WRK-0001.md)"*,[^2] which declared that "the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line".[^7] Copies of the 1900 London conference reports and the text of *"To the Nations of the World"* are preserved in the W. E. B. Du Bois Papers (MS 312, Box 4, Folder 26) at the University of Massachusetts Amherst.[^8]

In October 1945 Du Bois served as International President of the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](../Events/EVT-0004.md), which met at Chorlton-on-Medlock Town Hall in [Manchester](../Places/PLC-0014.md).[^1][^9] His exact role at the opening session is disputed: Thomas C. Holt states that Du Bois and [Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md) co-chaired the opening session.[^1] However, George Padmore's contemporary 1947 proceedings state that Ashwood Garvey chaired the first session on 15 October, and Du Bois chaired the first session on 16 October.[^9] Adi and Sherwood likewise say that Du Bois, made International President and Permanent Chairman, chaired every session except the first, which Ashwood Garvey chaired.[^4] Holt also records that Du Bois joined Amy Jacques Garvey and [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md) in sponsoring the Manchester conference.[^1] Padmore, a principal organiser for the Congress, lived at [22 Cranleigh Street, Somers Town](../Places/PLC-0012.md) during this period.[^10][^11]

## Connections

- **[Henry Sylvester Williams](../People/PER-0003.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois and Henry Sylvester Williams both took part in the First Pan-African Conference of July 1900, Williams as its organiser and Du Bois as a delegate.[^12]
- **[George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois and George Padmore worked together on the Fifth Pan-African Congress, held in Manchester in October 1945.[^13]
- **[Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois and Amy Ashwood Garvey both took a leading part in the opening of the Fifth Pan-African Congress in Manchester in October 1945; the sources disagree about whether they chaired a session together.[^14]
- **[Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](../People/PER-0010.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois recalled first seeing Samuel Coleridge-Taylor at a gathering in London, meeting him several times at public gatherings and being invited to his home.[^15]
- **[First Pan-African Conference](../Events/EVT-0001.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois attended the First Pan-African Conference at Westminster Town Hall in July 1900.[^16]
- **[To the Nations of the World](../Works/WRK-0001.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois chaired the committee that drafted 'To the Nations of the World', the address of the 1900 Pan-African Conference, and signed it.[^17]
- **[First Universal Races Congress](../Events/EVT-0002.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois was an honorary secretary of the First Universal Races Congress in London in July 1911 and contributed a paper to it.[^18]

## Evidence notes
1. **Who chaired the opening session in 1945:** Padmore's proceedings, published in 1947, put Amy Ashwood Garvey in the chair on 15 October and Du Bois in the chair on 16 October.[^9] Holt's modern account says that the two co-chaired the opening session.[^1] Adi and Sherwood agree with Padmore.[^4] Both versions are given here.
2. **The 1900 address:** Holt confirms that Du Bois attended the 1900 conference but does not mention his committee office, which rests on Clarke's 2014 *Afropean* article.[^2] The quotation is taken from a two-page transcription and from the same article, both of which read "color line"; no printed 1900 edition has been examined.[^7]
3. **The 1911 and 1921 congresses:** The published papers of the 1911 Universal Races Congress list Du Bois as Hon. Secretary for the United States and print his paper.[^3] Adi and Sherwood state that he returned to London for the congress and presented it there; they give 1921 for the second congress but not the month.[^4]
