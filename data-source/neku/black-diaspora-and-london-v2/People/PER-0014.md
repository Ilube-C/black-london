## At a glance
Cyril Lionel Robert James (1901 – 30 May 1989) was a Trinidadian historian and Marxist theorist; he died in London.[^1][^2] He was born on 4 January 1901.[^3]

## Life and significance
Born in Trinidad, James arrived in Britain in 1932.[^1] From 1934 he lived for several years in a top-floor flat at [9 Heathcote Street, Bloomsbury](../Places/PLC-0048.md), Nancy Cunard's former flat, moving there from Hampstead;[^3] he immersed himself in Trotskyist politics and researched the Haitian Revolution.[^3][^1]

In 1935, as Italy's invasion of Ethiopia became imminent, James founded and led the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](../Organisations/ORG-0008.md) (IAFA), whose officers included Jomo Kenyatta, Amy Ashwood Garvey and George Padmore.[^2] In 1936 his play [*Toussaint Louverture*](../Works/WRK-0014.md) was staged with [Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md) leading the cast.[^2] The Stage Society staged it at the [Westminster Theatre](../Places/PLC-0049.md) on [15 and 16 March 1936](../Events/EVT-0018.md).[^3] In May 1937, James and [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md) launched the [International African Service Bureau](../Organisations/ORG-0009.md) (IASB) in London,[^1] and James edited its journal *[International African Opinion](../Works/WRK-0013.md)*.[^2] In 1938 he published *The [Black Jacobins](../Works/WRK-0015.md)* in London, and that year a lecture tour took him to the United States, where he stayed fifteen years.[^1] *[A History of Negro Revolt](../Works/WRK-0016.md)* also appeared in 1938.[^2]

London became his home again in 1953, and he spent five years living in Hampstead and [Willesden](../Places/PLC-0050.md) before returning to Trinidad.[^4] From 1981 until his death in 1989 he lived at [165 Railton Road, Brixton](../Places/PLC-0051.md), in a second-floor flat above the offices of the journal *Race Today*.[^4] Bunce and Field date the *Race Today* Collective's move to 165 Railton Road, from Shakespeare Road, to mid-1982, and quote Darcus Howe's recollection that James "lived upstairs above the offices".[^5]

## Connections

- **[Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md)** — C. L. R. James and Jomo Kenyatta worked together in the International African Friends of Abyssinia; James introduced Kenyatta to his publisher and reviewed Facing Mount Kenya in 1938.[^6]
- **[Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md)** — C. L. R. James and Amy Ashwood Garvey founded the International African Friends of Abyssinia together in 1935 and were both officers of the International African Service Bureau.[^7]
- **[Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md)** — Paul Robeson played the title role in C. L. R. James's play Toussaint Louverture in March 1936, and James later sought his view of The Black Jacobins.[^8]
- **[George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md)** — George Padmore and C. L. R. James, boyhood friends from Trinidad, worked together in London's Pan-African organisations, launching the International African Service Bureau in 1937.[^9]
- **[Stuart Hall](../People/PER-0022.md)** — Stuart Hall held a conversation with C. L. R. James that was later published, and wrote a portrait of him.[^10]
- **[International African Friends of Abyssinia](../Organisations/ORG-0008.md)** — C. L. R. James founded and chaired the International African Friends of Abyssinia, formed in London in 1935 against Italy's war on Ethiopia.[^11]
- **[165 Railton Road](../Places/PLC-0051.md)** — C. L. R. James lived at 165 Railton Road, Brixton, above the offices of Race Today, in the last years of his life; he died there in 1989.[^12]

## Evidence notes
1. **Bloomsbury addresses:** James moved often. Høgsbjerg's 2012 chapter cites The National Archives file KV/2/1824/1z for a Special Branch report of a speech James made on 9 September 1936,[^1] but does not give 9 Heathcote Street. His 2014 book does: James gave that address in a letter to the *New Statesman* of 28 March 1936, and the same note cites KV/2/1824/1z.[^3]
2. **The 1936 production:** Høgsbjerg says that the Stage Society staged *Toussaint Louverture*.[^3] The sources examined do not describe James's own part in organising the production.
