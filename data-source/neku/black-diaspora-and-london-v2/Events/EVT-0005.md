# Nelson Mandela's Clandestine Visit

*Nelson Mandela's secret ten-day stay in London in June 1962, during his tour of Africa to win support for the ANC's armed struggle, when he saw Oliver Tambo and met British politicians.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | June 1962 |

In June 1962 [Nelson Mandela](../People/PER-0002.md) spent about ten days in London. He had left South Africa secretly in January 1962 and, as the Nelson Mandela Foundation records, travelled through Africa and "visited England to gain support for the armed struggle". He returned home in July and was arrested at a police roadblock.[^1] In the Commons tributes of December 2013, Gordon Brown described that June as "his first visit to London".[^2]

## Why he came

In *Long Walk to Freedom* Mandela gives several reasons for the journey. He was worried about [Oliver Tambo](../People/PER-0020.md)'s health and wanted him to seek treatment. He wanted to see Tambo's wife Adelaide, their children and Yusuf Dadoo, who represented the Congress movement in London. And he knew he could find literature on guerrilla warfare there.[^3]

## What he did

Mandela writes that he "resumed my old underground ways in London", so that word of his presence would not reach South Africa. He divided his days between ANC business, old friends and some sightseeing.[^3] With the writer Mary Benson and Tambo, he saw Westminster Abbey, Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament. Beside the statue of Jan Smuts, he and Tambo joked that one day there might be a statue of them in its place.[^3]

His political meetings were with the editor of the *Observer*, David Astor, at Astor's house, which Tambo arranged. In the company of the Labour MP Denis Healey he also met the Labour leader Hugh Gaitskell and the Liberal leader Jo Grimond.[^3] On his last night he argued with Dadoo, who feared that Mandela and Tambo were moving the ANC away from the non-racial policy of the Freedom Charter. Mandela replied that he was talking "about image", not policy: the ANC must appear "the first among equals" within the Congress Alliance.[^3] Callinicos adds that in London Mandela "had a long talk with Adelaide" Tambo.[^4]

## Significance

Mandela's own account shows him working and sightseeing with Tambo in London, so this is documented direct contact between them.[^3][^4] The visit came weeks before his arrest in July 1962.[^1] Thirty-four years later, as President, he addressed both Houses of Parliament in [Westminster Hall](../Places/PLC-0004.md) (see [Address in Westminster Hall](../Events/EVT-0006.md)).

## Evidence notes

- **Dates.** The exact dates of arrival and departure are not given in the sources consulted. The month comes from Gordon Brown's tribute, and the length of stay ("my ten days") from Mandela.
- **Where he stayed.** The sources consulted do not record where Mandela stayed in London.
