# Othello at the Savoy Theatre (opening night)

*The opening night, 19 May 1930, of Maurice Browne's production of Othello at the Savoy Theatre, London, with Paul Robeson as Othello and Peggy Ashcroft as Desdemona.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 19 May 1930 |

<!-- MEDIA: image slot — a production photograph of Robeson as Othello, Savoy Theatre, 1930; to be selected and added by the editor -->

On the evening of 19 May 1930 [Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md) opened as Othello at the [Savoy Theatre](../Places/PLC-0063.md) in London, in a production presented by the actor-manager Maurice Browne.[^1] The theatre historian Michael A. Morrison writes that Robeson was the first actor of African descent to appear in an extended-run Shakespearean production at a leading West End venue. Ira Aldridge, sixty-five years earlier, had made only three brief appearances at major London theatres.[^2]

## Background

After the success of *Journey's End*, which he had presented at the Savoy, Browne had set his heart on producing *Othello* with Robeson. Robeson at first resisted but agreed towards the end of August 1929. He was persuaded partly by a fee of £300 a week, reported to be the highest salary ever for an actor in a non-musical production.[^3] The production was announced amid well-publicised incidents of racial prejudice in London, among them an episode involving Robeson at the Savoy Grill in October 1929.[^2][^3] According to Peggy Ashcroft, Robeson had approval of the actress who would play Desdemona. Ashcroft, then twenty-two, was given the part.[^4]

## Rehearsals and opening

Full rehearsals began in the week of 14 April at the Globe Theatre, with five weeks scheduled.[^1] The play had first been announced to open "on Monday 19 May at the Globe". Browne moved it, and on the afternoon of 19 May "the cast assembled at the Savoy", hours before the first performance.[^1] That evening the first-night audience "filed into the Savoy Theatre". Eslanda Robeson sat in the third row of the stalls.[^5] Morrison records "tumultuous applause and twenty curtain calls".[^6]

## Significance

Duberman's biography follows the rehearsals and opening night and cites the London reviews of 20 May 1930.[^7] Adi and Sherwood describe it as Robeson's "first and much acclaimed performance in *Othello*".[^8] Morrison argues that it was "an event of barrier-breaking social significance". He quotes a British columnist writing before the opening that, for the people of Harlem, it was "more than a first night … a symbol of cultural aspirations".[^2]

## Evidence notes

- **The theatre.** Duberman's chapter does not name the theatre. The Savoy is established by Morrison.[^1][^7]
- **Length of run.** The sources consulted do not establish the length of the run.
