# Clement Gomwalk Incident

*The arrest of the Nigerian diplomat Clement Gomwalk by police in Brixton on 15 November 1969, in which the seventeen-year-old Olive Morris intervened and was arrested and, by her own account, beaten.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 15 November 1969 |

On 15 November 1969 police in [Brixton](../Places/PLC-0006.md) arrested Clement Gomwalk, a diplomat with the Nigerian High Commission. [Olive Morris](../People/PER-0025.md), then seventeen, intervened and was herself arrested.[^1] The incident has come to mark the beginning of her activism.[^1]

## What happened

Kimberly Springer, writing in *Do You Remember Olive Morris?*, sets the "common folklore" beside the newspaper record. In the folklore, Morris stepped in alone when police arrested "a black man driving a nice car". *The Times* named the man as Gomwalk, who had parked his Mercedes illegally in a no-waiting zone while he and his wife went shopping.[^1] The newspaper also reported that six people, not Morris alone, were arrested. They were charged with "assault on police, threatening behaviour, and possessing offensive weapons".[^1]

A photograph taken at King's College Hospital after her arrest shows Morris dishevelled, her shirt soiled and her face swollen. On the back she wrote: "Taken at about 10pm on 15th Nov 69 after the police had beaten me up."[^1] At her sentencing the newspaper reported that "During the melee Miss Morris kicked a police officer and hit him on the jaw". She received a three-year suspended sentence, later reduced to one year suspended.[^1]

## Significance

The arrest of a diplomat became a public matter: *The Times* of 18 November 1969 headed its report "Callaghan rebukes diplomat".[^1] For Springer, the incident's importance lies in how it came "to signal Olive's entry into activism with police abuses of the community as a key feature". Correcting the record to show that Morris "did not in fact act alone", she writes, "recasts Olive as an instigator of change".[^1] After the incident Morris joined the [Black Panther Movement](../Organisations/ORG-0031.md), and she was later a founder member of the [Brixton Black Women's Group](../Organisations/ORG-0032.md).[^2][^3]

## Evidence notes

- **Newspaper reports.** The reports in *The Times* of 18 November 1969 and 17 April 1970 are known here from Springer's notes and were not consulted directly.
- **Exact location.** The sources consulted do not give the street in Brixton where the arrest took place.
