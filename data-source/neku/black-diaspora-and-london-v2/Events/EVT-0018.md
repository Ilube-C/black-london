# Staging of Toussaint Louverture

*The Stage Society's production of C. L. R. James's play Toussaint Louverture at the Westminster Theatre in March 1936, with Paul Robeson in the title role.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 15 March 1936 |

In March 1936 the Stage Society, which Høgsbjerg calls "prestigious", staged [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md)'s play [*Toussaint Louverture: The Story of the Only Successful Slave Revolt in History*](../Works/WRK-0014.md) at the [Westminster Theatre](../Places/PLC-0049.md) in London. [Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md) played the title role.[^1][^2] The historian Christian Høgsbjerg calls it the first play explicitly about the Haitian Revolution performed on the British stage "for almost a century". He also notes that it was the only time Robeson starred in a play by a writer of African heritage.[^1]

## The production

James wrote the play in 1934.[^1] Robeson went into rehearsals as soon as he returned to London late in January 1936. A planned film about Toussaint had stalled, and in Duberman's words Robeson "tried out the stage version".[^2] Høgsbjerg records two performances, on 15 and 16 March 1936. Duberman writes that the play "was given on several Sunday evenings in March 1936".[^1][^2] James, by Fryer's account, acted in it himself alongside Robeson.[^3]

## Reception

Duberman summarises the critics' view: they praised Robeson but thought little of the play, calling it a "careful prose record". *The Times* wrote that by the usual rules his acting "is clumsy, but his appearance and voice entitle him to rules of his own". The *Evening Standard*'s critic compared him to "Japhet in search of a father … in search of a play".[^2] Duberman adds that Robeson nonetheless valued the experience.[^2]

## Significance

The production brought together two of the atlas's figures in a documented working partnership: James as author and Robeson as lead. It fell during James's chairmanship of the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](../Organisations/ORG-0008.md) (1935–36) and two years before he published his history of the Haitian Revolution, [*The Black Jacobins*](../Works/WRK-0015.md) (1938).[^1] The play was first published only in 2013.[^1]
