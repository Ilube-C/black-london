# Premiere of Hiawatha's Wedding Feast

*The first performance of Samuel Coleridge-Taylor's cantata Hiawatha's Wedding Feast, at a Royal College of Music concert conducted by Sir Charles Stanford on 11 November 1898.*

On 11 November 1898 the choir and orchestra of the [Royal College of Music](../Organisations/ORG-0015.md) gave the first performance of [*Hiawatha's Wedding Feast*](../Works/WRK-0007.md), a cantata by its former student [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](../People/PER-0010.md), then twenty-three. Sir Charles Stanford conducted.[^1] It made Coleridge-Taylor famous overnight.[^2]

## The work and its preparation

Coleridge-Taylor's biographer, W. C. Berwick Sayers, writes that the setting of Longfellow's poem began as a college composition. It owed something to the "suggestions and criticisms" of Stanford, with whom he had studied composition since 1892.[^3][^4] Coleridge-Taylor told Walter Hayson that "the essential beauty of the poem" was "its naive simplicity".[^3] The cantata was rehearsed through the autumn under Stanford. Although the students had agreed to keep it secret, word spread that something unusual would be heard. Novello had agreed to publish it.[^5]

## The concert

Sayers reports that on the morning of the performance Sir Arthur Sullivan, then seriously ill, met Coleridge-Taylor at Novello's and promised to come "even if I have to be carried into the room".[^5] In the evening the old hall of the Royal College was crowded, with people sitting on the platform steps and standing in the passages.[^5] The orchestra was led by the composer's friend Willie J. Read. Sayers admits that the performance was not perfect: the orchestra was nervous and the chorus "lacked balance". He singles out the tenor solo "Onaway, Awake", which "was recognised at once at its real worth".[^6] The composer was recalled again and again. Afterwards, Sayers was told, he hid in doorways to avoid members of the audience walking home.[^6]

## Reception

"Next morning he awoke to find himself indeed famous", Sayers writes. Every London paper reviewed the work, almost all of them enthusiastically.[^6] *The Wedding Feast* was planned as a complete work in itself. Only later, while writing *The Death of Minnehaha*, did Coleridge-Taylor decide to make the story into a trilogy, *The Song of Hiawatha*.[^7]

## Evidence notes

- **The building.** Sayers describes the venue only as "the old hall of the Royal College". The building is recorded under [Royal College of Music](../Places/PLC-0030.md).
