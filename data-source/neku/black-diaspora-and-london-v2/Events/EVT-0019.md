# Caribbean Carnival

*The first Caribbean Carnival organised by the West Indian Gazette, held indoors at St Pancras Town Hall on Friday 30 January 1959 and partly televised by the BBC, in response to the 1958 riots.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 30 January 1959 |

On Friday 30 January 1959 the [*West Indian Gazette*](../Works/WRK-0022.md) staged what it announced as the "Caribbean Carnival" at [St Pancras Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0059.md) in central London.[^1] Its organiser was the paper's founder and editor, [Claudia Jones](../People/PER-0018.md).[^1][^2] It was an indoor evening of cabaret, music and dance. English Heritage records that "the first event, sponsored by the West Indian Gazette, was held at St Pancras Town Hall on 30 January 1959".[^2]

## Why it was held

The Carnival was a response to the anti-Black riots in Notting Hill and Nottingham in the summer of 1958. A Caribbean Carnival Committee was set up in November 1958 under the *Gazette*'s sponsorship.[^1] Colin Prescod, writing in Sherwood's biography of Jones, describes it as an event to show "a spirit of bouncing back" after the riots. He notes Jones's belief in "the role of the arts in bringing people together for common aims".[^3] In her introduction to the souvenir brochure, titled "A people's art is the genesis of their freedom", Jones named the riots directly. She called "the event of Notting Hill and Nottingham" the matrix "binding West Indians in the United Kingdom together as never before".[^4] The brochure stated that part of its proceeds would help pay the fines of "coloured and white youths involved in the Notting Hill events".[^1]

## The evening

The souvenir programme recorded that the cabaret was directed by the actor and singer Edric Connor. It also announced that the BBC would televise part of the cabaret between 10.45 and 11.25 p.m.[^1] The broadcast took place, but Prescod reports that the BBC appears to keep no film or tape of it.[^1] The performers who worked with Jones from 1959 included Cleo Laine, Cy Grant and Edric Connor.[^3] Trevor Carter was among the early organisers; he later said that "the spirit of the carnival came out of Claudia's political knowledge of what to touch at a particular time".[^5]

## What followed

Jones's brochure announced "another determination": "to make the WIG Caribbean Carnival an annual event".[^4] It became one. The fourth annual Carnival was held at Seymour Hall, Seymour Place, W1, on 16 March 1962, with the Mighty Sparrow flown in to perform.[^6] Prescod traces the outdoor Notting Hill festival to 1965, after Jones's death. That year the community activist Rhaune Laslett sought carnival elements for the Notting Hill Fair parade and "was able to tap directly into an already existing London based Caribbean carnival".[^7]

## Evidence notes

- **Indoor and outdoor carnivals.** The 1959 Carnival was an indoor event and should not be confused with the outdoor Notting Hill Carnival, which began after Jones's death. The relationship between the two is set out in Jones's biography.
- **The programme.** The souvenir programme is known here only from Sherwood's book, which reproduces it; the original was not consulted.

## Connected people

- **[Claudia Jones](../People/PER-0018.md)** — Claudia Jones organised the indoor Caribbean Carnival held at St Pancras Town Hall on 30 January 1959, sponsored by her West Indian Gazette.[^8]
