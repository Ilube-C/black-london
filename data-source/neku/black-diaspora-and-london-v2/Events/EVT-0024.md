# Mangrove Demo

*The march of Sunday 9 August 1970 from the Mangrove restaurant in All Saints Road, Notting Hill, to protest at repeated police raids on the restaurant, organised by the Action Committee for Defence of the Mangrove with the Black Panther Movement.*

On 9 August 1970 a demonstration set off from [The Mangrove](../Places/PLC-0080.md), Frank Crichlow's restaurant at 8 All Saints Road, Notting Hill. It was protesting at police raids on the restaurant.[^1][^2] It was organised "by the Action Committee for Defence of the Mangrove in conjunction with the [Black Panther Movement](../Organisations/ORG-0031.md)".[^1] Clashes with the police led to the arrest of twenty-seven people and, the following year, the trial of the [Mangrove Nine](../Events/EVT-0025.md).[^1]

## Organisation

The National Archives records that the action group behind the march "was set up by anti-racist activists, like the leader of Britain's Black Panthers Altheia Jones-LeCointe". Its public statement called for an end to "the persecution of the Mangrove Restaurant".[^2] According to evidence given at the later trial, a small group settled the details of the march in July at the Willesden home of the activist Selma James.[^1]

## The march

Before the march set off, the activist Darcus Howe spoke from a makeshift platform: "We have become the own shapers of our destiny as from today."[^1] Bunce and Field write: "Next to speak was Althea Jones-Lecointe, the driving force behind the British Black Panthers."[^1] This was [Altheia Jones-LeCointe](../People/PER-0024.md), whose name the atlas spells in that form. "Speeches over", the marchers, accompanied at first by 200 police officers, headed west along Lancaster Road towards Notting Dale police station, the first of three stations on the route.[^1] Police documents show that 705 officers were available that day.[^1] Some marchers wore Black Panther-style black berets and leather jackets, and banners read "POWER TO THE PEOPLE".[^1]

The flashpoint came on Portnall Road, on the way to Harrow Road police station, where fighting broke out between marchers and police.[^1] A Special Branch report dated 9 August claimed that the march had been designed to lead the police into "a pre-calculated situation". The Home Secretary, Reginald Maudling, received this and other reports on the Black Power movement in the days that followed.[^3]

## Aftermath

Twenty-seven people were arrested. Nine, including Jones-LeCointe, Howe, Crichlow, Barbara Beese and Rhodan Gordon, were later charged with incitement to riot and other offences (see [Mangrove Nine trial](../Events/EVT-0025.md)).[^1][^4] On 31 October 1970 about 260 white supporters retraced the route, with Jones-LeCointe among the speakers.[^4]

## Connected people

- **[Altheia Jones-LeCointe](../People/PER-0024.md)** — Altheia Jones-LeCointe helped set up the action group behind the Mangrove demonstration of 9 August 1970 and spoke to the marchers before they set off.[^5]
