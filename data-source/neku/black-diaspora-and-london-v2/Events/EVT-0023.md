# Black People's Day of Action

*The Black People's Day of Action of Monday 2 March 1981, a national march through London called by the New Cross Massacre Action Committee to protest at the official and press response to the New Cross fire.*

| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 2 March 1981 |

On Monday 2 March 1981 some 20,000 Black people marched through London in the Black People's Day of Action. They were protesting at the response of the police, the press and the government to the New Cross fire. In the early hours of 18 January 1981 a fire at a birthday party at 439 New Cross Road, Deptford, had killed thirteen young people aged between 14 and 22, all from the African-Caribbean community.[^1] Bunce and Field call it "the largest demonstration of black political power Britain has ever seen".[^1]

## Organisation

After the fire an "impromptu grass-roots assembly" and the New Cross Massacre Action Committee were formed. The activist Darcus Howe was elected to give the movement shape.[^1] At a meeting on Tuesday 27 January 1981 they decided to call a Day of Action on a working day in term time. Howe described the idea as "a general strike of blacks".[^1] The Assembly voted for Howe and other members of the Race Today Collective to organise it. Howe toured the country to build support, and the committee printed 36,000 leaflets for London alone.[^1] Bunce and Field add that Howe secured white involvement "against the demands of nationalists who wished to keep the event exclusively black".[^1]

## Records in the atlas

The papers of [Jessica Huntley](../People/PER-0023.md) and Eric Huntley hold notes and preparations for the Day of Action. They include minutes, a list of the "Areas for New Cross Massacre Action Committee Day of Action 2nd March" and Eric Huntley's review of the demonstration. There are also files of press reports of the march.[^2] The Huntleys had been with Howe and John La Rose when news of the fire reached them (see [the Book Fair](../Events/EVT-0022.md)).[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Route.** The sources consulted do not give the route of the march.
- **The slogan.** Bunce and Field's chapter title, "Thirteen Dead and Nothing Said", repeats a slogan associated with the campaign. Its use on the day is not described in the passages consulted.
