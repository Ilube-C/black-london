# Westminster Parliamentary Elections

*The Westminster parliamentary elections of 1774 and 1780, in which the grocer and writer Ignatius Sancho voted as a male householder.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 1774 |

<!-- MEDIA: image slot — a page of the Westminster poll book of 1774 or 1780; to be selected and added by the editor -->

[Ignatius Sancho](../People/PER-0007.md), who kept a grocer's shop in Westminster, voted in the parliamentary elections for the City and Liberty of Westminster in 1774 and 1780.[^1][^2] His editor, Vincent Carretta, explains that his "financial independence as a male householder in Westminster" qualified him to vote.[^1]

## The elections

Carretta's notes to the *Letters* describe the 1780 general election. George III dissolved Parliament suddenly on 1 September 1780, after the Gordon Riots and news of victory at Charleston. In Westminster, George Brydges Rodney (5,298 votes) and Charles James Fox (4,878) defeated the ministerial candidate, Thomas Pelham Clinton.[^3] The sources consulted do not record how Sancho voted in either election.

Sancho's own letters show the campaign around him. On 9 September 1780 he wrote to John Spink:

> We are all election-bewitched here—I hope Sir C[harles] B[unbury] meets with no opposition—he is so worthy a character, that, should he be ill supported, it would impeach the good sense and honesty of his constituents.[^4]

## Sancho's place among Black voters

Carretta calls Sancho "the only eighteenth-century Afro-Briton known to have voted in parliamentary elections".[^1] Gillian Williamson of the ECPPEC project notes that he "is usually described as the first, or even 'only', such voter". She reports a recent archival find of an earlier Black voter in Westminster, John London, in the by-election of 1749, whose ethnicity is recorded only because his vote was challenged.[^2]

## Evidence notes

- **Sancho's address in the poll books.** Carretta writes that the Westminster poll books of 1774 and 1780 place Sancho's shop "at the corner of Crown Court on the Charles Street that runs east into Parliament Street". That street is now King Charles Street.[^5] Williamson describes him as "of Charles Street, St George Hanover Square".[^2] The difference is noted under [19 Charles Street](../Places/PLC-0020.md).
- **Poll books not consulted.** The poll books themselves were not consulted for this edition. A request for them is recorded in the source requests.
