# Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations (CAACO)

*The Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations, a London coalition initiated by Claudia Jones and the West Indian Gazette in the early 1960s, which campaigned against racial discrimination and the Commonwealth Immigrants Act and in solidarity with the US civil rights movement.*

The Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations (CAACO) was a coalition formed in London in the early 1960s under the leadership of [Claudia Jones](../People/PER-0018.md). A. Sivanandan, quoted by Marika Sherwood, describes it as "initiated by the [*Gazette*](../Works/WRK-0022.md) and working closely with the Indian Workers' Association and Fenner Brockway, and the Movement for Colonial Freedom". It held "its meetings and marches", but concentrated on lobbying the High Commissions and Parliament.[^1]

## Origins

Sherwood suggests that CAACO may have evolved from a conference Jones convened to oppose the Commonwealth Immigrants Bill, "once the action against the Immigration Bill was well and truly lost". She notes that its membership is unknown.[^1] The Communist Party did not permit its affiliated Indian organisations or its members to join.[^1] Sherwood's records of CAACO begin in June 1963, when its address was Africa Unity House.[^1]

## Campaigns and meetings

- **13 June 1963.** CAACO held a meeting at Conway Hall in support of the civil rights struggle in Birmingham, Alabama, and to plan a "solidarity protest on the American Negro People's Struggle". Speakers included Jones, Raymond Kunene of the [African National Congress](../Organisations/ORG-0002.md), the London County councillor Dr David Pitt and the trade unionist Clive Jenkins.[^1]
- **The US Embassy.** A delegation including Jones called at the US Embassy to protest at the murder of the civil rights activist Medgar Evers. Its note asked the American government "to uproot the scourge of racialism".[^2] On 28 August 1963, the day of the March on Washington, a deputation led by Ranjana Ash took a further statement to the embassy.[^2]
- **31 August 1963.** A solidarity march went from Ladbroke Grove, Notting Hill, to the embassy. According to the *Daily Worker*, the singer Pearl Prescod led the singing of "We Shall Overcome". Some three hundred marchers carried banners against the colour bar and for repeal of the Immigration Act.[^2]
- **May 1964.** CAACO sent a detailed memorandum to the Commonwealth Prime Ministers' Conference in London. It called for the release of political prisoners in British Guiana and South Africa, sanctions against South Africa, the release of Joshua Nkomo, and military measures against "any attempted coup by the white Smith Government" in Southern Rhodesia.[^3]

## Fate

The sources consulted do not record what became of CAACO after Jones's death in December 1964.

## Evidence notes

- **Founding date.** No founding date or founding meeting is recorded in the sources consulted.
- **Membership.** The organisations that belonged to the coalition are not known, beyond those named.
