# West African National Secretariat (WANS)

*The West African National Secretariat, formed in London in late 1945 after the Manchester Congress, with Kwame Nkrumah as general secretary, to work for "a free, united, independent and democratic West Africa"; it collapsed soon after Nkrumah left for the Gold Coast in 1947.*

The West African National Secretariat (WANS) was formed in London at the end of 1945 by delegates who had attended the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](../Events/EVT-0004.md).[^1] Its pamphlet ended with the slogan "WEST AFRICA IS ONE COUNTRY: PEOPLES OF WEST AFRICA UNITE!"[^2] [Kwame Nkrumah](../People/PER-0016.md) was its general secretary and, in the words of a fellow member, "the moving spirit from the beginning".[^1][^2] Adi writes that WANS "collapsed soon after Nkrumah's departure for the Gold Coast in 1947, but it was extremely active during its short life".[^3]

## Why it was formed

Nkrumah's biographer Marika Sherwood, drawing on interviews by the historian LaRay Denzer, writes that several Congress delegates met afterwards. Among them were Kojo Botsio, Bankole Akpata, Ashie Nikoi, I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson and Bankole Awoonor-Renner, who wanted a "radical progressive organisation for seizing power in Africa as quickly as possible".[^1] Botsio told Sherwood that it was the "moderate tone of the Congress resolutions" and the lack of follow-up that "gave rise to the establishment of the more radical" Secretariat.[^1] Its official sponsors were the Sierra Leone branch of the West African Youth League, the Aborigines' Rights Protection Society, the Gold Coast Farmers' Committee and the Sierra Leone Trade Union Congress.[^1]

## Founding meeting and officers

At a meeting in London on 14 December 1945, chaired by Wallace-Johnson, Botsio was appointed treasurer. The *Ghana Year Book* of 1958 names Nkrumah as general secretary, Awoonor-Renner as president and Mrs Olabisi Awoonor-Renner as treasurer.[^2] By March 1946 Wallace-Johnson had returned to Sierra Leone as WANS's representative there, and Awoonor-Renner had replaced him as chairman.[^4]

## Aims

The meeting set WANS's functions:

- to co-ordinate and inform progressive organisations in West Africa "with a view to realising a West African Front for a United West African National Independence";
- to educate the working class of the imperial countries about West Africa;
- to foster unity among West Africans opposed to imperialism;
- to publish a monthly paper.[^2]

Sherwood calls this "probably for the first time at least in anglophone West African history … a declaration of the need for mass organisation in order to achieve political aims".[^2]

## Office and meetings

WANS took office space at [94 Gray's Inn Road](../Places/PLC-0056.md) from the Ghanaian lawyer K. O. Larbi, who had attended the Manchester Congress.[^4] Its first public meeting, on 1 February 1946, called on the new United Nations to "bring about the complete liquidation of the Colonial system". *West Africa* printed its resolutions over the signatures of Nkrumah and Wallace-Johnson.[^4] On 27 March 1946 another meeting sent new resolutions to the UN demanding direct representation for colonial peoples.[^5] In May Nkrumah went to Paris to meet African deputies of the French National Assembly, including Apithy and Léopold Senghor.[^5]

## The West African Conference, 1946

WANS and the [West African Students' Union](../Organisations/ORG-0018.md) sponsored a West African conference from 30 August to 1 September 1946 on "Unity and Independence of all West Africa". Between 200 and 300 West Africans, mostly students, attended, with delegates from France.[^5] The conference called for a constitutional convention of the peoples of Africa and mandated WANS to "rally the masses" through territorial councils. It closed with a "resolution and plan of action" read by Nkrumah, demanding a politically independent and united West Africa and a congress in West Africa within a year.[^5][^6]

## Publications

Nkrumah was "chief editor" of the Secretariat's paper, [*The New African*](../Works/WRK-0018.md).[^7] Adi notes that it carried articles in French on the French and Belgian colonies.[^3]

## Fate

WANS did not long survive Nkrumah's return to the Gold Coast in 1947.[^3] Adi and Sherwood note that he also led "the Circle", a secret revolutionary organisation.[^7]
