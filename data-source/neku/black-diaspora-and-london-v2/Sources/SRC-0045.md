## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md): Lived at 95 Cambridge Street 1933–37, after a short stay there in 1930. — p. 121: he 'moved further down Cambridge Street to Number 95'; p. 181: 'returned to his old digs at 95 Cambridge Street'
- [Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md): Worked at University College London as a linguistic informant, including for Lilias Armstrong's work on Kikuyu phonology. — p. 180: introduced to London University's language specialists in the winter of 1929, and paid by the University College phonetics department as a Kikuyu speaker 'for several years'; Armstrong's book, 1935–7; p. 181: on University College's books from January to July 1935 and October 1935 to June 1936
- [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md): George Padmore and Jomo Kenyatta worked together from 1930, in Padmore's Comintern-backed International Trade Union Committee of Negro Workers and later in London's IAFA and IASB. — pp. 164 (Kenyatta on the Provisional Executive Committee of the Hamburg conference, July 1930), 166 ('His answer was to join Padmore in Germany'), 167 ('Padmore had taken him to Moscow')
- [Ladipo Solanke](../People/PER-0011.md): Jomo Kenyatta, arriving in London in March 1929, went to Ladipo Solanke, whose West African Students' Union had promised the Kikuyu Central Association hospitality; Adi records their friendship. — p. 114 (arrival on 8 March 1929; Solanke 'then living at 57 Castletown Road, W14'; 'In 1929, when Kenyatta met him'); p. 121 (Colonial Office note: 'the West African Students, Mr. Solanke, help him over here')
- [Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md): Jomo Kenyatta and Paul Robeson met while making Sanders of the River (1934–35); Murray-Brown records that they became better acquainted and visited each other's flats. — pp. 185–187 (Kenyatta an extra in Sanders of the River, starring Robeson; 'They became better acquainted'; 'They visited each other's flats'); p. 181 (Mrs Hocken, landlady at 95 Cambridge Street)
