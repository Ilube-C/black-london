## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md): Brought to Leigh Street, Bloomsbury in 1828 by the Woods. — History, p. 32: 'when we came up to live in Leigh Street'; p. 35: 'This was the first winter I spent in England', with the editor's note dating her visit to the Anti-Slavery Office to 'the latter end of November 1828'; note 63: Leigh Street 'in Bloomsbury'
- [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md): Took her trunk to the Moravian missionaries at Hatton Garden after the Woods turned her out. — History, p. 33: 'asked his wife to get somebody to go with me to Hatton Garden to the Moravian Missionaries'; p. 35: 'went and carried my trunk to the Moravians'
- [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md): Sought assistance from the Anti-Slavery Society. — History, p. 35, editor's note: 'She came first to the Anti-Slavery Office in Aldermanbury, about the latter end of November 1828'; Supplement, p. 39; title page: Joseph Phillips, '18, Aldermanbury'
- [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md): Dictated her history to Susanna Strickland; it was published in London in 1831. — Title page of the third edition (London: F. Westley and A. H. Davis, 1831); Salih's note 2: Susanna Strickland, 'who wrote down Prince's History'
- [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md): Gave evidence, as the first defence witness, in Wood's libel action against Pringle, heard in the Court of King's Bench on 27 February 1833 and reported in The Times on 1 March 1833 (Lambert); Salih's notes date the case to March 1833. — Salih's notes 26 and 52 (pp. 74, 76): 'In evidence given at the libel case which Wood brought against Pringle in March 1833'; 'See The Times, 1 March 1833'
