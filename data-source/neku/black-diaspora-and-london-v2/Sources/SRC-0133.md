- [Kwame Nkrumah](../People/PER-0016.md): Hakim Adi and Marika Sherwood, *Pan-African History: Political Figures from Africa and the Diaspora since 1787* (London: Routledge, 2003), 'Francis Nwia Kofi Kwame Nkrumah' (by Hakim Adi), pp. 143 (c.1909; born in Nkroful in the Nzima region; to London in 1945; WASU vice-president; worked with Padmore organising the Manchester Congress; a founder and general secretary of WANS) and 144 ('chief editor' of the WANS' *New Africa*; *Towards Colonial Freedom*; left Britain in 1947; Leader of Government Business in 1951 and Prime Minister from 1952); also 'George Padmore' (by Marika Sherwood), pp. 154 (the Congress held in Manchester in October 1945) and 156 (Padmore enlisted Nkrumah's help in organising it).

Secretary for the United States of the First Universal Races Congress (London, July 1911), to which he contributed the paper 'The Negro Race in the United States of America'. — p. 48: 'He returned to London in 1911 for the Universal Races Congress, where he presented a paper on ‘The Negro Race in the USA’.'
- [W. E. B. Du Bois](../People/PER-0001.md): Took the initiative to hold the 1921 Second Pan-African Congress, held in Paris, London and Brussels. — p. 50: 'Du Bois then took the initiative to hold a subsequent congress in 1921, located in Paris, London and Brussels'; p. 154 (Padmore entry): 'in recognition of his having called the previous four Congresses'

Du Bois](../People/PER-0001.md): Holt says that Du Bois co-chaired the opening session; Padmore's proceedings put him alone in the chair on 16 October. — p. 154 (Padmore entry): Du Bois 'chaired all the sessions except the first, which was chaired by AMY ASHWOOD GARVEY'; p. 50: 'made International President and Permanent Chairman of the Congress'
- [Henry Sylvester Williams](../People/PER-0003.md): Birth disputed: earlier biographers gave 19 February 1869 in Arouca, Trinidad; Sherwood reports a Barbados birth registration dated 24 March 1867. — p. 190: heading 'Henry Sylvester Williams (1869 – 1911)'; 'the first son of immigrant parents from Barbados settled in Trinidad' (the 1869 side)
