# *Caribbean Voices*

*The BBC radio programme for Caribbean writing devised by Una Marson, first broadcast on 11 March 1943 and running until 1958.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 1943–1958 |

*Caribbean Voices* was a BBC radio programme of Caribbean writing. [Una Marson](../People/PER-0013.md), then a programme assistant on the [BBC](../Organisations/ORG-0020.md)'s Empire Service, devised it. Jarrett-Macauley calls it her most enduring "creative invention".[^1] The first twenty-five-minute programme went out on the West Indian service on 11 March 1943. It included a short story by R. L. C. Aarons.[^1] Only published work could be used at first, as few West Indian writers were in wartime Britain. Under the later producer Henry Swanzy, unpublished work was sent in from the region.[^1] The programme ran until 1958. Busby likewise credits Marson with starting it.[^1][^2]
