# *The Black Jacobins*

*C. L. R. James's history of the Haitian Revolution, The Black Jacobins: Toussaint Louverture and the San Domingo Revolution, published in London by Secker and Warburg in September 1938.*

| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 1938 |

*The Black Jacobins: Toussaint Louverture and the San Domingo Revolution* is [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md)'s history of the revolution in the French colony of Saint-Domingue that created Haiti. It was written in London and published there in 1938.[^1] Høgsbjerg reproduces Secker and Warburg's advertisement for the book, dated September 1938.[^2] James had signed with the firm in 1936.[^3] Høgsbjerg calls the book "his masterful epic" and "one of the grandest 'grand narratives' ever written".[^4]

## Research

James researched the revolution for six months in France, "during the winter of 1933 and spring of 1934", working in the Bibliothèque Nationale and the Archives Nationales.[^5] The poet Léon-Gontran Damas, from French Guiana, helped him find his way. James remembered that Damas made him "aware of rapid ways in which to get the materials" he needed.[^5] In London he lived from 1934 at [9 Heathcote Street](../Places/PLC-0048.md), Bloomsbury.[^6]

## Argument

Høgsbjerg stresses the book's links with James's Marxism and with the politics of 1930s London. James compared the failure of Toussaint Louverture with the failure of the Russian revolution, writing that it was "for the same reason that the Russian socialist revolution failed, even after all its achievements—the defeat of the revolution in Europe".[^7] He described the most militant rebels of the Haitian Revolution as "revolutionaries through and through".[^8] Høgsbjerg argues that the book was "written with Pan-African resistance to European colonialism at the very forefront of his mind".[^9]

## Companion works

James had already dramatised the same story in his play [*Toussaint Louverture*](../Works/WRK-0014.md), staged with [Paul Robeson](../People/PER-0019.md) in 1936. Høgsbjerg treats the play and the history as "companion pieces of historical literature".[^4] James published [*A History of Negro Revolt*](../Works/WRK-0016.md) in the same month, September 1938.[^10] He left for the United States in October 1938.[^1]
