# *Address in Westminster Hall*

*Nelson Mandela's address to both Houses of Parliament in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996.*

On 11 July 1996 [Nelson Mandela](../People/PER-0002.md), President of South Africa, addressed both Houses of Parliament assembled in [Westminster Hall](../Places/PLC-0004.md) (see [the address](../Events/EVT-0006.md)).[^1] He set his visit against two centuries of British involvement in southern Africa: "Perhaps the fact that we are here today might serve to close a circle which is two hundred years old."[^2] He recalled that "eight decades ago" his predecessors in the leadership of the [African National Congress](../Organisations/ORG-0002.md) had come to these Houses to ask for help.[^2] He named William Wilberforce, Fenner Brockway, Harold Macmillan and Archbishop Trevor Huddleston as British friends of his country's freedom.[^3] Parliament printed the speeches of the day with the order of proceedings, and the Parliamentary Archives hold the record of the visit.[^1][^4]
