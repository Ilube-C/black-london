# *International African Opinion*

*The journal of the International African Service Bureau, first issued in July 1938 with C. L. R. James as its editor; it circulated among colonial Africans and argued for anti-colonial struggle based on "the great masses of the people".*

*International African Opinion* was the journal of the [International African Service Bureau](../Organisations/ORG-0009.md) (IASB). Its first issue, vol. 1, no. 1, is dated July 1938.[^1] [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md) edited it. Adi and Sherwood record his editorship, and Høgsbjerg lists him as its "editorial director".[^2][^3] Its predecessor, also edited by James, was *Africa and the World* (1937).[^3]

## A "journal of action"

Høgsbjerg writes that James took on more responsibility for the Bureau's affairs after finishing [*A History of Negro Revolt*](../Works/WRK-0016.md) in April 1938. He wanted the new journal to be not a "literary journal or giver of advice from the mountain-tops" but "a journal of action", "a living weapon in the struggle".[^4] James's first editorial declared:

> We base ourselves upon the great masses of the people. The individual achievements of a few black men do not and cannot solve the problems of the blacks.[^5]

The Bureau also acknowledged the limits of its role. "We know our limitations," the journal said. "That task no one can do but the black people themselves."[^6] Høgsbjerg writes that, through the experience and contacts of [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md) and Braithwaite and the network around I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson, the journal "from the beginning ... began to circulate in a serious manner among colonial Africans".[^4]

## Contents

Høgsbjerg's notes identify several items in its first issues. In August 1938 (vol. 1, no. 2) James reviewed [Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md)'s [*Facing Mount Kenya*](../Works/WRK-0020.md) under the title "The Voice of Africa".[^7] In October 1938 (vol. 1, no. 4) another Bureau member, the African American LSE student William Harrison, criticised James's *A History of Negro Revolt* in "Negro Life and Letters".[^8]

## Evidence notes

- **Copies.** No issue of the journal was consulted directly for this edition. The quotations come from Høgsbjerg, who cites Brent Hayes Edwards for the first issue.[^1]
- **Run.** The sources consulted do not give the date of the last issue. James left for the United States in October 1938.[^3]
