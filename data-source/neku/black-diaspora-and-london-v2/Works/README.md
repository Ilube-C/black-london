# Works

Books, plays, music, journals, speeches and broadcasts by the people in the atlas, with links to texts that can be read online.

- [*Address in Westminster Hall*](WRK-0002.md) (Short entry) — Nelson Mandela's address to both Houses of Parliament in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996.
- [*At What a Price*](WRK-0011.md) (Short entry) — Una Marson's first play, a comedy first staged in Kingston in June 1932 and then by the League of Coloured Peoples in London in November 1933 and at the Scala Theatre in January 1934.
- [*The Black Jacobins*](WRK-0015.md) (Full entry) — C. L. R. James's history of the Haitian Revolution, The Black Jacobins: Toussaint Louverture and the San Domingo Revolution, published in London by Secker and Warburg in September 1938.
- [*Black Teacher*](WRK-0023.md) (Short entry) — Beryl Gilroy's memoir of her years as a teacher in London, first published in 1976.
- [*Caribbean Voices*](WRK-0012.md) (Short entry) — The BBC radio programme for Caribbean writing devised by Una Marson, first broadcast on 11 March 1943 and running until 1958.
- [*Facing Mount Kenya*](WRK-0020.md) (Short entry) — Jomo Kenyatta's study of Kikuyu life, based on his London School of Economics diploma thesis and published by Secker and Warburg in September 1938 with an introduction by Bronisław Malinowski.
- [*Frangipani House*](WRK-0024.md) (Short entry) — Beryl Gilroy's first novel, set in an old people's home in Guyana, published by Heinemann in 1986.
- [*The Groundings with My Brothers*](WRK-0026.md) (Short entry) — Walter Rodney's talks on Black Power and African history given in Jamaica in 1968, published in London in 1969 as the first book of Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications.
- [*Hiawatha's Wedding Feast*](WRK-0007.md) (Full entry) — Samuel Coleridge-Taylor's cantata setting part of Longfellow's The Song of Hiawatha, first performed at the Royal College of Music on 11 November 1898; it made him famous and became the first part of his Hiawatha trilogy.
- [*The History of Mary Prince, a West Indian Slave*](WRK-0006.md) (Full entry) — Mary Prince's account of her life in slavery in Bermuda, Turks Island and Antigua and of her escape in London, dictated to Susanna Strickland and published by Thomas Pringle in 1831; the first narrative of a Black British woman's escape from slavery.
- [*A History of Negro Revolt*](WRK-0016.md) (Short entry) — C. L. R. James's short survey of Black resistance and revolt, written for Raymond Postgate's Fact series and published in September 1938.
- [*The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano*](WRK-0003.md) (Full entry) — Olaudah Equiano's autobiography, published by subscription in London in 1789 from his lodging in Union Street; a founding work of Black British writing and an abolitionist text.
- [*International African Opinion*](WRK-0013.md) (Full entry) — The journal of the International African Service Bureau, first issued in July 1938 with C. L. R. James as its editor; it circulated among colonial Africans and argued for anti-colonial struggle based on "the great masses of the people".
- [*The Keys*](WRK-0010.md) (Full entry) — The quarterly journal of the League of Coloured Peoples, published from 1933, which printed the League's aims in every issue; Una Marson edited it in its early years.
- [*Letters of the Late Ignatius Sancho, an African*](WRK-0004.md) (Full entry) — The collected letters of Ignatius Sancho, published in two volumes in London in 1782, two years after his death, with a memoir of his life; they were reissued in a third edition in 1784.
- [*Minuets, Cotillons & Country Dances*](WRK-0005.md) (Short entry) — Ignatius Sancho's first published collection of dance music, 'Composed by an African' and dedicated to the Duke of Buccleuch, published in London in the 1760s.
- [*The New African*](WRK-0018.md) (Short entry) — The monthly paper of the West African National Secretariat, edited in London by Kwame Nkrumah, which first appeared in March 1946.
- [*New Left Review*](WRK-0025.md) (Short entry) — The journal of the British New Left, formed in January 1960 from Universities and Left Review and The New Reasoner, with Stuart Hall as its first editor.
- [*Sanders of the River*](WRK-0021.md) (Short entry) — Alexander Korda's 1935 film of Edgar Wallace's colonial adventure stories, starring Paul Robeson as Bosambo, in which Jomo Kenyatta appeared as an extra.
- [*To the Nations of the World*](WRK-0001.md) (Full entry) — The address adopted by the First Pan-African Conference in London in July 1900, signed by Alexander Walters, Henry B. Brown, Henry Sylvester Williams and W. E. B. Du Bois; it contains the first version of Du Bois's phrase on "the problem of the color line".
- [*Toussaint Louverture (Play)*](WRK-0014.md) (Short entry) — C. L. R. James's play about the leader of the Haitian Revolution, written in 1934 and staged by the Stage Society at the Westminster Theatre on 15 and 16 March 1936 with Paul Robeson in the title role.
- [*Towards Colonial Freedom*](WRK-0019.md) (Short entry) — Kwame Nkrumah's pamphlet against colonial rule, drafted in the United States and published in London in 1947.
- [*Twenty-Four Negro Melodies, Op. 59*](WRK-0008.md) (Short entry) — Samuel Coleridge-Taylor's Twenty-Four Negro Melodies, transcribed for the piano, Op. 59, published in Boston in 1905 with a preface by Booker T. Washington.
- [*Wasu (Journal)*](WRK-0009.md) (Full entry) — The journal of the West African Students' Union, launched in London in March 1926; its Yoruba title means "Preach", and it carried political and cultural writing by West Africans, including Ladipo Solanke.
- [*West Indian Gazette*](WRK-0022.md) (Full entry) — The monthly newspaper founded and edited by Claudia Jones in London in 1958, later titled West Indian Gazette and Afro-Asian-Caribbean News; it campaigned against racism, sponsored the first Caribbean Carnival and closed after her death.
