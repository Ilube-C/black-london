# How this atlas was made


*The Black Diaspora and London* describes twenty-five people of African descent and the places, organisations, events and writings that connect them with London, from the eighteenth century to the late twentieth. This second edition keeps every person, record reference and connection of the first edition, and adds longer entries for places, organisations, events and works, a page for every connection, and a library of the sources.

## Evidence

- **Every statement is cited.** Footnotes give the source and, wherever the source has page numbers, the page. Each footnote links to the source's entry in the [source library](Sources/README.md), which gives the full reference, a link to the original where one is freely available, and the other places in the atlas where that source is cited.
- **Sources that disagree are reported, not reconciled.** Where sources give different dates, names or numbers, the entry says so and cites each. These points are collected under *Evidence notes*.
- **Inference is marked as inference.** When a historian infers something that the records do not state, the entry names the historian and says that it is an inference.
- **Meetings.** An entry says that two people met only when a source records a meeting or a personal exchange. Being at the same event, belonging to the same organisation or living in the same district is not treated as evidence of a meeting. Connection pages say which kind of evidence they rest on.
- **What a meeting decided.** For meetings and gatherings, entries report what the surviving records say was discussed and decided. Where a source records only that a meeting took place, the entry says so.
- **Quotations.** Works in the public domain are quoted more fully. Modern works under copyright are quoted only in short passages.
- **What was consulted.** Each source entry says how it was consulted: a copy of a book, an online text, or only a catalogue description. Material that was not seen is not described as if it had been.

## Places

- **Present-day status** is given only from official records, such as the National Heritage List for England or a council's register of listed buildings, and is dated.
- **Positions** are given only where an official record supplies a grid reference, and the record and its precision are named. Most places therefore have no position yet.
- **Records not published.** Some places, organisations and works examined during research are not published because no consulted source connects them with the people in the atlas. Their reference numbers are not reused.

## Record references

Every entry ends with a record reference (for example PLC-0001). These references are permanent and match the downloadable data, so that each entry can be found in the data files and on a future map.
