# Anti-Slavery Society Office

*The office of the Anti-Slavery Society at 18 Aldermanbury, City of London, where Mary Prince sought help in November 1828.*

The [Anti-Slavery Society](../Organisations/ORG-0013.md) had its office at 18 Aldermanbury, in the City of London.[^1] Thomas Pringle, its secretary, writes that "About the latter end of November 1828" [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md) found her way to the Society's office in Aldermanbury.[^2] He took her to the Society's honorary solicitor, George Stephen, to see whether her freedom could be secured if she returned to Antigua.[^2] Prince herself recalls going back to "the Anti-Slavery office to ask a supply" when her savings ran out.[^3] The title page of the 1831 *History* gives the address of the Society's supplier to anti-slavery associations as "Joseph Phillips, 18, Aldermanbury".[^4] Vigne records that the Agency Committee, a more radical abolitionist body, later shared the building.[^5]
