# Solly Terrace, Pentonville

*Solly Terrace, Pentonville, where Thomas Pringle and his wife lived and where Mary Prince worked for them as a domestic servant from 1829.*

After leaving her enslavers, [Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md) entered the service of Thomas Pringle, secretary of the [Anti-Slavery Society](../Organisations/ORG-0013.md), and his wife.[^1] Vigne writes that she moved in with the Pringles in Pentonville as their domestic servant in 1829. He places their home in Solly Terrace, where they lived until they moved away in March 1833.[^2] In this household her life story was taken down by Susanna Strickland, Pringle's friend and lodger, and published by Pringle in 1831 as [*The History of Mary Prince*](../Works/WRK-0006.md).[^2] Prince writes that she went "into the service of Mr and Mrs Pringle, where I have been ever since", and that "My dear mistress teaches me daily to read the word of God".[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Address.** The street name comes from Vigne. The *History* itself does not give the Pringles' address.[^2][^3]

## Connected people

- **[Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md)** — Mary Prince lived with Thomas and Susan Pringle in Solly Terrace, Pentonville, as their domestic servant from 1829.[^4]
