# 9 Heathcote Street

*The top-floor flat at 9 Heathcote Street, Bloomsbury, formerly Nancy Cunard's, where C. L. R. James lived from 1934.*

[C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md) moved from Hampstead to a top-floor flat at 9 Heathcote Street, Bloomsbury, in 1934. Høgsbjerg notes that it had been the writer Nancy Cunard's flat.[^1] In these years James immersed himself in Trotskyist politics and researched the Haitian Revolution, the subject of his play [*Toussaint Louverture*](../Works/WRK-0014.md) (1936) and of [*The Black Jacobins*](../Works/WRK-0015.md) (1938).[^1][^2] He gave the address in a letter to the *New Statesman* of 28 March 1936.[^3] He also led the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](../Organisations/ORG-0008.md) in 1935.[^4]
