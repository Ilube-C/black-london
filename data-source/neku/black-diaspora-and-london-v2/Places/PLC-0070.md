# Partisan Coffee House

*The Partisan coffee house at 7 Carlisle Street, Soho, which shared premises with the offices of Universities and Left Review, the journal Stuart Hall co-founded.*

In the late 1950s the New Left gathered at 7 Carlisle Street, Soho. [Stuart Hall](../People/PER-0022.md)'s memoir describes the offices of *Universities and Left Review* there, with a clubhouse, a library and a coffee bar.[^1] He recalls editing the journal in Soho in the afternoons, and the Partisan Café and its circle, including the historian Raphael Samuel.[^1] Hall was a founding editor of *Universities and Left Review*. He became the first editor of its successor, [*New Left Review*](../Works/WRK-0025.md), in 1960.[^2][^3] The Bishopsgate Institute's New Left Archive documents the movement.[^3]

## Evidence notes

- **Name.** The memoir refers to the "Partisan Café". The atlas's name for the place, Partisan Coffee House, is a variant.[^1]

## Connected people

- **[Stuart Hall](../People/PER-0022.md)** — Stuart Hall edited Universities and Left Review at its Soho offices above the Partisan Coffee House, 7 Carlisle Street, in the late 1950s.[^4]
