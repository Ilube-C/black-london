# Memorial Hall, Farringdon Street

*The Memorial Hall in Farringdon Street, London, a meeting hall used in the 1930s by the League of Coloured Peoples, the International African Friends of Abyssinia and other anti-colonial groups.*

The Memorial Hall in Farringdon Street was one of the halls that London's Black and anti-colonial organisations hired for public meetings in the 1930s. The sources consulted place four documented meetings there. They link it to [Harold Moody](../People/PER-0012.md), [Amy Ashwood Garvey](../People/PER-0004.md), [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md), [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md) and [Una Marson](../People/PER-0013.md).

## Meetings recorded there

- **28 July 1935: the International African Friends of Abyssinia.** Høgsbjerg writes that the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](../Organisations/ORG-0008.md) held its second public meeting "at the Memorial Hall, Farringdon Street" on Sunday 28 July 1935. He quotes *West Africa*'s report of "A crowded meeting of sympathisers with Abyssinia ... presided over by Mrs. A. A. Garvey". The first speaker was C. L. R. James.[^1]
- **October 1935: the Independent Labour Party rally.** On the night that Italy attacked Ethiopia, Høgsbjerg writes, "1,200 people packed into an ILP rally in London's Memorial Hall". There they heard James, chair of the IAFA and of the ILP's Finchley branch, with the party chairman James Maxton MP and Fenner Brockway.[^2]
- **31 July 1936: the Pan-African Federation.** Padmore "convened a public meeting of the Pan-African Federation at Memorial Hall, on Farringdon Street". His fellow speakers included Arthur Creech Jones and William Mellor of the Labour Party, Fenner Brockway, Dorothy Woodman and the Indian nationalist Krishna Menon.[^3]
- **The League of Coloured Peoples and the Caribbean, 1938.** Fryer records that Moody presided at a protest meeting "in the Memorial Hall, Farringdon Street" during the Jamaican strikes of 1938. It carried a resolution demanding land redistribution, universal free education, universal adult suffrage and "Federation of the West Indies with complete self-government".[^4] Jarrett-Macauley describes the League's "special meeting on the West Indian situation at the Memorial Hall in Farringdon" on 30 September 1938. That was the day Marson gave evidence to the Moyne Commission. At the meeting she shared the platform with Lord Olivier, a former Governor of Jamaica, and the Bishop of Jamaica.[^5]

## A regular meeting place

Jarrett-Macauley names the Memorial Hall, "where League meetings were held", as one of Una Marson's "regular haunts" after she arrived in London in 1932. The other was the West African students' hostel in [Camden Road](../Places/PLC-0035.md).[^6] The [League of Coloured Peoples](../Organisations/ORG-0019.md) itself had been founded at the [Central YMCA](../Places/PLC-0040.md) in 1931.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **Address.** Most sources give Farringdon Street. Jarrett-Macauley writes "Farringdon Road" in one passage and "Farringdon" in another.[^6][^5]
- **The two Caribbean meetings.** Fryer's protest meeting and Jarrett-Macauley's special meeting of 30 September 1938 may be the same occasion, but the sources do not say so.
- **Same platform, not a meeting between people.** Several atlas figures spoke at meetings in the hall. The sources show who shared a platform on a particular day. They do not by themselves show other contact between them.
- **Other uses.** Sayers records a rehearsal of a Coleridge-Taylor choral work "in the Memorial Hall" by the London Choral Society, but he does not give the street. It is not attributed to this building.[^7]
- **The building today.** No official record of the hall's present status was consulted for this edition.
