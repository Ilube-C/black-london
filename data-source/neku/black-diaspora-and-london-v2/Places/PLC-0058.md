[Claudia Jones](../People/PER-0018.md) edited the [*West Indian Gazette*](../Works/WRK-0022.md) from 250 Brixton Road, in south London. Donald Hinds, who worked on the paper, writes that "The Gazette spent the first five of its seven years of its existence in two crowded, chaotic and untidy rooms above Theo Campbell's record shop at 250 Brixton Road".[^1] The Serpentine's 2021 guide to London's places of belonging lists "West Indian Gazette, 250 Brixton Road". It describes the paper as founded by Claudia Jones "and widely considered to have been Britain's first major black newspaper", operating from 1958 to 1965.[^2]

## The office at work

Hinds wrote in 1965 that the paper had "topped its printing order of thirty thousand more than once". He went on:

> Its premises along the Brixton Road were near one of the largest concentrations of West Indians in Britain. Its cramped offices were a beehive of activities which rivalled the West Indian High Commission Office in Mayfair.[^3]

From the office, Hinds recalled, a deputation went to the Home Office to protest about the treatment of Black people, especially in Notting Hill. Other deputations went to local government offices and police stations. People "fearing for their life and property would come, waiting impatiently to pour out their troubles".[^3] The building was shared. Jones took legal advice from John Morris of Wilkes & Co., "who had chambers above the office of the Gazette at 250 Brixton Road".[^4]

## Carnival

The *Gazette* sponsored the first [Caribbean Carnival](../Events/EVT-0019.md), held indoors at [St Pancras Town Hall](../Places/PLC-0059.md) on 30 January 1959.[^5] A former *Gazette* reporter, speaking at a symposium printed in Sherwood's book, insisted that the Carnival "didn't start there, in Notting Hill, ... it started in the room above Theo Campbell's record shop in 250 Brixton Road".[^6]

## Leaving Brixton Road

Relations with the landlord did not last. Sherwood's book records that "Relationships with Theo Campbell, who owned the premises at 250 Brixton Road, did not improve and the Gazette found new premises off Coldharbour Lane in Brixton".[^7] A letter from the *Gazette* office quoted in the same book complains that "Theo" was dispatching a rival paper "from here now".[^8]

## Evidence notes

- **Dates at the address.** Hinds gives "the first five of its seven years"; the Serpentine guide dates the paper 1958–65. Together these suggest about 1958 to 1963 at 250 Brixton Road. No source consulted gives the date of the move.[^1][^2]
- **Spelling.** The text of Hinds's chapter as consulted reads "Thea Campbell's record shop" at one point. Elsewhere the book names the owner as Theo Campbell, and that form is used here.[^1][^7]
- **The building today.** No official record of 250 Brixton Road's present status was consulted for this edition.
