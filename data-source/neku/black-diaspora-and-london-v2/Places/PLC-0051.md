The Trinidadian writer and political activist [C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md) spent the last years of his life at 165 Railton Road, Brixton. English Heritage, whose blue plaque marks the building, writes that he lived there "from 1981 until his death eight years later". His "cramped second-floor flat", it continues, was "located above the offices of the journal *Race Today*" and "became a place of pilgrimage for students, journalists and politicians".[^1]

## Race Today and the Collective

*Race Today* began as the journal of the Institute of Race Relations. Bunce and Field describe how, under the editorship of Darcus Howe from the end of 1973, it broke with the Institute and became the paper of the Race Today Collective.[^2] They write that the Collective's offices, "at 74 Shakespeare Road, and from mid-1982 at 165 Railton Road, became a hive of activity". The offices were frequented by "local people, councillors, community organizers, artists of various descriptions and, on a good day, world-class cricketers".[^3] A list of periodicals compiled in 1985 for *The Heart of the Race* gives the journal's address as "Race Today, The Basement, 165 Railton Rd".[^4]

Bunce and Field describe James's relationship with the Collective as a close one. They quote Howe, speaking in 1992: "he lived with us, the small organisation, for the last ten years of he life ... He lived upstairs above the offices."[^3] They describe members of the Collective cooking for him, polishing his shoes and fetching wine and flowers when he was entertaining.[^3] During the Brixton uprising of April 1981, Bunce and Field write, James "was by that point living in the flat above the Race Today offices", and would "debrief all he met about their lives and families".[^5]

## Railton Road

Railton Road was at the centre of Black political life in Brixton in the 1970s and 1980s. The squat at [121 Railton Road](../Places/PLC-0081.md), further along the same road, was associated with [Olive Morris](../People/PER-0025.md) and housed Sabarr Bookshop.[^6] Bunce and Field note that when *Race Today* left the Institute of Race Relations, its equipment was taken for a few days to Farrukh Dhondy's squat on Railton Road. It then moved, with Olive Morris's help, to another squat on Shakespeare Road.[^7]

## The building today

English Heritage erected a blue plaque in 2004. It reads: "C.L.R. JAMES 1901-1989 West Indian Writer and Political Activist lived and died here".[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **When James and Race Today came to 165 Railton Road.** English Heritage dates James's residence from 1981. Bunce and Field date the Race Today offices' move to 165 Railton Road to "mid-1982". They also describe James as already living "in the flat above the Race Today offices" in April 1981, when the offices were in Shakespeare Road.[^1][^3][^5] The sources consulted do not resolve whether James lived at 165 Railton Road before the offices moved there.
- **Length of stay.** Howe speaks of "the last ten years" of James's life. English Heritage gives eight years.[^3][^1]
- **Position.** No official record giving a grid reference was consulted, so no position is recorded.

## Connected people

- **[C. L. R. James](../People/PER-0014.md)** — C. L. R. James lived at 165 Railton Road, Brixton, above the offices of Race Today, in the last years of his life; he died there in 1989.[^8]
