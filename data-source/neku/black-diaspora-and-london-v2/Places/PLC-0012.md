# 22 Cranleigh Street (Cranleigh House)

*The flat at 22 Cranleigh Street (Cranleigh House), Somers Town, where George Padmore lived from 1941 to 1957 and where, according to Nkrumah, he and Padmore organised the 1945 Pan-African Congress.*

The Trinidadian Pan-Africanist [George Padmore](../People/PER-0005.md) lived at 22 Cranleigh Street in Somers Town, near Euston, between 1941 and 1957.[^1] He shared the flat with his partner Dorothy Pizer.[^2] Through the 1940s and 1950s it was a meeting place for anti-colonial activists from Africa, the Caribbean and Asia. The *Camden New Journal* quotes the activist Selma James: "Every anti-colonial activist organising against British imperialism came to 22 Cranleigh House, Cranleigh Street".[^1]

## A working flat

Pizer, a former member of the London Communist Party, earned a steady income as a secretary. Adi and Sherwood write that she "greatly assisted" Padmore "with both his research, his writing and the hosting of countless visitors".[^2] Selma James recalled that "George and Dorothy would give dinners to all the people who came to them".[^1] Murray-Brown describes Padmore at the start of the Second World War "in his flat in Cranleigh Houses, nearly next door to Dinah Stock's", a friend of [Jomo Kenyatta](../People/PER-0017.md).[^3]

## The 1945 Congress

In 1945 [Kwame Nkrumah](../People/PER-0016.md) arrived in London and joined Padmore, T. R. Makonnen and Peter Abrahams in preparing the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](../Events/EVT-0004.md). He writes that "George Padmore and I became joint secretaries of the Organisation Committee and we worked night and day in George's flat". They sat in Padmore's small kitchen, the table covered with papers, while Padmore typed.[^4] Sherwood notes that a longer walk, or three stops on the Underground, would have taken Nkrumah from his lodgings to Padmore's flat, "where undoubtedly he would have met most anti-imperialist thinkers and activists living in or passing through London".[^5] She also quotes a Sri Lankan student, T. B. Subasinghe, who met Padmore in 1937 and "was a frequent visitor to Padmore's flat".[^6]

## Remembered

In 2011 the *Camden New Journal* reported the unveiling of a blue plaque recording that Padmore "lived at 22 Cranleigh Street between 1941 and 1957".[^1] In 2021 the same paper interviewed Professor Esther Leslie about "The flat that changed history". She said: "People who went on to change the world and shape 20th century history were always in and out of this small flat".[^7]

## Evidence notes

- **Name of the building.** The sources call it 22 Cranleigh Street, Cranleigh House and Cranleigh Houses.[^1][^3]
- **Who visited.** The sources describe the flat as a gathering place in general terms. Apart from Nkrumah's own account of working there, the sources consulted do not show which named atlas figures visited it or when.
- **Dates.** Murray-Brown places Padmore in Cranleigh Houses at the start of the war, in 1939 or 1940, slightly earlier than the 1941 given in the 2011 report.[^3][^1]
- **The plaque and the building today.** The 2011 plaque is known here from a newspaper report. The scheme that put it up, and the building's present status, were not checked against an official record for this edition.
