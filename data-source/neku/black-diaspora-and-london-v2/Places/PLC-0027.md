# Orange Tree Public House

*The Orange Tree public house in Orange Street, Red Lion Square, Holborn, where the Chartist "Ulterior Committee" met in August 1848 and where police arrested eleven conspirators on 16 August.*

The Orange Tree was a public house in Orange Street, off Red Lion Square, in Holborn. In August 1848 it was the meeting place of the Chartist and Irish Confederate "Ulterior Committee". Chase describes this as a conspiracy "to foment a general rising, to be signalled by co-ordinated arson attacks on key London buildings".[^1] The plot ended there on 16 August 1848, when police raided the pub (see [the Orange Tree raid](../Events/EVT-0013.md)).[^2] Its importance to the atlas lies in [William Cuffay](../People/PER-0009.md), the Black Chartist tailor who became the committee's secretary and was later transported for life.[^1][^3]

## The committee's meetings

Chase writes that Cuffay "was not among the originators of the conspiracy and first attended the committee only on 4 August, but within eleven days he became its secretary".[^1] The trial record in the Old Bailey Proceedings gives a glimpse of the meetings. The informer Thomas Powell told the court that "the next meeting I attended was at the Orange Tree Orange-street, Red Lion-square, on Monday night, 14th—I went up stairs". He added that "to the best of my belief, there were twenty-five persons there".[^3] Another informer, George Davis, testified: "we met at the Orange Tree, Red Lion-square on the 14th—there were between twenty and thirty present". His list of those present includes Cuffay.[^3]

## The raid, 16 August 1848

Chase gives the end of the conspiracy:

> The eleven leading conspirators assembled at 5.00 p.m. on Wednesday 16 August in The Orange Tree pub, near Holborn, to make the final preparations ahead of the muster that night. They had been there scarcely an hour when police poured into the room and arrested them all.[^2]

A second group was arrested the same evening at the Angel Tavern, south of the river.[^2] A witness at the trial, Charles Goodfellow, said that he had arranged to meet a man at the Orange Tree that evening. He arrived at about half past five and saw the police crossing Red Lion Square. He could not get in "because the police went in and shut the door, and would not let any one in".[^3]

Cuffay was not among those taken at the Orange Tree. He was arrested at home on 18 August.[^4]

## Afterwards

Six of the "Orange Tree conspirators, as they quickly became known", were transported, and fifteen others received sentences of eighteen months to two years.[^5] Cuffay was tried at the Old Bailey with William Lacey and Thomas Fay in the September 1848 session ([see the trial](../Events/EVT-0014.md)).[^3] At the trials the defence sought to discredit Powell. Chase notes that Harney and others argued that Powell "bore prime responsibility for the Orange Tree conspiracy".[^5]

## Evidence notes

- **Location.** The Old Bailey Proceedings place the pub in "Orange-street, Red Lion-square". Chase gives "near Holborn".[^3][^1] No street number is given in the sources consulted.
- **Numbers.** Powell estimated twenty-five people at the meeting of 14 August, and Davis "between twenty and thirty". Chase gives eleven conspirators at the Orange Tree on 16 August.[^3][^2]
- **Informers' evidence.** The details of the meetings come from police informers, whose reliability the defence challenged at the trial.[^5]
- **The building today.** The sources consulted do not record whether the building survives.
