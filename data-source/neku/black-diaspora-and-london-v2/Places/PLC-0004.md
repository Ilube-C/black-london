# Westminster Hall

*The medieval hall of the Palace of Westminster, where Nelson Mandela, as President of South Africa, addressed both Houses of Parliament on 11 July 1996.*

Westminster Hall is the oldest part of the Palace of Westminster. Historic England's list entry for the Palace dates the hall to "1097-99", remodelled in 1394–1401. It singles out "a vast hammerbeam roof of an exceptionally early date and scale".[^1] Its place in this atlas comes from one event: [Nelson Mandela](../People/PER-0002.md)'s address to both Houses of Parliament on 11 July 1996 (see [the address in Westminster Hall](../Events/EVT-0006.md)).[^2][^3]

## Mandela's address, 11 July 1996

The printed programme of the occasion sets out the ceremony minute by minute. At 9.45 a.m. the North Door and other doors of the hall were opened to admit members and guests. At 11.05 the Lord Chancellor and the Speaker conducted "Mr Nelson Mandela into Westminster Hall". The band of the Grenadier Guards then played the South African national anthem.[^2] Mandela's daughter, Princess Zenani Mandela-Dlamini, attended with him.[^2]

The Lord Chancellor, Lord Mackay of Clashfern, welcomed him "to Westminster Hall". He recalled that the hall had "witnessed the slow evolution of our own democracy". He explained that the two Houses only "at rare intervals" invited a guest "to address both Houses of Parliament assembled in this Great Hall".[^2] In reply, Mandela set his visit against two centuries of British involvement in southern Africa: "Perhaps the fact that we are here today might serve to close a circle which is two hundred years old."[^2] The Speaker, Betty Boothroyd, thanked the President. She said that she had never expected "to stand here under the famous hammer beams of Westminster Hall" and see Mandela honoured by both Houses.[^4] The Parliamentary Archives hold the speeches of the day.[^3] Parliament's own record notes that Mandela "addressed both Houses of Parliament in Westminster Hall on 11 July 1996".[^5]

The speech is entered in the atlas as a work ([Address in Westminster Hall](../Works/WRK-0002.md)).

## Earlier connection

In 1962, as a fugitive travelling on ANC business, Mandela visited the Houses of Parliament as a sightseer with [Oliver Tambo](../People/PER-0020.md) (see [Mandela's visit of 1962](../Events/EVT-0005.md)).[^6] The sources consulted do not say whether that visit included Westminster Hall.

## The building today

Westminster Hall forms part of the "Houses of Parliament and The Palace of Westminster", listed at Grade I on the National Heritage List for England. The entry was first listed on 5 February 1970.[^1] The list entry gives a single National Grid Reference, TQ 30267 79504, for the whole Palace. The atlas's position is converted from it and so marks the Palace as a whole rather than the hall.[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Position.** No separate grid reference for Westminster Hall was found in the official records consulted.
