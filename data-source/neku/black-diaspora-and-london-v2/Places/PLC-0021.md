# Leigh Street, Bloomsbury

*Leigh Street, Bloomsbury, where Mary Prince lodged with her enslavers, the Woods, after arriving in London in 1828.*

[Mary Prince](../People/PER-0008.md) came to London in 1828 with her enslavers, John and Margaret Wood. In her *History* she describes their lodgings in Leigh Street, Bloomsbury.[^1] There Mrs Wood set her and the cook to wash five bags of clothes, although she was ill with rheumatism. When Prince protested that she had come only to nurse the child, the Woods "opened the door and bade me get out".[^1] She eventually left the house and, with help from Mash, a shoeblack, found lodging elsewhere. In November 1828 she went to the [Anti-Slavery Society](../Organisations/ORG-0013.md)'s office at [18 Aldermanbury](../Places/PLC-0023.md).[^1][^2] Her account of these months was later central to the libel trials of 1833 ([Wood v. Pringle](../Events/EVT-0011.md)).
