## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): Lived at 9 Heathcote Street, Bloomsbury, for several years from 1934. — p. 9: '9 Heathcote Street, where he lived for several years beginning in 1934'; p. 236 n. 85: he gives his address as 9 Heathcote Street in a letter of March 28, 1936
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): Founded and led the International African Friends of Abyssinia in 1935. — p. 6: 'chair of the International African Friends of Abyssinia (Ethiopia), 1935–36'; p. 90: 'James became chair of the resulting International African Friends of Abyssinia'
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): His play Toussaint Louverture was staged in 1936, with Paul Robeson leading the cast. — p. 159: 'staged by the prestigious Stage Society on March 15 and 16, 1936, at the Westminster Theatre'
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): Published A History of Negro Revolt. — p. 7: 'Author of A History of Negro Revolt (1938)'; pp. 114–15
- [Paul Robeson](Atlas/People/PER-0019.md): Led the cast of C. L. R. James's play Toussaint Louverture in 1936. — p. 6: 'performed by the Stage Society in London’s Westminster Theatre in 1936 and starring Paul Robeson in the title role'
- [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md): George Padmore and Jomo Kenyatta worked together from 1930, in Padmore's Comintern-backed International Trade Union Committee of Negro Workers and later in London's IAFA and IASB. — p. 91 (IAFA officers: Kenyatta honorary secretary; Padmore on the executive)
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): C. L. R. James and Jomo Kenyatta worked together in the IAFA (James chairman, Kenyatta honorary secretary); James introduced Kenyatta to his publisher and reviewed Facing Mount Kenya. — p. 91 (IAFA officers); p. 239 n. 155 (James's 'fraternal but difficult relationship with Kenyatta'; review 'The Voice of Africa', International African Opinion, August 1938)
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): C. L. R. James and Amy Ashwood Garvey, who had met through the League of Coloured Peoples, together revived the committee that became the IAFA in 1935, and both were officers of the IASB in 1937. — p. 90 (met through the LCP; 'Together they revived an ad hoc committee'); p. 91 (IAFA officers); pp. 110–112 (IASB officers); p. 10 (James's memory of her as 'a wonderful cook')
- [Stuart Hall](Atlas/People/PER-0022.md): Stuart Hall held a recorded conversation with C. L. R. James, published as 'A Conversation with C. L. R. James' (1996), and wrote 'C.L.R. James: A Portrait' (1992). — pp. 161 ('As he puts it to Stuart Hall'), 206 ('as he later told Stuart Hall'); bibliography, p. 272 (S. Hall, 'A Conversation with C. L. R. James', in Rethinking C. L. R. James, ed. Grant Farred, Oxford: Blackwell, 1996, pp. 15–44; 'C.L.R. James: A Portrait', in C. L. R. James's Caribbean, Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 1992, pp. 3–16)
