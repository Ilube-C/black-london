## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Born 1905, died 1965. — p. 1: 'born on 6 February 1905'; p. 224: 'died on 6 May 1965'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Origin Jamaica. — p. 1: born on 6 February 1905; 'Sharon village, her birthplace in rural Jamaica'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Lodged with the Moody family at 164 Queen's Road, Peckham, from July 1932 until spring 1935. — p. 46: 'found lodgings at 164 Queen’s Road, Peckham'; p. 80: 'left the Moodys’ busy household in spring 1935'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Secretary of the League of Coloured Peoples in London (Busby); its unpaid assistant secretary from autumn 1933 (Jarrett-Macauley). — p. 52: 'in autumn 1933 became the League’s unpaid assistant secretary'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Edited the League of Coloured Peoples' journal, The Keys. — p. 54: 'She became editor of The Keys'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Directed her play At What a Price at the Scala Theatre, Charlotte Street, in a three-night run from 15 January 1934, after a League performance on 23 November 1933. — p. 53: a League performance 'on the evening of 23 November 1933'; 'After Christmas, the play transferred for a three-night run, beginning on 15 January, at the Scala Theatre in Charlotte Street'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Worked for the Abyssinian (Ethiopian) Legation in Kensington from October 1935, through the Emperor's arrival in 1936. — p. 99: 'Una went over to Kensington, to the Abyssinian Legation'; called there 'the day she arrived back, 2 October'; p. 102: letters 'pouring into Princes Gate'
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Radio broadcaster with the BBC World Service during her second stay in England, from 1938. — p. 149: 'On 3 March 1941 ... Una was appointed full-time programme assistant on the Empire Service'; p. 173: taken back to Jamaica in April 1946
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md): Started the BBC programme Caribbean Voices. — p. 158: 'devising a similiar programme which she called Caribbean Voices'; first broadcast on 11 March 1943
