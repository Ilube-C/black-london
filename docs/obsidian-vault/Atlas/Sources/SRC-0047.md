## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md): Arrived in London. — p. 15: 'Claudia arrived in London in December 1955.'; p. 26: the Smith Act and the McCarran Act 'under which she was deported'
- [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md): Organised the indoor Caribbean Carnival. — p. 156: 'on Friday 30 January 1959 it was able to mount what was announced as the Caribbean Carnival, at St Pancras Town Hall'
- [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md): Claudia Jones's newspaper, the West Indian Gazette, had offices at 250 Brixton Road. — p. 125: 'two crowded, chaotic and untidy rooms above Thea Campbell's record shop at 250 Brixton Road'
- [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md): Claudia Jones and Paul Robeson were friends and political colleagues in New York and London; in 1959–60 her West Indian Gazette held receptions and a concert featuring Robeson. — pp. 23, 25–26 (1955: parole delegation; Robeson visited her on the afternoon of her farewell reception and sent a message); pp. 44–46 (friendship in Britain; 'Meet Paul Robeson' receptions of 27 June and 18 July 1959; Gazette anniversary concert, 28 September [1960])
- [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md): Amy Ashwood Garvey and Claudia Jones were friends and colleagues in London; Jones was general secretary of Ashwood Garvey's Association for the Advancement of Coloured People, founded in 1958. — pp. 44–45 (among Jones's 'earliest friendships in Britain'; AACP; letters and telegrams of 1964); pp. 92–93 (AACP; meeting at 1 Bassett Road, September 1958; 'Claudia, clearly a friend and colleague of Amy's ... was the general secretary')
