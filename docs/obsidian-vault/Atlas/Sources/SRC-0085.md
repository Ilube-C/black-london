## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md): Born at 15 Theobald's Road, Holborn, on 15 August 1875. — p. 2: 'at 15, Theobald's Road, Red Lion Square, in Holborn, and there on August 15, 1875, a son was born to them'
- [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md): Studied at the Royal College of Music from the Christmas Term 1890 to the end of the Easter Term 1897. — p. 16: 'commenced his study there at the Christmas Term, 1890'; p. 42: 'He left the Royal College at the end of the Easter Term, 1897'
- [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md): Hiawatha's Wedding Feast was first performed at a Royal College of Music concert on 11 November 1898, conducted by Sir Charles Stanford. — p. 56: 'the year 1898'; p. 58: 'the college concert on November 11, at which it was to be produced'; p. 59: 'Sir Charles Stanford took the baton'
- [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md): In a letter of 3 January 1904 he called Du Bois's The Souls of Black Folk 'about the finest book I have ever read by a coloured man, and one of the best by any author, white or black'. — pp. 149–50: letter from 10 Upper Grove, South Norwood, to Mr Hilyer, 3 January 1904; Sayers identifies the book as 'Dr. Du Bois's "The Souls of Black Folk"'
