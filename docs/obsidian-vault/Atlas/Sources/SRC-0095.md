## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md): Resided at 165 Railton Road. — ch. 12: the Collective's offices were at 74 Shakespeare Road 'and from mid-1982 at 165 Railton Road'; Howe (1992): James 'lived upstairs above the offices'
- [Altheia Jones-LeCointe](Atlas/People/PER-0024.md): Helped set up the action group behind the Mangrove demonstration of 9 August 1970, and was tried for riot at the march. — ch. 8: the demonstration 'was organized by the Action Committee for Defence of the Mangrove in conjunction with the Black Panther Movement'; before the march 'Next to speak was Althea Jones-Lecointe, the driving force behind the British Black Panthers'
- [Altheia Jones-LeCointe](Atlas/People/PER-0024.md): Conducted her own defence at the 1971 Mangrove Nine trial at the Old Bailey; all nine defendants were acquitted of the most serious charges. — ch. 10: 'The trial began in Court 1 of The Old Bailey on 5 October 1971 and would last 12 weeks'; Howe and Jones-Lecointe 'would defend themselves'
- [Jessica Huntley](Atlas/People/PER-0023.md): On 18 January 1981 attended the meeting at John La Rose's home in Finsbury Park called to discuss holding the Book Fair, first held in April 1982; later one of the fair's joint directors. — ch. 14: on the day of the fire Howe was at a meeting with John La Rose and 'Jessica and Erica [sic] Huntley' at La Rose's home in Finsbury Park 'to discuss the proposal to hold a Book Fair of Radical and Third World Books'; the fire began 'in the early hours of Sunday, 18 January' [1981]
- [Olive Morris](Atlas/People/PER-0025.md): One of the 'Old Bailey Three', arrested with Darcus Howe and Abdul Macintosh after a scuffle with police outside the Old Bailey on 20 June 1971. — ch. 13: 'on 20 June 1971, Howe was arrested after a scuffle between demonstrators and police outside the Old Bailey'; 'The ‘Old Bailey Three’ comprised Howe, Olive Morris and Abdul Macintosh'
