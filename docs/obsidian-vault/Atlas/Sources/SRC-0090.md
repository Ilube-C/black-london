## Passages recorded from this source

These are the passages, page references and short quotations recorded from this source as evidence for the atlas's data.

- [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md): Helped organise Nigerian Progress Union. — p. 28: 'largely due to Amy Ashwood Garvey’s enthusiasm, went on to form the Nigerian Progress Union (NPU). The NPU was founded by thirteen Nigerian students in London in July 1924'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Wrote for Wasu, the WASU journal launched in March 1926, and for a time had editorial responsibility for it. — p. 34: 'Wasu, the journal of the Union, was launched in March 1926'; p. 122: Solanke 'was relieved of the responsibility of editorial work'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): First warden of Africa House, the WASU hostel opened on Camden Road in 1933. — p. 57: Solanke and Olu Obisanya, 'the future Mrs Solanke', preparing the hostel; 'The hostel officially opened on 9 March 1933'; p. 84, n. 29: 'Africa House was the name given to WASU hostel'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Birth year disputed: c. 1884 (Adi & Sherwood, 2003) or 1886 (Adi, 1998). — p. 47, n. 6: 'Ladipo Solanke (1886-1958)'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Origin Nigeria. — p. 27: 'the Nigerian students in London, led by Ladipo Solanke'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Studied law at University College London, 1923–7. — p. 47, n. 6: 'He was a law student at UCL from 1923-1927'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Founded the Nigerian Progress Union in 1924 with the assistance of Amy Ashwood Garvey. — p. 28: 'largely due to Amy Ashwood Garvey’s enthusiasm, went on to form the Nigerian Progress Union (NPU). The NPU was founded by thirteen Nigerian students in London in July 1924'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Founded the West African Students' Union in 1925 with Herbert Bankole-Bright and became its first secretary-general. — p. 32: 'In August 1925, at a meeting of West African students in London, Bankole-Bright urged the students to forge a unity'; p. 145: secretary-general of WASU, 'a position he had held since the founding of the Union in 1925'
- [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md): Jomo Kenyatta, arriving in London in March 1929, went to Ladipo Solanke, whose West African Students' Union had promised the Kikuyu Central Association hospitality; Adi records their friendship. — p. 45 ('Solanke's friendship with Jomo Kenyatta'); pp. 50–51 n. 74 (Special Branch report of 19 June 1929); p. 77 ('Solanke's old friends Kenyatta')
