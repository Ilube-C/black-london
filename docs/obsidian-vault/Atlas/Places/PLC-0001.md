Westminster Town Hall stood in Caxton Street, Westminster. Today it is known as Caxton Hall. Historic England's official list entry describes it as a "Former town hall", "Built as town hall in 1878-82 by the architects Lee and Smith".[^1] For the history of Black London it is best known as the venue of the [First Pan-African Conference](Atlas/Events/EVT-0001.md). Its plenary sessions were held there on 23, 24 and 25 July 1900.[^2]

## The 1900 Conference

The conference was organised by [Henry Sylvester Williams](Atlas/People/PER-0003.md) and the [African Association](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0001.md).[^2][^3] Fryer writes that about 37 delegates took part, with about ten other participants and observers. Bishop Alexander Walters took the chair.[^2] Williams said afterwards that black people had never before "assailed London with a conference".[^2] Three figures in the atlas are documented in its programme:

- **[Henry Sylvester Williams](Atlas/People/PER-0003.md)** was its principal organiser.[^3]
- **[W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md)** spoke as a delegate. Adi and Sherwood record that he chaired the committee that drafted the appeal ["To the Nations of the World"](Atlas/Works/WRK-0001.md).[^4]
- **[Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md)** "took charge of the musical side of the programme at the Pan-African Conference held at Westminster Town Hall in July 1900", in Fryer's words.[^5]

The Address adopted there opened: "In the metropolis of the modern world, in this the closing year of the nineteenth century, there has been assembled a congress of men and women of African blood".[^6] It is the source of the phrase "the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line".[^6] The sources record these figures in one conference programme. They do not show that any two of them met privately in the building.

## Other gatherings

The hall was used for other political conferences. An announcement printed in the papers of the 1911 Universal Races Congress advertised the published addresses of "the International Conference held in Caxton Hall last June" on "Nationalities and Subject Races".[^7] Sherwood's study of Claudia Jones cites the published report of the Communist Party's second conference of Communist and Workers' parties "within the Sphere of British Imperialism", held at Caxton Hall in London in April 1954.[^8] Neither conference is linked in the sources consulted to a figure in the atlas.

## The building today

The building is listed at Grade II on the National Heritage List for England as "No. 10, Caxton Hall", Caxton Street, SW1. It was first listed on 15 March 1984, and the entry was last amended in 2018.[^1] The list entry gives it a 10-figure National Grid Reference, TQ2955579401.[^1] The atlas's latitude and longitude are converted from that reference.

## Evidence notes

- **Name.** Contemporary and secondary accounts of the 1900 Conference call the venue Westminster Town Hall.[^2][^3] Richards identifies it as the building "now Caxton Hall".[^9] The official list entry calls it a former town hall and does not use the name Westminster Town Hall.[^1] The identification therefore rests on Richards together with the list entry's description.
- **Rooms.** The sources consulted do not say which room or rooms the conference used.
