The Mangrove was a Caribbean restaurant at 8 All Saints Road, Notting Hill, run by the Trinidadian Frank Crichlow. Bunce and Field write that "in March 1968, he opened the Mangrove at 8 All Saints Road, Notting Hill, a much bigger and classier venture" than his earlier café, the Rio. Police harassment of the Rio had prompted the move.[^1] Darcus Howe recalled that the Mangrove was a fully fledged restaurant serving West Indian food that Crichlow had learned to cook from his mother.[^1] The Serpentine's 2021 guide to London's places of belonging describes it as "a meeting place for the Black community in the area, as well as for white radicals, artists, authors, and musicians". The guide adds that it "functioned as the unofficial headquarters for the Notting Hill Carnival".[^2]

## A community centre

A contemporary account, quoted by Bunce and Field from *IT* of September 1969, called the Mangrove "the central eating place for the black community in Notting Hill ... the one clean, late night social centre which also serves good food". The account added that "black families use it as a meeting place as well as a place of entertainment".[^1] *The Hustler*, a community newspaper launched in May 1968, was published on the premises.[^1]

## Police raids

The police raided the Mangrove repeatedly on the grounds that drugs were being sold there. Bunce and Field write that "drugs were never found". The first raid took place at about 11 p.m. on 24 January 1969. The police refused to let the manager examine their warrant, and the search found nothing.[^1] The National Archives records that the campaign that followed demanded an end to "the persecution of the Mangrove Restaurant".[^3]

## The march and the trial

On 9 August 1970 a demonstration set off from the Mangrove in protest at the raids. It was organised by the Action Committee for Defence of the Mangrove "in conjunction with the [Black Panther Movement](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0031.md)" (see [the Mangrove march](Atlas/Events/EVT-0024.md)).[^4] [Altheia Jones-LeCointe](Atlas/People/PER-0024.md), leader of the Black Panthers, spoke before the march set off. Clashes with the police on the route led to twenty-seven arrests.[^4] Nine people, including Crichlow, Jones-LeCointe and Howe, were tried at the Old Bailey in 1971 as the "Mangrove Nine" (see [the trial](Atlas/Events/EVT-0025.md)). All nine were acquitted of the principal charge of incitement to riot.[^5]

## Evidence notes

- **Opening date.** Bunce and Field give March 1968. The Serpentine guide gives 1968.[^1][^2]
- **Closing.** The sources consulted do not record when the restaurant closed.
- **People in the atlas.** Jones-LeCointe is the only atlas figure documented at the Mangrove in the sources consulted, through the march. Crichlow and Howe do not have entries of their own.
- **The building today.** No official record of 8 All Saints Road's present status was consulted for this edition.
