Chorlton-on-Medlock Town Hall, in central Manchester, was the meeting place of the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](Atlas/Events/EVT-0004.md) in October 1945. George Padmore's published report of the Congress records its sessions there between 15 and 19 October.[^1] Fryer writes that "Assembled in Chorlton Town Hall, in fact, was 'the future political leadership of British territories in Africa around 1960'", quoting the historian Imanuel Geiss.[^2]

## Why Manchester

The participant Ras Makonnen, quoted by Fryer, gave three reasons for meeting in Manchester. He had businesses and political contacts there. The city had become "quite a point of contact with the coloured proletariat in Britain". And there were "age-old connections between cotton, slavery and the building up of cities in England".[^2] A delegate conference in Manchester on 11–12 August 1945 reviewed the preparations.[^1]

## The Congress in the town hall

Padmore's report sets out a programme of sessions over five days. The first, on 15 October, was chaired by [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) and devoted to "The Colour Problem in Britain". On the same day the Lord Mayor of Manchester welcomed the delegates. [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md) chaired the sessions of 16, 17 and 18 October.[^1] Rapporteurs included [Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md) on "Imperialism in North and West Africa", [Jomo Kenyatta](Atlas/People/PER-0017.md) on "The East African Picture" and [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md) on the Caribbean.[^1] The University of Manchester describes how "200 delegates including future African national leaders Kwame Nkrumah … and Jomo Kenyatta … met in Chorlton-on-Medlock Town Hall".[^3] Fryer gives a lower count of about 90 delegates and 11 fraternal delegates and observers.[^2]

The Congress ended with two declarations, "The Challenge to the Colonial Powers" and the "Declaration to the Colonial Workers, Farmers and Intellectuals". The second closed: "Colonial and Subject Peoples of the World—Unite!"[^1] Fryer notes that the British press "all but ignored" the Congress.[^2]

## The building today

Historic England's National Heritage List names the listed structure "Former Town Hall Facade to Mabel Tylecote Building, Manchester Metropolitan University". The entry records that the original town hall was redeveloped except for the listed facade.[^4] Manchester City Council's register of listed buildings records it at Grade II, with the date 25.2.52, as "Manchester Polytechnic refectory. Formerly listed as Chorlton Town Hall".[^5] In October 2025 Manchester Metropolitan University described the former town hall as "now home to Manchester Met's Grosvenor East building", "where a plaque commemorating the fifth Pan African Congress is located".[^6]

## Evidence notes

- **Dates.** The sessions in Padmore's report run from 15 to 19 October 1945. Peter Abrahams, writing in the same report, gives "October 13th–21st".[^1]
- **Attendance.** Totals range from about 90 delegates (Fryer) to 200 (the University of Manchester and Manchester Metropolitan University).[^2][^3][^6]
- **Building names.** The official list entry and the university use different names for the modern building behind the facade, Mabel Tylecote Building and Grosvenor East.[^4][^6] The sources consulted do not explain the difference.
- **Position.** The National Grid Reference in the official list entry could not be read in the copy consulted, so no position is recorded.
