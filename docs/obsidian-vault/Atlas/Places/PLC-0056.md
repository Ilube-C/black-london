The [West African National Secretariat](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0024.md) (WANS) worked from a single room at 94 Gray's Inn Road, London WC1. [Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md), its general secretary, describes finding the room after the [Manchester Congress of 1945](Atlas/Events/EVT-0004.md). West African delegates had asked for "a centre in London from which they could effectively direct their programme", and "Before long I found one-a small room in 94 Gray's Inn Road which was at that time the office of a Gold Coast barrister, Koi Larbi."[^1] Sherwood identifies the barrister as K. O. Larbi, who had himself attended the Manchester Congress.[^2]

## "The busiest little place"

Nkrumah's autobiography gives the fullest description of the office:

> This little room became our headquarters and it must have been the busiest little place of its size in the whole of London. It was rarely empty for as soon as word went round of its whereabouts, it became the rendezvous of all African and West Indian students and their friends.[^1]

He adds that they were "invariably in the office until well after midnight", and that much of the winter was spent walking around London looking for coal.[^1] Sherwood treats this account with some caution. She calls it a "perhaps somewhat exaggerated description" of the Secretariat's poverty.[^3]

## The Secretariat's address

A letter from Nkrumah to Dr Rita Hinden of the Fabian Colonial Bureau, dated 25 March 1946, is reproduced by Sherwood. It is written on WANS letterhead giving the address "94, Grays Inn Road, London, W.C.1" and a Holborn telephone number. The letterhead names I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson as chairman, Bankole Awoonor-Renner as vice-chairman, Nkrumah as general secretary, and G. Ashie-Nikoi and Mrs O. Renner as joint treasurers.[^4] In the letter Nkrumah accepts an invitation to speak at the Fabian Colonial Conference of 12–14 April 1946.[^4]

## Work done from the office

From this base WANS organised its first public meeting, on 1 February 1946. The meeting called on the United Nations to "bring about the complete liquidation of the Colonial system".[^2] In 1946 WANS and the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) sponsored a West African conference on "Unity and Independence of all West Africa", held from 30 August to 1 September.[^5] Nkrumah was "chief editor" of the Secretariat's paper, [*The New African*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0018.md).[^6] He left London on 14 November 1947.[^7]

Nkrumah lived during these years at [60 Burghley Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0054.md), Kentish Town.[^7] He also worked with George Padmore at Padmore's flat in [Cranleigh Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0012.md).[^7]

## Evidence notes

- **Larbi's name.** Nkrumah writes "Koi Larbi"; Sherwood writes "K. O. Larbi".[^1][^2]
- **Dates of use.** The sources consulted show the address in use from early 1946, on the letter of 25 March 1946, to Nkrumah's departure in 1947. They do not say when WANS left the room.
- **The building today.** No official record of 94 Gray's Inn Road's present status was consulted for this edition.
