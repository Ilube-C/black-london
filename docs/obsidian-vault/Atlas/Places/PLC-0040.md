The Central YMCA in Tottenham Court Road is where the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) was founded. Fryer writes that the League was formed "at a meeting in the Central YMCA, Tottenham Court Road, London, on 13 March 1931".[^1] Fryer calls the League "the first effective black pressure group in this country".[^1]

## The founding meeting

The League's founder was [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md), a Jamaican-born doctor with a practice in Peckham. Fryer describes how, before 1931, Moody used his religious and philanthropic contacts to help Black people facing the colour bar in lodgings and work. In the end "the time was ripe to form an organization". He was encouraged by the visiting African American historian Dr Charles Wesley.[^1] Moody was president of the League from its founding until his death in 1947.[^1]

Fryer names Stella Thomas, later the first woman barrister and magistrate in West Africa, among the foundation members. The original executive included Dr Belfield Clark of Barbados, Sergeant George Roberts of Trinidad, Samson Morris of Grenada, Robert Adams of British Guiana and Desmond Buckle of the Gold Coast.[^1] [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md)'s biographer, Delia Jarrett-Macauley, also dates the founding to 13 March 1931. Marson was then lodging with the Moodys at [164 Queen's Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0039.md), Peckham, and Jarrett-Macauley describes how his lodgers were drawn into the new League.[^2]

The League's first four aims were later printed in every issue of its journal, [*The Keys*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0010.md). The first was "To promote and protect the Social, Educational, Economic and Political Interests of its members". The others were to interest members in "the Welfare of Coloured Peoples in all parts of the World", "To improve relations between the Races", and to co-operate with sympathetic organisations.[^1] See [the founding of the League](Atlas/Events/EVT-0017.md) for the meeting itself.

## What the League did afterwards

The League did not stay at the YMCA. Its meetings, Jarrett-Macauley writes, were held at the [Memorial Hall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0041.md) in Farringdon, which became one of Marson's "regular haunts".[^3] The League campaigned against the colour bar, published *The Keys* from 1933, and in 1938 pressed for the inquiry into conditions in the Caribbean that became the Moyne Commission.[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **The building.** Fryer gives the address only as "Central YMCA, Tottenham Court Road". The sources consulted do not describe the building in 1931 or say which room the meeting used.
- **Attendance.** No attendance figure or record of speeches at the founding meeting appears in the sources consulted.
- **Other YMCA meetings.** Sherwood records a meeting between the West African National Secretariat and the Conference of British Missionary Societies "held at the YMCA" in October 1947. She does not say which YMCA, so the meeting is not attributed to this building.[^4]
- **The building today.** No official record of the building's present status was consulted for this edition.
