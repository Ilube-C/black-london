[Jomo Kenyatta](Atlas/People/PER-0017.md) lodged at 95 Cambridge Street in Pimlico during two of his stays in London. English Heritage, whose blue plaque marks the house, writes that he stayed "for a short spell in 1930 and for a further four years in 1933–7".[^1] These were the years in which he represented the Kikuyu Central Association in London, joined the circle of Pan-Africanists around [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) and [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md), studied at the [London School of Economics](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0023.md) and wrote [*Facing Mount Kenya*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0020.md).[^1]

## First stay, 1930

Murray-Brown's biography traces Kenyatta's moves. Before leaving for Moscow he had moved from west London to 23 Cambridge Street. "On his return he moved further down Cambridge Street to Number 95" and wrote again to the Colonial Office asking to see the Secretary of State.[^2] Later, according to Murray-Brown, his supporter McGregor Ross received a letter "posted at Dover" saying that Kenyatta "had left 95 Cambridge Street". Further letters followed from Berlin and Hamburg.[^3]

## Second stay, 1933–7

"On his arrival back in London from Russia", Murray-Brown writes, "Kenyatta returned to his old digs at 95 Cambridge Street and because of his sick appearance prevailed upon his landlady, Mrs Hocken, to take him in again."[^4] Money was a constant problem. By August 1934 Kenyatta had paid Mrs Hocken nothing for twelve months. A fortnight later she wrote that she was "out of pocket more than £100 for Kenyatta's board and lodging". By October his debts had reached £120.[^5] Even so, Murray-Brown writes, the Hockens "continued to serve him meals and welcome him to their billiards table in the basement of their house".[^5] English Heritage adds that he sold the stamps from his Kenyan mail to buy "penny buns".[^1]

During these years Kenyatta worked as a linguistic informant at University College and SOAS, which Berman calls his main source of income after his return from Russia.[^6] He also studied under Bronisław Malinowski at the LSE from 1935.[^6] He was among the founders of the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md) in 1935 and worked with the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) in 1937.[^7]

## The house today

English Heritage erected a blue plaque in 2005. It reads: "JOMO KENYATTA c1894-1978 First President of the Republic of Kenya lived here 1933-1937".[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Dates.** The plaque gives 1933–1937. English Heritage's account and Murray-Brown also record the short first stay around 1930.[^1][^2]
- **Kenyatta's birth year.** The plaque gives "c1894"; the atlas's biography of Kenyatta records the range of dates given by different sources.
- **Position.** No official record giving a grid reference for the house was consulted, so no position is recorded.
