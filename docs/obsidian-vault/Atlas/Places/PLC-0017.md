*Plasterers' Hall in Addle Street, City of London, where Olaudah Equiano was living when he made his will in May 1796.*

Carretta writes that "Equiano was living in Plasterers' Hall when he drew up his will on 28 May 1796".[^1] The will itself mentions the lease of "Plasterers Hall situate in Addle Street", in the City of London.[^1] Walvin notes that Equiano's wife Susanna had been buried in February 1796, a few months before he made the will.[^2] He died in London on 31 March 1797 and was buried at [Whitefield's Tabernacle burial ground](Atlas/Places/PLC-0018.md) in Tottenham Court Road.[^1] The sources consulted do not explain how Equiano came to hold the lease or which part of the building he occupied.
