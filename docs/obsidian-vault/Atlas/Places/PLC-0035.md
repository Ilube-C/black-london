Africa House was the first hostel of the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) (WASU). It opened on Camden Road, in north London, in 1933. The National Archives describes it as "The first hostel for African students and visitors opened by WASU on Camden Road, NW1, in 1933", with 18 rooms as well as outdoor and common spaces.[^1] The January 1933 issue of WASU's journal, held by the National Archives, gives the Union's address as "W.A.S.U. Hostel, 62, Camden Road, London, N.W.1".[^1] Adi and Sherwood call it "the first such facility established by Africans in London".[^2]

## Why a hostel

The colour bar made it hard for West African students to find lodgings in London. The National Archives notes that the hostel "provided cheap accommodation, and a place for West Africans to gather". It even had its own restaurant.[^1] Adi and Sherwood write that [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md) wanted a hostel not only as a "home from home" but to prove that Africans could organise themselves on the basis of self-help. Between 1929 and 1932 he raised money for it on a tour of West Africa.[^2] Adi records that Solanke had collected over £1,300 by his return in 1932.[^3]

## Opening, 1933

WASU announced that it had found premises in Camden and appealed for furniture in Britain and West Africa. In the spring of 1933, Adi writes, *Wasu* reported that Solanke and Olu Obisanya, "the future Mrs Solanke", had been busy preparing the hostel. "The hostel officially opened on 9 March 1933".[^3] Solanke became its first warden.[^2] In January 1933, the National Archives notes, WASU had written to the Colonial Secretary, Philip Cunliffe-Lister, hoping for support as it launched "the African Student's Hostel in Camden Road".[^1]

## Rival to Aggrey House

The Colonial Office supported a rival club, Aggrey House in Doughty Street, opened with [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md)'s involvement. WASU and its allies boycotted it and formed the Africa House Defence Committee, which campaigned to keep "the present African hostel ... under the complete control of African students".[^4] While Aggrey House remained closed, Adi writes, "WASU's Africa House occupied a unique position in the capital".[^5]

## Visitors and gatherings

[Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md)'s biographer names "the West African students' Camden Road hostel" as one of Marson's regular haunts. Students gathered there "to rail against colonialism, lack of opportunity, the cold country and the cold people".[^6] Adi records that the Colonial Secretary, Lord Lloyd, "made history by visiting Africa House" in 1940, and that the Deputy Prime Minister, Clement Attlee, visited in 1941.[^7] The hostel's address at those dates is discussed in the evidence notes below.

## Evidence notes

- **Address.** The street number 62 comes from the address printed in the January 1933 issue of WASU's journal. The National Archives' own text gives only "Camden Road, NW1".[^1]
- **Opening date.** Adi gives the official opening as 9 March 1933. Fryer gives January 1933 for WASU's hostel (see [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md)).[^3] The journal of January 1933 already gives the hostel as the Union's address.[^1]
- **Moves.** By 1938 WASU had raised enough to buy freehold premises in Camden Square.[^8] The name Africa House was still used for WASU's Camden hostel in the 1950s.[^9] The sources consulted do not say when the hostel left Camden Road, or whether the visits of 1940 and 1941 took place there or at the Camden Square premises (see [WASU hostel, South Villas](Atlas/Places/PLC-0036.md)).
- **The building today.** No official record of 62 Camden Road's present status was consulted for this edition.

## Connected people
