*The West African Students' Union hostel at South Villas, Camden Square, bought freehold in 1938 and the subject of a long legal dispute.*

By 1938 the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md)'s hostel appeal had raised over £1,500. Adi writes that this made it "possible to purchase freehold premises in Camden Square".[^1] A dispute followed over the trust deed, which made WASU "merely tenants, at will, of the Trustees".[^1] Fryer describes the move to Camden Square as a move to new headquarters.[^2] Adi refers to "the hostel at South Villas in Camden" as one of the two hostels still fought over in the 1950s. Writing in 1998, he adds that "the legal dispute continues to this day".[^3] When [Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md) arrived in 1945, George Padmore took him to a WASU hostel, where a room had been reserved for him.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **Which hostel in 1945.** Nkrumah and Sherwood do not name the hostel to which he was taken.[^4]
- **Address.** The street number is not given in the sources consulted.
