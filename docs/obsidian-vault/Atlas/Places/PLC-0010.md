The Florence Mills Social Parlour was a nightclub and restaurant in Carnaby Street, in London's West End, run by [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) in the mid-1930s. It was named after the African American singer and actress Florence Mills. Høgsbjerg writes that it "quickly became 'a haunt of black intellectuals'".[^1] The *Women Who Meant Business* profile of Ashwood Garvey gives its address: "In 1936, Amy added a nightclub, the Florence Mills Social Parlour, at 50 Carnaby Street".[^2]

## Opening

Ashwood Garvey had earlier planned to take a company of artists of African descent to West Africa. When this plan fell through, Høgsbjerg writes, she and her partner, the Trinidadian musician and actor Sam Manning, first opened the International Afro Restaurant in 1935 at 62 New Oxford Street. Then in 1936, "with the Guyanese clarinetist Rudolph Dunbar", she opened a nightclub nearby: the Florence Mills Social Parlour.[^1] Adi and Sherwood write instead that she opened it "with Sam Manning", about a year after the West African plans failed.[^3]

## Who came

Adi and Sherwood write that the Parlour "became the haunt of the many Black peoples in London". They name "such political figures such as GEORGE PADMORE, C.L.R. JAMES and RAS T. MAKONNEN". Makonnen recalled that "you could get a lovely meal, dance and enjoy yourself".[^3] Høgsbjerg quotes [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md)'s memory that Ashwood Garvey was "a wonderful cook". He also quotes Jarrett-Macauley's picture of the scene: "In the evenings artists, activists, students drank and supped and kept their spirits high at Amy Ashwood Garvey's West End restaurant."[^1]

These were the months in which Ashwood Garvey, James and [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md) were organising together. She was a founder member and the treasurer of the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md), formed in 1935.[^3] In 1937 she was among the founders of the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md).[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **Partner in the venture.** Høgsbjerg names Rudolph Dunbar as Ashwood Garvey's partner in the nightclub. Adi and Sherwood name Sam Manning. Høgsbjerg names Manning as her partner in the earlier International Afro Restaurant.[^1][^3]
- **Restaurant or nightclub.** The quotations from Jarrett-Macauley and James, as given by Høgsbjerg, may refer to the restaurant in New Oxford Street, the Parlour, or both.[^1]
- **Shared haunt, not shared meetings.** The sources say that Padmore, James and Makonnen frequented the Parlour. They do not record particular meetings there.
- **Address.** The street number 50 comes from the *Women Who Meant Business* profile. Høgsbjerg and Adi and Sherwood give only Carnaby Street.[^2][^1][^3]
- **The building today.** No official record of 50 Carnaby Street's present status was consulted for this edition.
