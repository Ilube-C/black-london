*The meeting of West African students in London in August 1925 at which, after a heated debate, the West African Students' Union was founded, with J. B. Danquah as president and Ladipo Solanke as secretary-general.*

The [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) (WASU) was founded at a meeting of West African students in London in August 1925. Fryer dates it to 7 August.[^1][^2] Hakim Adi, the Union's historian, traces the meeting to a visit to London by Dr Herbert Bankole-Bright, a member of Sierra Leone's Legislative Council and of the National Congress of British West Africa. Bankole-Bright urged the students to "forge a unity" that would mirror the Congress's unity in West Africa.[^1]

## The debate

London already had four organisations in which West African students were active: the African Progress Union, the Union of Students of African Descent, the [Nigerian Progress Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0007.md) and the new Gold Coast Students' Association. Bankole-Bright argued that a single West African body, run on the lines of the Indian Students' Union, would serve them better.[^1] Adi records that his proposal "sparked off a heated debate". Defenders of the Union of Students of African Descent called [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md) and his supporters "segregationists" who put West African unity before Pan-African unity. They replied that they wished to be self-reliant and that the older bodies had achieved little.[^3] When the proposal was put to the vote, "it was unanimously supported by all those present".[^3]

## Who was there

Fryer describes the founders as twenty-one law students "who afterwards became famous in West Africa as judges, magistrates, barristers, and politicians".[^2] J. B. Danquah of the Gold Coast became WASU's first president. Solanke became secretary-general, a post he held for twenty-five years.[^3]

## What it decided

The new Union's eight aims included:

- providing a hostel for students of African descent;
- acting as a bureau of information and a research centre on Africa;
- presenting "a true picture of African life and philosophy" to the world;
- fostering "a spirit of national consciousness and racial pride";
- publishing a magazine, *Wasu*.[^3][^4]

The journal ([*Wasu*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0009.md)) appeared in March 1926.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **The date.** Adi gives the month, August 1925; Fryer gives the day, 7 August.
- **The venue.** The sources consulted do not name the building where the meeting was held.
