*The trial of the Mangrove Nine at the Old Bailey, from 5 October to mid-December 1971, at which all nine were acquitted of incitement to riot and the judge acknowledged "evidence of racial hatred on both sides".*

The trial of the "Mangrove Nine", arising from the [demonstration of 9 August 1970](Atlas/Events/EVT-0024.md), began in Court 1 of the [Old Bailey](Atlas/Places/PLC-0028.md) on 5 October 1971 before Judge Edward Clarke.[^1][^2] The defendants included [Altheia Jones-LeCointe](Atlas/People/PER-0024.md), leader of the [Black Panther Movement](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0031.md), along with Darcus Howe, Frank Crichlow, Barbara Beese and Rhodan Gordon.[^1]

## The defence

Jones-LeCointe and Howe "took the unusual step of defending themselves at the trial".[^3][^2] Bunce and Field describe a co-ordinated strategy in which the self-representing defendants and the barrister Ian Macdonald repeatedly challenged the judge and prosecution. They also record the defence's demand for an all-Black jury, which was supported by a community petition.[^1] Outside the court, the Black Panther Movement under Jones-LeCointe's leadership organised support for the Nine.[^1]

## Verdict

After eight and a quarter hours the jury reached its verdict. All nine were acquitted of the principal charge of incitement to riot, and five were acquitted of all charges.[^1] The Open University's account records that the defendants "were acquitted of 23 of the 32 charges".[^4] A Home Office report of December 1971 records that the trial ended on 15 December "when five were acquitted and four convicted". It names Jones-LeCointe among those convicted of causing an affray and of assault on police.[^5] The judge suspended the sentences of those convicted, so none of the Nine went to prison.[^1]

In summing up, Clarke said that the trial had "regrettably shown evidence of racial hatred on both sides".[^1] Bunce and Field call this "an official judicial acknowledgement of racial hatred within the Met", and record that the Metropolitan Police sought a retraction.[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Length of the trial.** Bunce and Field give twelve weeks, and The National Archives "a long 55-day trial". These figures may count sitting days and calendar weeks differently.[^1][^2]
- **Jones-LeCointe's sentence.** The length of her suspended sentence is recorded in her biography.

## Connected people

- **[Altheia Jones-LeCointe](Atlas/People/PER-0024.md)** — Altheia Jones-LeCointe was one of the Mangrove Nine tried at the Old Bailey in 1971, and conducted her own defence.[^6]
