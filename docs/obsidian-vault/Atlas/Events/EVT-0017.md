*The meeting at the Central YMCA, Tottenham Court Road, on 13 March 1931 at which Dr Harold Moody and his supporters founded the League of Coloured Peoples.*

The [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) was founded "at a meeting in the [Central YMCA](Atlas/Places/PLC-0040.md), Tottenham Court Road, London, on 13 March 1931", in Peter Fryer's words.[^1] Its founder and president until his death in 1947 was [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md), a Jamaican-born doctor in Peckham. Fryer records that he had spent years using his religious and philanthropic contacts to help Black people facing the colour bar in housing and work.[^1]

## How it came about

Fryer writes that the work grew until "at last the time was ripe to form an organization". Moody had help and encouragement from Dr Charles Wesley, an African American history professor then visiting Britain.[^1] [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md)'s biographer, Delia Jarrett-Macauley, also dates the founding to 13 March 1931. Marson was then lodging at the Moodys' house, and Jarrett-Macauley describes how his lodgers were drawn into the new League.[^2]

## Who took part

Fryer names among the foundation members Stella Thomas, later the first woman barrister and magistrate in West Africa. Members of the original executive included Dr Belfield Clark of Barbados, Sergeant George Roberts of Trinidad, Samson Morris of Grenada, Robert Adams of British Guiana and Desmond Buckle of the Gold Coast.[^1]

## What it decided

The League's first four aims, later printed in every issue of its journal [*The Keys*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0010.md), were:

- "To promote and protect the Social, Educational, Economic and Political Interests of its members";
- "To interest members in the Welfare of Coloured Peoples in all parts of the World";
- "To improve relations between the Races";
- "To co-operate and affiliate with organizations sympathetic to Coloured People".[^3]

## Evidence notes

- **What is not recorded.** The sources consulted give no attendance figure and no record of speeches at the founding meeting.
