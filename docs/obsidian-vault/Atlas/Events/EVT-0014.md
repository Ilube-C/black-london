In the September 1848 session of the [Central Criminal Court (Old Bailey)](Atlas/Places/PLC-0028.md), [William Cuffay](Atlas/People/PER-0009.md) was tried with William Lacey and Thomas Fay. They were indicted for that they "feloniously did compass, imagine, devise, and intend, to levy war against the Queen".[^1] The record reads: "CUFFEY— GUILTY. Aged 60." He was sentenced to be "Transported for Life".[^1] It was the last act of the [Orange Tree conspiracy](Atlas/Events/EVT-0013.md).

## The trial

Cuffay struck a defiant pose. As the jury was empanelled from shopkeepers and professional men, he demanded "a fair trial by a jury of my peers in accord with Magna Carta" and was refused.[^2] Chase notes that the trials of other conspirators had already established the plot's scope, so "the outcome was hardly in doubt".[^2] The evidence against the conspirators included that of the police informer Charles Filden.[^3]

## Cuffay's speech

When he was brought up for sentence, Cuffay protested that the trial had not been fair, but did not blame the jurors:

> It has not been a fair trial, and my request was not complied with to have a jury of my equals … but the jury as it is I have no fault to find with; I daresay they have acted conscientiously.[^2]

He spoke bitterly of the prejudice the press had stirred against him:

> I crave no pity. I ask no mercy … I know my cause is good, and I have a self-approving conscience that will bear me up against anything, and that would bear me up even to the scaffold … I feel no disgrace at being called a felon.[^2]

Chase quotes these words from the official *Reports of State Trials*. Fryer prints the speech in full as an appendix to *Staying Power*.[^2][^4]

## Aftermath

In their Newgate cell William Dowling, convicted just before him, drew Cuffay's portrait. Sales of the lithograph raised money for Cuffay's wife Mary.[^2] Cuffay was transported to Tasmania, where Mary joined him in 1853. He stayed after the political prisoners were pardoned in 1856, campaigned against the colony's master-and-servant law and died in the Brickfield Invalid Depot in 1870, aged 82.[^5]
