*The police raid of 16 August 1848 on the "Ulterior Committee" of Chartists and Irish Confederates meeting at the Orange Tree public house near Holborn, which ended a planned rising; William Cuffay, its secretary, was arrested two days later.*

By the summer of 1848 a group of London Chartists and Irish Confederates was planning a rising. It was to be signalled by co-ordinated arson attacks on key London buildings. Their "Ulterior Committee" met at the [Orange Tree public house](Atlas/Places/PLC-0027.md) near Holborn.[^1] [William Cuffay](Atlas/People/PER-0009.md) was not among the originators. He first attended the committee on 4 August and within eleven days had become its secretary.[^1]

## The conspiracy

Chase writes that the conspirators were encouraged by unrest in Ireland. Cuffay believed "we shall be able to master the police since the Soldiers were being drained away".[^1] He and his wife cast bullets from old printers' type. At the Chartist class he led in Soho, according to an informer's later evidence, he demonstrated a pike and explained how to make cartridges.[^1] Chase notes that police informers were monitoring the plot and may have pushed it on. One of them, Charles Filden, admitted that he himself had given Cuffay the idea of using ginger-beer bottles as bombs.[^1]

## The raid

Chase gives the end of the plot:

- On the evening of Wednesday 16 August 1848, eleven leading conspirators gathered at the Orange Tree to prepare for that night's muster.
- "They had been there scarcely an hour when police poured into the room and arrested them all."
- A second group was arrested at the Angel Tavern, south of the river.
- At Seven Dials, one of the four muster points, around 150 waiting men dispersed within minutes once word spread.[^2]

Cuffay was not taken at the Orange Tree. He was arrested at home with his wife on 18 August.[^3] Chase records his remark to the constables: "Oh that's quite sufficient as I am a Chartist I understand it."[^3]

## Outcome

Six "Orange Tree conspirators" were transported and fifteen others received sentences of eighteen months to two years.[^4] Cuffay was tried at the Old Bailey in September (see [Old Bailey trial of William Cuffay](Atlas/Events/EVT-0014.md)). At the trials the defence sought to discredit the informer Thomas Powell. Chase notes that Harney and others blamed Powell for the conspiracy.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **The informer's name.** Chase spells the informer's name "Charles Filden".[^1] The printed Proceedings of the Old Bailey trial give "Charles Tilden", a brass-chaser of Little Earl Street, Seven Dials, who said that Cuffay was his class-leader.[^5]
- **Cuffay at the Orange Tree.** Cuffay was not at the Orange Tree on 16 August. The informer George Davis, however, told the court that Cuffay was among the twenty to thirty men who met there on Monday 14 August.[^5]
