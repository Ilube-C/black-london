## At a glance
Una Maud Victoria Marson (1905–1965) was a Jamaican feminist poet, journalist and playwright who, during her second stay in England, worked as a broadcaster for the [BBC](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0020.md) World Service and started *[Caribbean Voices](Atlas/Works/WRK-0012.md)*.[^1] She was born on 6 February 1905 and died on 6 May 1965.[^2] Jarrett-Macauley calls her a BBC producer during the Second World War.[^2]

## Life and significance
Born in St Elizabeth, Jamaica, Marson had published two volumes of poetry, *Tropic Reveries* (1930) and *Heights and Depths* (1931), by 1932, when she left for England.[^1] She arrived at Plymouth on 9 July 1932 aboard the SS *Jamaica Settler*.[^2] She lodged with Dr [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md) and his family at [164 Queen's Road, Peckham](Atlas/Places/PLC-0039.md), until spring 1935, when she moved to 29 [Brunswick Square](Atlas/Places/PLC-0042.md), a road in Camberwell.[^2] Busby says she was secretary of the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) and edited its journal, *The [Keys](Atlas/Works/WRK-0010.md)*;[^1] Jarrett-Macauley says she became the League's unpaid assistant secretary in autumn 1933 and later editor of *The Keys*.[^2]

After a League performance on 23 November 1933 at the YWCA's Central Club in Great Russell Street, her play *[At What a Price](Atlas/Works/WRK-0011.md)*, which she directed, transferred for a three-night run from 15 January 1934 at the Scala Theatre in [Charlotte Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0044.md); Jarrett-Macauley calls it the first Black colonial production in the West End.[^2] She later became private secretary to Emperor Haile Selassie of Ethiopia during his exile in Britain, and was active in the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom and the International Alliance of Women.[^1] She began working for the Abyssinian (Ethiopian) Legation at [Princes Gate, Kensington](Atlas/Places/PLC-0045.md), in October 1935, just as Italy invaded Abyssinia, and her workload grew when the Emperor arrived in London in 1936.[^2]

Back in Jamaica from 1936, she did social work, helped establish the weekly *Public Opinion* and the journal *Jamaica Standard*, and staged several of her own plays. In 1938 she began a second stay in England, as a radio broadcaster for the BBC World Service.[^1] She began with freelance work, including the television programme *Picture Page*.[^2] Her biographer is explicit that she did not work at [Broadcasting House](Atlas/Places/PLC-0046.md): her office was in the old Bedford College in Regent's Park, the temporary home of the Empire Talks Department.[^3] She devised the programme [*Caribbean Voices*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0012.md), first broadcast on 11 March 1943.[^4] She went back to Jamaica after the war, where she remained active as a publisher, journalist and social worker until her death.[^1] She was appointed a full-time programme assistant on the BBC's Empire Service on 3 March 1941, and returned to Jamaica in April 1946.[^2]

## Connections

- **[Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md)** — Una Marson lodged with Harold Moody's family at 164 Queen's Road from 1932 to 1935 and worked for his League of Coloured Peoples as assistant secretary and editor of The Keys.[^5]
- **[164 Queen's Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0039.md)** — Una Marson lodged with Harold Moody's family at 164 Queen's Road, Peckham, from July 1932 until spring 1935.[^6]
- **[League of Coloured Peoples (LCP)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md)** — Una Marson worked for the League of Coloured Peoples during her first stay in London, as its unpaid assistant secretary from autumn 1933 and editor of The Keys.[^7]
- **[British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0020.md)** — Una Marson worked for the BBC during her second stay in England, as a full-time programme assistant on the Empire Service from March 1941 until 1946.[^8]

## Evidence notes
1. **Her London addresses:** Jarrett-Macauley gives 164 Queen's Road, Peckham, from 1932 until spring 1935, and then 29 Brunswick Square, a road in Camberwell.[^2] No other London address for her is confirmed in the sources examined.
2. **The Scala production:** Jarrett-Macauley places the run at the Scala Theatre in January 1934, after a League performance in November 1933.[^2]
3. **The Ethiopian Legation:** Jarrett-Macauley places the legation at Princes Gate, Kensington, but gives no house number.[^2]
