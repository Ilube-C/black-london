## At a glance
Jessica Huntley (23 February 1927 – 13 October 2013) was a Guyanese activist and publisher who co-founded [Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0030.md) in London in 1968;[^1] London Remembers gives her dates and dates the founding to 1969.[^2] Working from [141 Coldershaw Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0075.md) in West Ealing and the [Walter Rodney Bookshop](Atlas/Places/PLC-0076.md), she was a joint director of the [International Book Fair of Radical Black and Third World Books](Atlas/Events/EVT-0022.md).[^3][^4]

## Life and significance
Born in British Guiana, Huntley followed her husband to London in 1958: Eric had gone ahead in 1957, and her passage was fixed for 15 April 1958.[^3] The household and publisher had moved to 141 Coldershaw Road in West Ealing by 1972.[^4] In 1968, she co-founded Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications, publishing Walter Rodney's *The [Groundings with My Brothers](Atlas/Works/WRK-0026.md)* (1969).[^3] [^1]

In October 1975, the Huntleys opened a bookshop at 5a Chignell Place, West Ealing, renaming it the Walter Rodney Bookshop in 1980.[^4] The Huntley papers hold records of the [Black People's Day of Action](Atlas/Events/EVT-0023.md) of 2 March 1981, of the second International Fair of Radical Black and Third World Books, mentioned in an Eric Huntley notebook of 1983, and of Jessica Huntley's resignation as one of the fair's joint directors, recorded in minutes of 1983–6.[^3] Bunce and Field place her, with John La Rose and Darcus Howe, at the meeting at La Rose's home in Finsbury Park on 18 January 1981, the day of the New Cross fire, which had been called to discuss holding the book fair; the first fair was held for a week in April 1982 at [Islington Town Hall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0077.md).[^5]

## Connections

- **[Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0030.md)** — Jessica Huntley co-founded Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications with Eric Huntley in 1968 or 1969 and ran it with him.[^6]
- **[141 Coldershaw Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0075.md)** — Jessica Huntley's household and the business of Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications moved to 141 Coldershaw Road, West Ealing, in 1972.[^7]
- **[Walter Rodney Bookshop](Atlas/Places/PLC-0076.md)** — Jessica Huntley ran Bogle-L'Ouverture's bookshop at 5a Chignell Place, West Ealing, renamed the Walter Rodney Bookshop in 1980, from 1975 until 1991.[^8]
- **[International Book Fair of Radical Black and Third World Books (first fair)](Atlas/Events/EVT-0022.md)** — Jessica Huntley helped plan the first International Book Fair of Radical Black and Third World Books from January 1981 and became one of its joint directors.[^9]

## Evidence notes
1. **The founding of Bogle-L'Ouverture:** The British Library dates it to 1968;[^1] London Remembers gives 1969.[^2] Both years are given here.
2. **The Day of Action and the Book Fair:** The Huntley papers document both, and her joint directorship of the fair.[^3] Bunce and Field place her at the planning meeting for the fair and date the first fair to April 1982;[^5] they credit the organisation of the Day of Action to the New Cross Massacre Action Committee and the *Race Today* Collective. The sources examined do not describe her own part in organising the Day of Action.
