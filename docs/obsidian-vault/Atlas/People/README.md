Twenty-five people of African descent whose lives connect with London, from the eighteenth century to the late twentieth.

- [William Cuffay](Atlas/People/PER-0009.md) — William Cuffay (1788 – 1870) was a Black British tailor who became a leading London Chartist in the 1840s: Westminster's representative on the National Charter Association's metropolitan district council from 1841 and its treasurer from 1842.
- [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md) — William Edward Burghardt Du Bois (1868–1963) was an African American sociologist, historian, and Pan-African organiser.
- [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md) — Samuel Coleridge-Taylor (15 August 1875 – 1 September 1912) was a London-born composer and conductor of Sierra Leonean and English parentage who achieved international acclaim for Hiawatha's Wedding Feast.
- [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md) — Harold Arundel Moody (1882 – 24 April 1947) was a Jamaican physician who practised medicine in Peckham, south London, from 1913 (from 1922 at 164 Queen's Road), and who founded the League of Coloured Peoples (LCP) in 1931; Fryer dates its founding meeting to 13 March 1931.
- [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) — Amy Ashwood Garvey (1897 – 3 May 1969) was a Jamaican Pan-Africanist and feminist whose London clubs and networks served the anti-colonial movements of the 1930s and 1940s.
- [Paul Robeson](Atlas/People/PER-0019.md) — Paul Leroy Robeson (9 April 1898 – 23 January 1976) was an African American bass-baritone, actor, and anti-imperialist activist who made his home in London in the late 1920s and 1930s, returned to live in Britain in 1958, and began a long semi-retirement in the United States in 1963.
- [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) — Cyril Lionel Robert James (1901 – 30 May 1989) was a Trinidadian historian and Marxist theorist; he died in London.
- [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md) — Una Maud Victoria Marson (1905–1965) was a Jamaican feminist poet, journalist and playwright who, during her second stay in England, worked as a broadcaster for the BBC World Service and started Caribbean Voices.
- [Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md) — Kwame Nkrumah (21 September 1909 – 27 April 1972) was a Gold Coast revolutionary, Pan-African theorist, and Prime Minister of the Gold Coast from 1952 and the first Prime Minister of independent Ghana from 1957, then its President from 1960 until a military coup deposed him in 1966.
- [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md) — Claudia Jones (21 February 1915 – 24 December 1964) was a Trinidadian-born Communist theorist, Black feminist, and journalist who spent the final nine years of her life in political exile in London (1955–1964).
- [Oliver Tambo](Atlas/People/PER-0020.md) — Oliver Reginald Kaizana Tambo (27 October 1917 – 23 April 1993) was a South African anti-apartheid leader who led the African National Congress from exile, as acting president after Chief Albert Luthuli's death and later as its President.
- [Constance Cummings-John](Atlas/People/PER-0015.md) — Constance Agatha Cummings-John (1918 – 21 February 2000) was a Sierra Leonean educator, politician and pioneer of African women's rights who trained as a teacher in London and was involved there in the West African Students' Union, the League of Coloured Peoples and the International African Service Bureau.
- [Nelson Mandela](Atlas/People/PER-0002.md) — Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela (1918–2013) was a South African anti-apartheid revolutionary, African National Congress leader, and first democratically elected President of South Africa (1994–1999).
- [Beryl Gilroy](Atlas/People/PER-0021.md) — Beryl Agatha Gilroy (30 August 1924 – 4 April 2001) was a Guyanese novelist and educator who became Camden's first Black headteacher at Beckford Primary School in West Hampstead in 1969; the Guardian obituary calls her London's first Black head teacher.
- [Jessica Huntley](Atlas/People/PER-0023.md) — Jessica Huntley (23 February 1927 – 13 October 2013) was a Guyanese activist and publisher who co-founded Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications in London in 1968; London Remembers gives her dates and dates the founding to 1969.
- [Stuart Hall](Atlas/People/PER-0022.md) — Stuart McPhail Hall (3 February 1932 – 10 February 2014) was a Jamaican-born cultural theorist whose work reshaped modern understandings of race and diaspora identity.
- [Olive Morris](Atlas/People/PER-0025.md) — Olive Elaine Morris was a Black feminist and community activist who squatted at 121 Railton Road in Brixton, was a founder member of the Brixton Black Women's Group (1973 or 1974) and worked to launch the Organisation of Women of African and Asian Descent (1978).
- [Henry Sylvester Williams](Atlas/People/PER-0003.md) — Henry Sylvester Williams (born 1867 or 1869 – died on 26 March 1911 in Port of Spain) was a barrister, writer and local councillor in London; he published the journal The Pan-African (1901) and The British Negro: A Factor in the Empire (1902).
- [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md) — George Padmore (born Malcolm Ivan Meredith Nurse; 1902 or 1903 – died 23 September 1959) was a Trinidadian journalist and Pan-African organiser.
