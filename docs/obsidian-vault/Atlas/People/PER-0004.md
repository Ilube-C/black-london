## At a glance
Amy Ashwood Garvey (1897[^1] – 3 May 1969) was a Jamaican Pan-Africanist and feminist whose London clubs and networks served the anti-colonial movements of the 1930s and 1940s.[^2] She helped organise the [Nigerian Progress Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0007.md), was a member of the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md), and co-founded the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md).[^3]

## Life and significance
Adi and Sherwood give her birth as 1897, in Port Antonio, Jamaica.[^1] During her 1924 stay in London she helped organise the Nigerian Progress Union; Adi's study of West Africans in Britain says that the union was formed in July 1924 "largely due to Amy Ashwood Garvey's enthusiasm".[^3][^4]

Returning to London in the 1930s, Ashwood Garvey joined the International African Friends of Abyssinia in 1935 and then worked with [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md) and [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) to found the International African Service Bureau in 1937.[^3] In 1936, she also operated the Florence Mills Social Parlour at [50 Carnaby Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0010.md) in Soho, which became a regular meeting place for activists, artists and students.[^5][^3][^2]

In October 1945, Ashwood Garvey participated in the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](Atlas/Events/EVT-0004.md) in [Manchester](Atlas/Places/PLC-0014.md). Her exact role is historically disputed: Thomas C. Holt states she co-chaired the opening session with [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md), whereas George Padmore's 1947 proceedings state she chaired the 15 October session alone, and Du Bois chaired the 16 October session.[^6][^7] A heritage plaque at [1 Bassett Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0011.md) in Ladbroke Grove was unveiled on 5 November 2009.[^8] English Heritage names her, with Paul Robeson and Ben Davis, among the civil rights campaigners and Pan-Africanists whom [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md) counted as friends.[^9]

## Connections

- **[Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md)** — Ladipo Solanke and Amy Ashwood Garvey founded the Nigerian Progress Union in London in July 1924.[^10]
- **[C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md)** — C. L. R. James and Amy Ashwood Garvey founded the International African Friends of Abyssinia together in 1935 and were both officers of the International African Service Bureau.[^11]
- **[George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey and George Padmore worked together in London's Pan-African organisations of the 1930s, sitting together on the executive of the IAFA and among the leaders of the IASB.[^12]
- **[W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois and Amy Ashwood Garvey both took a leading part in the opening of the Fifth Pan-African Congress in Manchester in October 1945; the sources disagree about whether they chaired a session together.[^13]
- **[Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey and Claudia Jones were friends and colleagues in London; Jones was general secretary of Ashwood Garvey's Association for the Advancement of Coloured People, founded in 1958.[^14]
- **[Nigerian Progress Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0007.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey encouraged the founding of the Nigerian Progress Union in London in July 1924.[^15]
- **[International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey was a founder member and the honorary treasurer of the International African Friends of Abyssinia, formed in London in 1935.[^16]
- **[Florence Mills Social Parlour](Atlas/Places/PLC-0010.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey ran the Florence Mills Social Parlour, a nightclub and restaurant in Carnaby Street, from 1936.[^17]
- **[International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey was among the founders of the International African Service Bureau in 1937 and its vice-president.[^18]
- **[1 Bassett Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0011.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey, who lived at 1 Bassett Road, Ladbroke Grove, in the late 1950s, is commemorated by a plaque there, unveiled on 5 November 2009.[^19]

## Evidence notes
1. **Year of birth:** Adi and Sherwood give 1897.[^1] No source examined for this atlas gives a different year.
2. **Her part in the organisations:** Reddock's article supports her help in organising the Nigerian Progress Union and her membership of the International African Friends of Abyssinia, not a founding role in either.[^3]
3. **1 Bassett Road:** The Jamaica Information Service report records the unveiling of the plaque but not the years in which she lived at the address, so the house is listed as a place of commemoration only.[^8]
