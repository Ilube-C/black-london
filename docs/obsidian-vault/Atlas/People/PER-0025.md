## At a glance
Olive Elaine Morris was a Black feminist and community activist who squatted at [121 Railton Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0081.md) in Brixton, was a founder member of the [Brixton Black Women's Group](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0032.md) (1973 or 1974) and worked to launch the [Organisation of Women of African and Asian Descent](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0033.md) (1978).[^1][^2] She was born in Jamaica in 1952 and died on 12 July 1979.[^3]

## Life and significance
On [15 November 1969](Atlas/Events/EVT-0026.md), aged seventeen, Morris intervened in [Brixton](Atlas/Places/PLC-0006.md) when police arrested Clement Gomwalk, a diplomat with the Nigerian High Commission; her own caption on a photograph of herself says it was taken after the police had beaten her up. She received a suspended sentence.[^3] After the incident she joined the [Black Panther movement](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0031.md),[^2] whose centre had by then moved to [Shakespeare Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0079.md) in Brixton.[^4] The Lambeth Archives catalogue places her in the Youth Section of its Brixton core, with Linton Kwesi Johnson, Neil Kenlock and Clovis Reid, and Liz Obi describes the youth collective as based at 38 Shakespeare Road.[^3]

On 20 June 1971 Morris was one of three protesters, with Darcus Howe and Abdul Macintosh, arrested after a scuffle with police outside the [Old Bailey](Atlas/Places/PLC-0028.md); the trial of the "Old Bailey Three" there began at the end of October 1972. Bunce and Field, who record this, also say that she helped Howe find a squat on Shakespeare Road for *Race Today* when the journal moved to Brixton.[^5]

In 1972 Morris and Liz Turnbull (Liz Obi), both members of the Brixton Black Panthers, occupied a flat above a launderette at 121 Railton Road, Brixton, and fought off attempts at illegal eviction, including a police eviction in January 1973; a photograph of Morris climbing onto the roof became the cover of the 1979 *Squatters Handbook*.[^1][^3] The Lambeth Archives catalogue dates the opening of the squat to 1973 and says they resisted three illegal evictions; the building remained a squat until 1999, and Morris herself later squatted at 65 Railton Road and then at 2 Talma Road.[^3] After the Panthers declined she helped set up the Sabarr Bookshop (spelt 'Sarbarr' in *The Heart of the Race*), which that book calls the first Black self-help community bookshop in south London.[^2] The Lambeth Archives catalogue says 121 Railton Road went on to house Sabarr, which it calls the second Black bookshop to open in Brixton.[^3] She was one of the founder members of the Brixton Black Women's Group, formed in 1973 according to *The Heart of the Race*;[^2] the Lambeth Archives catalogue and Liz Obi give 1974.[^3] From 1975 she studied for a social science degree at Manchester University, graduating in 1978. *The Heart of the Race* dates her visit to China to 1978;[^2] the Lambeth catalogue and the booklet's introduction to her own account of the trip give 1977.[^3] She began in Manchester the work to launch OWAAD, which was formed after a meeting at Warwick University in February 1978; when she returned to Brixton that year, women in the Brixton group took the work up.[^2] *The Heart of the Race* says she then fell ill with leukaemia and died within the year;[^2] the Lambeth Archives catalogue and Liz Obi give Hodgkin's lymphoma. She died at St Thomas' Hospital, [London](Atlas/Places/PLC-0082.md), on 12 July 1979, and is buried in Streatham Vale Cemetery.[^3]

## Connections

- **[121 Railton Road Squat](Atlas/Places/PLC-0081.md)** — Olive Morris and Liz Turnbull (later Liz Obi) squatted a flat at 121 Railton Road, Brixton, from 1972 or 1973, resisting attempts at eviction.[^6]
- **[Brixton Black Women's Group](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0032.md)** — Olive Morris was a founder member of the Brixton Black Women's Group, formed in 1973 or 1974.[^7]

## Evidence notes
1. **Where the sources differ:** *The Heart of the Race*, libcom and the Lambeth Archives catalogue (reprinted in *Do You Remember Olive Morris?*) differ on the year the Railton Road squat opened, the year the Brixton Black Women's Group was formed, the year of her visit to China and the illness from which she died; both versions are given above.[^2][^1][^3]
2. **Date of birth:** The sources examined give 1952 but not the day.[^3]
3. **The 1969 incident:** Springer gives the date and places the arrest in Brixton but does not name the street.[^3]
