## At a glance
Constance Agatha Cummings-John (1918 – 21 February 2000) was a Sierra Leonean educator, politician and pioneer of African women's rights who trained as a teacher in London and was involved there in the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md), the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) and the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md).[^1][^2]

## Life and significance
Born in Freetown, Sierra Leone,[^1] Cummings-John was sent to London in 1935 to train as a teacher, a qualification she gained in a year.[^2] Sierra Leone Web says she went to England at seventeen and trained as a teacher at [Whitelands College](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0021.md) in Putney.[^3]

During her training she was involved in the major Black organisations in London: the West African Students' Union, led by [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md), and the League of Coloured Peoples, led by [Harold Moody](Atlas/People/PER-0012.md).[^2][^3]

After further study in the United States (at Cornell University, according to Adi and Sherwood), she became involved, back in London, with the International African Service Bureau.[^1][^2] Sources differ on its leadership: the *Guardian* obituary says the Bureau was founded by I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson, while Adi and Sherwood describe it as led by George Padmore and say she and Wallace-Johnson were fellow members.[^1][^2] The year is uncertain, since Adi and Sherwood date her departure for Cornell to September 1937 but her return to London to 1936.[^2] Sierra Leone Web dates a six-month course at Cornell to 1936, and her marriage to the lawyer Ethanan Cummings-John and their return to Sierra Leone to 1937.[^3] She returned to Freetown, was elected to its municipal council in 1938, and in 1966 became mayor of Freetown, the first African woman to be mayor of a major African city.[^1] After the 1967 military coup she settled in Tooting, south London; she went back to Sierra Leone in 1976 but later returned to London. She died in 2000, aged 82.[^1]

## Connections

- **[League of Coloured Peoples (LCP)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md)** — Constance Cummings-John took part in the League of Coloured Peoples while training as a teacher in London in 1935–36.[^5]
- **[International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md)** — Constance Cummings-John was a member of the International African Service Bureau in London in the 1930s.[^6]

## Evidence notes
1. **Dates in London:** Adi and Sherwood say that she was sent to London in 1935 and date her departure for Cornell to September 1937, but her return to London to 1936;[^2] Sierra Leone Web dates a six-month course at Cornell to 1936 and her marriage and return to Sierra Leone to 1937.[^3] Both accounts are given above.
2. **Her memoir:** Her own *Memoirs of a Krio Leader* (1995, with LaRay Denzer) could not be examined for this atlas. Details that rest on it alone, such as her day of birth and the committees on which she is said to have sat, are therefore not given.
