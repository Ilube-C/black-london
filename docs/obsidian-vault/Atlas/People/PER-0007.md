## At a glance
Ignatius Sancho (c. 1729 – 14 December 1780) was a British composer, epistolary writer and grocer whose shop was at [19 Charles Street, Westminster](Atlas/Places/PLC-0020.md).[^1][^2] As a male householder he voted in the [Westminster elections of 1774 and 1780](Atlas/Events/EVT-0009.md).[^2][^3] Carretta calls him the only eighteenth-century Afro-Briton known to have voted; the ECPPEC project has since found a Black Westminster voter, John London, in the by-election of 1749.[^2][^3]

## Life and significance
According to the biographical memoir prefixed to Sancho's [*Letters*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0004.md) (first published 1782), usually attributed to Joseph Jekyll, whom Project Gutenberg's edition credits as contributor,[^4] Ignatius Sancho was born in 1729 on board a ship in the slave trade, a few days after it left the coast of Guinea for the Spanish West Indies.[^1] After the second Duke of Montagu died in 1749, his widow took Sancho on as her butler; he later became valet to the new Duke of Montagu, whose residence in the Privy Gardens, [Whitehall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0019.md), took the name Montagu House.[^1][^2] During his years in service Sancho composed and published collections of [minuets, cotillons and country dances](Atlas/Works/WRK-0005.md), and his *Twelve Country Dances for the Year 1779*.[^2]

On 17 December 1758, at St Margaret's Church, [Westminster](Atlas/Places/PLC-0015.md), Sancho married Ann Osborne.[^2] When gout forced him to retire from the Montagu household in late 1773,[^1] the Duke helped him and Ann set up a grocery shop in early 1774 at 19 Charles Street, beside their new home at No. 20.[^2] J. T. Smith's recollection, which Carretta reprints, places the shop itself at No. 20.[^2]

His financial independence as a male householder in Westminster qualified him to vote.[^2] He voted in the Westminster elections of 1774 and 1780.[^2][^3] Sancho died at home in Charles Street on 14 December 1780 and was buried on 17 December in St Margaret's Chapel and Burying Ground, Westminster, the burial ground in the Broadway.[^2]

## Connections

- **[19 Charles Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0020.md)** — Ignatius Sancho kept a grocer's shop at 19 Charles Street, Westminster, from early 1774 until his death in 1780.[^5]
- **[Westminster Parliamentary Elections](Atlas/Events/EVT-0009.md)** — Ignatius Sancho voted in the Westminster parliamentary elections of 1774 and 1780.[^6]
- **[Montagu House, Whitehall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0019.md)** — Ignatius Sancho served the Montagu family as butler and valet; their Whitehall residence was Montagu House.[^7]

## Evidence notes
1. **The shop's number:** Carretta places the shop at 19 Charles Street, beside the family's home at No. 20; J. T. Smith's recollection, which Carretta reprints, places it at No. 20.[^2]
2. **The elections:** Carretta and the ECPPEC project confirm that he voted in 1774 and 1780;[^2][^3] the poll books themselves have not been examined, so the months of the elections are not given.
