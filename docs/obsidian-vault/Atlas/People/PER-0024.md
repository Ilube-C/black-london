## At a glance
Altheia Jones-LeCointe (Althea Lecointe in Wild's thesis) is a Trinidadian who led the British [Black Panther Movement](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0031.md) in London. Wild describes her as a Trinidadian postgraduate student when she became its leader after Obi Egbuna withdrew at the end of 1968, and as a doctor by 2008.[^1] The National Archives describes her as "a PhD student who had recently moved from Trinidad to London".[^2] After the [9 August 1970 demonstration](Atlas/Events/EVT-0024.md) defending the [Mangrove Restaurant](Atlas/Places/PLC-0080.md), she conducted her own defence in the 1971 Mangrove Nine trial at the [Old Bailey](Atlas/Places/PLC-0028.md), being acquitted of the most serious charges, of riot, but convicted of causing an affray and of assaulting police officers.[^3][^2][^4]

## Life and significance
After Egbuna's departure the movement's centre moved from Portobello Road in Notting Hill to [Shakespeare Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0079.md) in Brixton,[^1] where Liz Obi places the movement's youth collective at No. 38.[^5]

Police raided the Mangrove Restaurant at 8 All Saints Road[^6] twice in 1969 and ten times over six weeks in 1970.[^4] On 9 August 1970 Black Power activists led a march against police harassment of the Black community in Notting Hill and of the Mangrove. Bunce and Field say that it was organised by the Action Committee for Defence of the Mangrove together with the Black Panther Movement, and that Jones-LeCointe, "the driving force behind the British Black Panthers", spoke to the marchers before they set off.[^7] The National Archives says that the action group behind it was set up by anti-racist activists "like the leader of Britain's Black Panthers Altheia Jones-LeCointe", together with local community leaders; the following year nine people, Jones-LeCointe among them, were tried at the Old Bailey for causing a riot at the march.[^8][^2] During the fifty-five-day trial at the Old Bailey, which began on 5 October 1971 and ended on 15 December, she represented herself.[^8][^2][^4] She was acquitted of riot but convicted of causing an affray and of assaulting police officers, and was sentenced to fifteen months' imprisonment, suspended for two years.[^3][^4]

## Evidence notes
1. **Her studies:** The National Archives calls her a PhD student who had recently moved from Trinidad, and Wild a postgraduate student;[^2][^1] neither names her university or subject, and the sources examined do not give the year in which she came to London.
2. **The length of the trial:** The National Archives gives 55 days in one account and three months in another;[^8][^2] Bunce and Field, whose chapter on the trial is titled "55 Days at the Old Bailey", say that it began on 5 October 1971 and lasted twelve weeks.[^7] It ended on 15 December.[^4][^3]
3. **Shakespeare Road:** Wild places the movement's centre in Shakespeare Road without a house number;[^1] Obi gives No. 38 as the base of its youth collective.[^5]
