## At a glance
Harold Arundel Moody (1882 – 24 April 1947) was a Jamaican physician who practised medicine in Peckham, south London, from 1913 (from 1922 at [164 Queen's Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0039.md)), and who founded the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) (LCP) in 1931; Fryer dates its [founding meeting](Atlas/Events/EVT-0017.md) to 13 March 1931.[^1][^2][^3]

## Life and significance
The son of a prosperous druggist in Kingston, Jamaica, Harold Moody sailed to England in 1904 to enter London University as a medical student, and gained his degree in 1912 after winning a number of prizes.[^2] Fryer says he came to London in 1904 to study medicine at [King's College London](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0005.md), qualified in 1910 after winning many prizes, and was then refused a hospital house appointment.[^3] Having failed to obtain a hospital post because of racial discrimination, he decided to set up his own private practice.[^2] English Heritage likewise says that he was "denied a hospital appointment out of racial prejudice".[^1] He opened his general medical practice in Peckham in 1913, in King's Road (now King's Grove), moving to 164 Queen's Road in 1922.[^1]

After serving as vice-chair of the Council to Promote Understanding Between White and Coloured People, set up by a Friends Service Council conference on the colour bar in November 1929, Moody founded his own organisation, the League of Coloured Peoples (LCP), in 1931.[^2] Fryer dates its founding meeting to 13 March 1931, at the Central YMCA in [Tottenham Court Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0040.md), and records Moody presiding at a later protest meeting in the Memorial Hall, [Farringdon Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0041.md).[^3] By 1933 the LCP was issuing a quarterly journal, *The [Keys](Atlas/Works/WRK-0010.md)*.[^2] It appeared from summer 1933, and [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md), who lodged with the Moody family at 164 Queen's Road from 1932 to 1935, became its editor.[^4]

[Constance Cummings-John](Atlas/People/PER-0015.md) was involved in the LCP under his leadership while training in London, and the LCP took up the classification of colonial seamen in Cardiff as 'aliens', which deprived them of employment.[^2] In 1946 he and his wife left on a fundraising tour of the Caribbean and the United States; it was unsuccessful, and he returned to London ill and disappointed. He died on 24 April 1947,[^2] at 164 Queen's Road, Peckham, where he had lived and worked since 1922.[^1]

## Connections

- **[Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md)** — Una Marson lodged with Harold Moody's family at 164 Queen's Road from 1932 to 1935 and worked for his League of Coloured Peoples as assistant secretary and editor of The Keys.[^5]
- **[164 Queen's Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0039.md)** — Harold Moody lived and practised medicine at 164 Queen's Road, Peckham, from 1922 until his death in 1947.[^6]
- **[League of Coloured Peoples (LCP)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md)** — Harold Moody founded the League of Coloured Peoples in March 1931 and was its president until his death in 1947.[^7]

## Evidence notes
1. **His medical training:** Adi and Sherwood say that he entered London University as a medical student in 1904 and gained his degree in 1912;[^2] Fryer says that he came to study medicine at King's College London and qualified in 1910.[^3] Both dates are given here. The sources examined do not say where he did his clinical training; King's College Hospital stood in Portugal Street until it moved to Denmark Hill in 1913.[^8]
2. **Date of birth:** Adi and Sherwood and English Heritage give the year 1882 but not the day.[^1][^2]
