## At a glance
George Padmore (born Malcolm Ivan Meredith Nurse; 1902 or 1903 – died 23 September 1959) was a Trinidadian journalist and Pan-African organiser.[^1][^2] He launched the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) in London in 1937 and lived from 1941 to 1957 at [22 Cranleigh Street in Somers Town](Atlas/Places/PLC-0012.md).[^1][^3]

## Life and significance
George Padmore's birth year is the subject of historical discrepancy: the George Padmore Institute gives 1903,[^4] as does J. R. Hooker,[^5] while the *South Asian Britain* project cites 28 July 1902,[^2][^4] as do Adi and Sherwood, who give Tacarigua, Trinidad, as his birthplace.[^6] Padmore settled in London in 1935.[^2]

In 1937 Padmore worked with [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) and [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) to launch the International African Service Bureau in London.[^7] Between 1941 and 1957, he resided at 22 Cranleigh Street, where George and Dorothy hosted dinners for visitors. Selma James recalled: "Every anti-colonial activist organising against British imperialism came to 22 Cranleigh House, Cranleigh Street".[^1]

While living at 22 Cranleigh Street, Padmore helped organise the [Fifth Pan-African Congress](Atlas/Events/EVT-0004.md), held in Manchester in October 1945; Holt records that [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md) joined Padmore and Amy Jacques Garvey in sponsoring it.[^1][^8] In 1957, Padmore left London and later became an adviser on African affairs to Kwame Nkrumah in the newly independent Ghana.[^5]

## Connections

- **[Jomo Kenyatta](Atlas/People/PER-0017.md)** — George Padmore and Jomo Kenyatta worked together from 1930, first in Padmore's Comintern network in Germany and Moscow, then in London's Pan-African organisations of the 1930s.[^9]
- **[Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey and George Padmore worked together in London's Pan-African organisations of the 1930s, sitting together on the executive of the IAFA and among the leaders of the IASB.[^10]
- **[C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md)** — George Padmore and C. L. R. James, boyhood friends from Trinidad, worked together in London's Pan-African organisations, launching the International African Service Bureau in 1937.[^11]
- **[W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois and George Padmore worked together on the Fifth Pan-African Congress, held in Manchester in October 1945.[^12]
- **[Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md)** — Kwame Nkrumah and George Padmore were joint secretaries of the organising committee of the Fifth Pan-African Congress in 1945, and worked together afterwards.[^13]
- **[International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md)** — George Padmore was a founder and the chair of the International African Service Bureau, launched in London in 1937.[^14]
- **[22 Cranleigh Street (Cranleigh House)](Atlas/Places/PLC-0012.md)** — George Padmore lived at 22 Cranleigh Street, Somers Town, from 1941 to 1957.[^15]

## Evidence notes
1. **Year of birth:** The George Padmore Institute and J. R. Hooker give 1903;[^4][^5] *South Asian Britain* and Adi and Sherwood give 28 July 1902.[^2][^6] Both years are given here.
2. **The founding of the IASB:** Reddock's article describes Padmore, James and Ashwood Garvey working together to form the bureau in 1937.[^7]
