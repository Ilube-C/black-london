*Walter Rodney's talks on Black Power and African history given in Jamaica in 1968, published in London in 1969 as the first book of Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications.*

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Date | 1969 |

*The Groundings with My Brothers* collects talks by the Guyanese historian Walter Rodney. Rodney taught at the University of the West Indies in Jamaica from January 1968 and was soon asked to speak to groups off the campus, including Rastafarians. Some of those talks make up the book.[^1] Howe records that after the 1968 Congress of Black Writers in Montreal, the Jamaican government banned Rodney from returning to his post, and that on 18 October he gave the talk now known by the book's title.[^2] [Jessica Huntley](Atlas/People/PER-0023.md) and Eric Huntley published the collection in London in 1969. It was the first book of [Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0030.md).[^3][^4] Oates writes that work on it began in June and July 1969, with a first print run of 3,000 copies.[^5] Adi and Sherwood record that C. L. R. James called it "one of the best [books] that I know".[^1]
