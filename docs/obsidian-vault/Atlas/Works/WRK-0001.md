"To the Nations of the World" was the public address of the [First Pan-African Conference](Atlas/Events/EVT-0001.md), held at [Westminster Town Hall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0001.md) in July 1900. Fryer records that the conference adopted it unanimously, to be sent to the heads of states where people of African descent lived.[^1] Adi and Sherwood write that [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md) "chaired the committee charged with drafting its appeal 'To the Nations of the World'".[^2]

## The text

The address opens:

> In the metropolis of the modern world, in this the closing year of the nineteenth century, there has been assembled a congress of men and women of African blood, to deliberate solemnly upon the present situation and outlook of the darker races of mankind. The problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line …[^3]

It goes on to appeal to the leading powers by name. It asks Britain to give, "as soon as practicable, the rights of responsible government to the black colonies of Africa and the West Indies".[^3]

## Signatories and copies

The address was signed by Bishop Alexander Walters, the Revd Henry B. Brown, [Henry Sylvester Williams](Atlas/People/PER-0003.md) and Du Bois.[^4] The W. E. B. Du Bois Papers at the University of Massachusetts Amherst hold "Copy of reports from the 1900 conference in London; copy of an address, 'To the Nations of the World,' signed by Du Bois, Alexander Walters, Henry Brown and Sylvester Williams".[^4]

## Afterlife

Du Bois reused the phrase about "the problem of the colour-line" three years later in *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903).[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **Authorship.** The address was a collective document. Its attribution to Du Bois rests on his chairing the drafting committee and on his signature.[^2][^4]
- **The text consulted.** The quotations come from a two-page transcription with no publisher named. The signed copy in the Du Bois Papers was not consulted directly.

## Connected people

- **[W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md)** — W. E. B. Du Bois chaired the committee that drafted 'To the Nations of the World', the address of the 1900 Pan-African Conference, and signed it.[^5]
