*The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano, or Gustavus Vassa, the African. Written by Himself* was published in two volumes in London in 1789. The title page of the first volume states that it was "Printed for and sold by the author, no. 10, Union-Street, Middlesex Hospital". It was also sold by booksellers including "Mr. Johnson, St. Paul's Church-Yard" and "Mr. Murray, Fleet-Street".[^1] [Olaudah Equiano](Atlas/People/PER-0006.md) paid for the book himself, raising the money in advance from subscribers.[^2]

## Purpose

Equiano addressed the book to Parliament. His dedication, "TO THE LORDS SPIRITUAL AND TEMPORAL, AND THE COMMONS OF THE PARLIAMENT OF GREAT BRITAIN", explains:

> Permit me, with the greatest deference and respect, to lay at your feet the following genuine Narrative; the chief design of which is to excite in your august assemblies a sense of compassion for the miseries which the Slave-Trade has entailed on my unfortunate countrymen.[^3]

The dedication is dated "Union-Street, Mary-le-bone, March 24, 1789" (see [No. 10 Union Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0016.md)).[^3]

## Contents

The *Narrative* traces Equiano's life from an African childhood, through kidnap and the Middle Passage, enslavement at sea under Captain Pascal, a Royal Navy officer, his baptism at [St Margaret's, Westminster](Atlas/Places/PLC-0015.md) in 1759, the purchase of his freedom, and his conversion.[^3][^4][^5] In London, he writes, he went with "some of the Africans" in October 1785 to present an address of thanks to the Quakers. This was one of the earliest documented actions of the group later known as the [Sons of Africa](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0010.md).[^6]

## Evidence notes

- **Birthplace.** The *Narrative* gives an African birth. Carretta points to baptismal and naval records that give South Carolina.[^4] Adi and Sherwood summarise both positions.[^5]
- **Editions.** The text consulted for quotations is the Project Gutenberg edition, which does not reproduce the imprint. The imprint is quoted from the New York Public Library's record of the first volume of 1789.[^1][^3]
