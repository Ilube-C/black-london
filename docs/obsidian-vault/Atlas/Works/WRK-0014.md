*Toussaint Louverture: The Story of the Only Successful Slave Revolt in History* is a play by [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) about the leader of the Haitian Revolution. He wrote it in 1934.[^1] The Stage Society staged it at the [Westminster Theatre](Atlas/Places/PLC-0049.md) on 15 and 16 March 1936, with [Paul Robeson](Atlas/People/PER-0019.md) in the title role (see [the production](Atlas/Events/EVT-0018.md)).[^1][^2] Høgsbjerg writes that this was the first time for almost a century that a play explicitly about the Haitian Revolution had been performed on the British stage.[^1] He reads the play and James's history [*The Black Jacobins*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0015.md) (1938) together, as "companion pieces of historical literature".[^1]
