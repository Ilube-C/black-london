*Letters of the Late Ignatius Sancho, an African* was published in 1782, two years after the death of [Ignatius Sancho](Atlas/People/PER-0007.md).[^1] It was prefaced by a memoir of his life, usually attributed to Joseph Jekyll. Project Gutenberg's edition credits Jekyll as contributor.[^2] The memoir gives the main outline of Sancho's life: his birth in 1729 on a slave ship, his years as butler and valet to the Montagu family, and the grocery at [19 Charles Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0020.md).[^3] A third edition followed in 1784.[^3]

## The letters

The letters run up to Sancho's death in December 1780, and those of 1775–80 are headed "Charles Street".[^3] The best known is his first letter to the novelist Laurence Sterne, of 21 July 1766. In it Sancho quotes Sterne's sermon on slavery and adds:

> Of all my favorite authors, not one has drawn a tear in favour of my miserable black brethren—excepting yourself, and the humane author of Sir George Ellison.[^4]

Carretta's edition also prints Sterne's own revised version of Sancho's letter in an appendix.[^5]

## Politics

Sancho voted as a householder in the Westminster elections of 1774 and 1780 (see [the Westminster elections](Atlas/Events/EVT-0009.md)).[^1][^6] In a letter of 9 September 1780 he described the election in Westminster.[^7] Carretta calls him the only eighteenth-century Afro-Briton known to have voted. The ECPPEC project has since identified an earlier Black voter in Westminster, John London, in 1749.[^6]

## Where to read it

The 1784 third edition is available at the Internet Archive, and a transcription of it at Project Gutenberg.[^3][^2] The edition used for page references here is Vincent Carretta's (Penguin Classics, 1998).[^1]

## Evidence notes

- **The memoir's author.** The memoir is unsigned in the edition consulted. The attribution to Jekyll follows Project Gutenberg's catalogue.[^2][^3]
