*Wāsù* was the journal of the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) (WASU). The National Archives, which holds four copies, writes that it was "first published in March 1926". It explains that the name, "which also meant 'preach'", comes from Yoruba.[^1] WASU's aims, set out at its founding in 1925, included "to publish a monthly magazine".[^2] Adi quotes the first "Editorial Notes", of March 1926:

> As our name indicates, we have been brought forth to preach; and the first message we have to declare in general to all Negroes, and in particular to those of British West Africa is the saving one of co-operation.[^2]

The National Archives notes that it was released monthly and cost a shilling in 1933. It was distributed internationally, linking WASU's short-lived branches in Africa.[^1]

## A typical issue

The National Archives reproduces the cover and contents of the issue of January 1933 (Vol. II, No. 1), subtitled "The Journal of the West African Student's Union of Great Britain".[^1] Its contents included an editorial and the president's letter, and an item on the "W.A.S.U. Hostel". There were orations by the Revd M. S. Cole and the Revd I. O. Ransome Kuti, a pen-portrait of Herbert Macaulay, and news of branches. [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md) contributed "Yoruba constitutional law". The back of the contents page states: "Copies may be obtained from the Secretary-General, W.A.S.U., W.A.S.U. Hostel, 62, Camden Road, London, N.W.1".[^1] That issue was sent to the Colonial Secretary, Philip Cunliffe-Lister, with a letter asking for support for the hostel at [Camden Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0035.md).[^1]

## Purpose

The first issue also promised to explain West African concerns to the world. "About no other people or country", it declared, "has there been so much misconception as about the African and his land."[^2] Adi and Sherwood write that many of Solanke's essays appeared in the journal.[^3]

## Evidence notes

- **Run.** The sources consulted do not give the date of the last issue.
- **Spelling.** The National Archives prints the title with accents, *Wāsù*; other sources write *Wasu*.[^1][^2]
