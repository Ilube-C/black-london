*The monthly newspaper founded and edited by Claudia Jones in London in 1958, later titled West Indian Gazette and Afro-Asian-Caribbean News; it campaigned against racism, sponsored the first Caribbean Carnival and closed after her death.*

The *West Indian Gazette* was founded in London in 1958 by [Claudia Jones](Atlas/People/PER-0018.md), who published and edited it. Adi and Sherwood describe it as "A monthly paper that covered British and world political, economic and cultural news". They add that it "was also a campaigning tool".[^1] Its full title became the *West Indian Gazette and Afro-Asian-Caribbean News*.[^1] The Serpentine's guide to London's places of belonging calls it "widely considered to have been Britain's first major black newspaper", operating between 1958 and 1965.[^2]

## Making the paper

The paper was owned by the Coloured People's Publishing Company, which Jones set up. Sam King, its first circulation manager, recalled: "Our first print run was somewhere in the order of ten thousand. I took one thousand and distributed them all over the country."[^3] For its first five years the paper was produced in "two crowded, chaotic and untidy rooms" above a record shop at [250 Brixton Road](Atlas/Places/PLC-0058.md).[^4] Donald Hinds, who worked on it, wrote in 1965 that it had more than once "topped its printing order of thirty thousand".[^5]

## Campaigns

Adi and Sherwood write that it "contained articles written by and about Black leaders around the world" and brought news of campaigns and protests.[^1] In 1964 Jones described it to American readers as a paper that had "campaigned vigorously on issues facing West Indians and other coloured people", against police frame-ups and discrimination, and for "unity of coloured and white workers".[^6] Hinds recalled that a deputation went from its office to the Home Office to protest about the treatment of Black people, "especially in the Notting Hill district".[^5] The *Gazette* sponsored the first [Caribbean Carnival](Atlas/Events/EVT-0019.md) at [St Pancras Town Hall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0059.md) in January 1959, and the carnivals that followed.[^7]

## Evidence notes

- **Run.** The Serpentine guide dates the paper to 1958–65.[^2] The sources consulted do not give the date or number of the last issue.
- **Copies.** No issue of the *Gazette* was consulted directly for this edition.
