*Union of Nigerian students in London founded in July 1924 by Ladipo Solanke and others with Amy Ashwood Garvey's encouragement, to promote Nigerian welfare and mass education; a forerunner of the West African Students' Union.*

The Nigerian Progress Union (NPU) was founded "by thirteen Nigerian students in London in July 1924". Its stated purpose was "to promote the general welfare of Nigerians from an educational not political point of view".[^1] Its leading figure was the law student [Ladipo Solanke](Atlas/People/PER-0011.md). The historian Hakim Adi writes that the Union was formed "largely due to [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md)'s enthusiasm".[^1]

## Founding

Adi traces the NPU's origins to Solanke's protests in 1924 against the British Empire Exhibition at Wembley and against the "every day ridicule" of Black people in the press. Amy Ashwood Garvey, the estranged wife of Marcus Garvey, read his protest letters in the *Evening News* and *West Africa* and wrote to congratulate him. The two "became friends" and went on to form the NPU.[^1] Adi and Sherwood also record that Solanke and Ashwood Garvey founded the Union together.[^2]

## Aims and ideas

The NPU's motto was *Sol Omnibus Lucet*, "The sun shines for all". Its aims included:

- securing a hostel in London;
- fostering "the habit of self-control, self-sacrifice, self-knowledge and self-help";
- promoting African literature, customs and institutions;
- educating "the masses" in Nigeria through "national schools";
- sending Nigerian students abroad for professional training.[^3]

Adi writes that its philosophy "owed as much to the ideas of Marcus Garvey as it did to those of his estranged wife". It also reflected the programme of West African nationalist bodies such as the National Congress of British West Africa.[^1] The Union gave education priority over politics. Solanke wrote that "political agitation should be left to only a few who love and understand it".[^4] Ashwood Garvey shared this emphasis: "The Negro as a race is not yet ripe for political emancipation."[^5]

## Publications and campaigns

Through an introduction by Ashwood Garvey, Solanke's "Open Letter to the Negroes of the World" and other articles on the NPU appeared in the African American journal *The Spokesman* in 1925.[^5] The letter called for a National Education Fund and for West African "Tuskegees", and appealed to the "brethren in America" for co-operation.[^5][^6] The Union's patrons included Dr C. Adeniyi-Jones, J. H. Doherty, Dr Henry Carr and Chief Richard Henshaw.[^4] Officials at University College London warned the Colonial Office that the Union's activities were "a concealed form of political propaganda".[^4]

## Members, meetings and places

The NPU claimed thirty members, with branches in Britain and Nigeria.[^7] A photograph of 1925 shows its members with Amy Ashwood Garvey seated in the second row.[^8] The sources consulted do not record how often it met or where.

## Relationships with other bodies and fate

The NPU was one of four organisations in which West African students were active in 1925. That year a meeting called at the urging of Herbert Bankole-Bright led to the formation of the [West African Students' Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) (see [Founding of WASU](Atlas/Events/EVT-0016.md)).[^9] Adi notes that "even after the founding of WASU, the NPU continued to function". The sources consulted do not record when it ceased.[^10]

## Connected people

- **[Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md)** — Amy Ashwood Garvey encouraged the founding of the Nigerian Progress Union in London in July 1924.[^11]
