*The International African Friends of Abyssinia, formed in London in 1935 by C. L. R. James, Amy Ashwood Garvey and others to oppose Italy's threatened invasion of Ethiopia; it held rallies at the Memorial Hall and Trafalgar Square and disbanded in 1936.*

<!-- MEDIA: image slot — photograph of C. L. R. James speaking in Trafalgar Square, mid-1930s (reproduced in Høgsbjerg); to be selected and added by the editor -->

The International African Friends of Abyssinia (IAFA) was formed in London in the summer of 1935, as Mussolini prepared to invade Ethiopia (then called Abyssinia).[^1][^2] Its purpose was to rally British sympathy for Ethiopia, one of the last African states free of European rule. Fryer quotes its aim of assisting "in the maintenance of the Territorial integrity and political independence of Abyssinia".[^2] The historian Christian Høgsbjerg argues that it revived the Pan-Africanist movement in Britain.[^3]

## Founding

[C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) had met [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) through the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md). James recalled that both "felt that there ought to be an opposition" to Mussolini's war.[^1] Together they revived an ad hoc committee formed in 1934 to support a delegation from the Gold Coast Aborigines' Rights Protection Society. That committee became the IAFA, with James as chair.[^1][^2]

## Officers

Padmore later listed the officers:[^4]

- C. L. R. James, chairman;
- Dr Peter Milliard and T. Albert Marryshow, vice-chairmen;
- [Jomo Kenyatta](Atlas/People/PER-0017.md), honorary secretary;
- Amy Ashwood Garvey, honorary treasurer;
- Sam Manning, Mohammed Said and [George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md), with the officers above, on the executive.

Its sponsors included the Gold Coast delegates George A. Moore and Samuel R. Wood and Dr J. B. Danquah.[^1]

## Meetings and campaigns

The IAFA's first public meeting, held in London on 23 July 1935, was "crowded" with "men and women of African descent", according to *West Africa*. It sent resolutions of sympathy to the Ethiopians and opened a fund for an ambulance or a hospital.[^4]

On Sunday 28 July 1935 a second meeting at the [Memorial Hall, Farringdon Street](Atlas/Places/PLC-0041.md), presided over by Amy Ashwood Garvey, heard James survey Ethiopia's treaties with foreign powers.[^5] Kingsley Martin of the *New Statesman* counted "a couple of hundred coloured people, and perhaps fifty white". When speakers hoped for justice from the League of Nations or the British government, Martin wrote, "the audience was silent or ironical".[^6]

On Sunday 25 August 1935 the IAFA held a mass meeting in Trafalgar Square. The *Daily Herald* reported "ringing cheers" for Ashwood Garvey's declaration that "the hour has struck for our complete emancipation". James and the seamen's organiser Chris Braithwaite also spoke, and Ras Makonnen, who was passing through London, joined them on the plinth.[^6][^7] Høgsbjerg adds that the IAFA could call on Padmore and Kenyatta as speakers.[^3] By August 1935 Ashwood Garvey had offered her residence at 62 New Oxford Street, where she ran her International Afro Restaurant, as the IAFA's headquarters.[^8] When Haile Selassie came to London in 1936, the IAFA organised a reception for him at Waterloo station.[^2]

## Debates

The IAFA's main recorded disagreement was over the League of Nations. At its launch, James recalled, it passed a resolution demanding League action against Italy, which he later called "a blunder". Under his lead it then voted to reject "any appeal to the League of Nations".[^9] James wrote to the *New Statesman* that some members believed in "the international socialist order" and others supported Ethiopia "because God said so in the Bible", but that "we are in this struggle as one". The IAFA also seriously considered raising volunteers to fight for Ethiopia.[^6][^8]

## Fate and successors

After the main fighting ended, the IAFA disbanded. In April 1936 others formed a broader Abyssinian Association to continue the campaign.[^10] Its leading members went on to found the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) in 1937.[^10][^2]

## Evidence notes

- **Kenyatta's title.** Padmore's list calls Kenyatta "honorary secretary"; Fryer calls him its secretary.
- **The 1934 committee.** That an ad hoc committee of 1934 was revived as the IAFA is stated by both Høgsbjerg and Fryer.
