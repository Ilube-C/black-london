The societies, unions, committees, colleges and publishers through which the people in the atlas organised.

- [African Association](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0001.md) (Full entry) — Black-led association founded in London in 1897 or 1898 by Henry Sylvester Williams to lobby for Africans and people of African descent in the British Empire; it called the 1900 Pan-African Conference and merged into the Pan-African Association.
- [African National Congress](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0002.md) (Full entry) — The South African liberation movement founded in 1912, banned in South Africa from 1960 to 1990; its external mission under Oliver Tambo made London one of its bases, with a headquarters at 28 Penton Street, Islington, that South African agents bombed in 1982.
- [Anti-Slavery Society](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0013.md) (Full entry) — The London society founded in 1823 to campaign for the abolition of colonial slavery, known as the Anti-Slavery Society; Thomas Pringle was its secretary from 1827, and Mary Prince sought its help at its office at 18 Aldermanbury in 1828.
- [Bogle-L'Ouverture Publications](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0030.md) (Full entry) — The Ealing-based Black publishing house founded by Jessica and Eric Huntley in 1968 or 1969, named after Paul Bogle and Toussaint L'Ouverture; it first published Walter Rodney's The Groundings with My Brothers, ran the Walter Rodney Bookshop and co-founded the International Book Fair of Radical Black and Third World Books.
- [British Black Panther Movement](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0031.md) (Full entry) — The British Black Panther Movement, founded in Notting Hill in 1968 by Obi Egbuna and led from the end of that year by Altheia Jones-LeCointe from Brixton; it took part in the Mangrove campaign and in 1973 renamed itself the Black Workers Movement.
- [British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0020.md) (Short entry) — The national broadcaster for which Una Marson worked during the Second World War, as a programme assistant on the Empire Service from 1941 and as the founder of Caribbean Voices.
- [Brixton Black Women's Group](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0032.md) (Full entry) — An autonomous Black women's group formed in Brixton in 1973 or 1974, largely by women from the Black Power organisations; Olive Morris was a founder member, and the group published Speak Out, helped set up OWAAD and initiated the Brixton Defence Campaign after 1981.
- [Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations (CAACO)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0025.md) (Full entry) — The Committee of Afro-Asian Caribbean Organisations, a London coalition initiated by Claudia Jones and the West Indian Gazette in the early 1960s, which campaigned against racial discrimination and the Commonwealth Immigrants Act and in solidarity with the US civil rights movement.
- [Honourable Society of Gray's Inn](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0004.md) (Short entry) — One of the Inns of Court in Holborn, where Henry Sylvester Williams prepared for the Bar from 1897 and Kwame Nkrumah enrolled in 1945.
- [Iniva & Autograph ABP](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0029.md) (Short entry) — The Institute of International Visual Arts and Autograph ABP (the Association of Black Photographers), the two arts organisations, both chaired by Stuart Hall, that commissioned Rivington Place in Shoreditch.
- [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md) (Full entry) — The International African Friends of Abyssinia, formed in London in 1935 by C. L. R. James, Amy Ashwood Garvey and others to oppose Italy's threatened invasion of Ethiopia; it held rallies at the Memorial Hall and Trafalgar Square and disbanded in 1936.
- [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) (Full entry) — The International African Service Bureau, launched in London in 1937 by George Padmore, C. L. R. James, I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson, Ras Makonnen and others to inform British opinion about the colonies and press for African self-determination; it joined the Pan-African Federation in 1944.
- [King's College London](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0005.md) (Short entry) — The London college where Henry Sylvester Williams studied from 1896 and where, according to Fryer, Harold Moody studied medicine from 1904.
- [League of Coloured Peoples (LCP)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md) (Full entry) — The League of Coloured Peoples, founded in London on 13 March 1931 by Dr Harold Moody, who led it until his death in 1947; a welfare and pressure group that published The Keys, campaigned against the colour bar and drafted a "Charter for Coloured Peoples" in 1944.
- [London School of Economics (LSE)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0023.md) (Short entry) — The London college where Jomo Kenyatta studied social anthropology under Bronisław Malinowski from 1935, and where Kwame Nkrumah attended lectures in 1945.
- [National Charter Association](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0014.md) (Full entry) — The membership organisation of the Chartist movement, founded at Manchester in July 1840 and wound up in 1860; William Cuffay represented Westminster on its London district council from 1841 and was the council's treasurer from 1842.
- [Nigerian Progress Union](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0007.md) (Full entry) — Union of Nigerian students in London founded in July 1924 by Ladipo Solanke and others with Amy Ashwood Garvey's encouragement, to promote Nigerian welfare and mass education; a forerunner of the West African Students' Union.
- [Organisation of Women of African and Asian Descent](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0033.md) (Full entry) — The Organisation of Women of African and Asian Descent (OWAAD), a national network of Black women's groups formed in February 1978 after a meeting at Warwick University; it held national conferences from 1979, published FOWAAD, and broke up in 1982 or 1983.
- [Royal College of Music](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0015.md) (Short entry) — The London conservatoire where Samuel Coleridge-Taylor studied from 1890 to 1897, and where Hiawatha's Wedding Feast was first performed in 1898.
- [School of Oriental Studies (SOAS)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0026.md) (Short entry) — The London school where Paul Robeson enrolled to study Swahili and phonetics in 1934, and where Jomo Kenyatta worked as a language informant.
- [Sons of Africa](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0010.md) (Full entry) — The name under which a group of Black abolitionists in London, among them Olaudah Equiano and Ottobah Cugoano, signed letters and addresses against the slave trade in the later 1780s.
- [St Marylebone Borough Council](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0006.md) (Short entry) — The London borough council to which Henry Sylvester Williams was elected in November 1906 as a Progressive for the Church Street ward.
- [University College London (UCL)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0016.md) (Short entry) — The London college where Ladipo Solanke studied law from 1923 to 1927, where Kwame Nkrumah registered to read philosophy, and where Jomo Kenyatta worked as a language informant.
- [West African National Secretariat (WANS)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0024.md) (Full entry) — The West African National Secretariat, formed in London in late 1945 after the Manchester Congress, with Kwame Nkrumah as general secretary, to work for "a free, united, independent and democratic West Africa"; it collapsed soon after Nkrumah left for the Gold Coast in 1947.
- [West African Students' Union (WASU)](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0018.md) (Full entry) — The West African Students' Union, founded in London in August 1925 with Ladipo Solanke as secretary-general; it ran the Africa House hostel, published the journal Wasu, lobbied the Colonial Office and became a training ground for West African nationalist leaders.
- [Whitelands College](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0021.md) (Short entry) — The teacher-training college in Putney where Constance Cummings-John trained as a teacher in the mid-1930s.
