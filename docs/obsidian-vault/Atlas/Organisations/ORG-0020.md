*The national broadcaster for which Una Marson worked during the Second World War, as a programme assistant on the Empire Service from 1941 and as the founder of Caribbean Voices.*

The British Broadcasting Corporation employed [Una Marson](Atlas/People/PER-0013.md) during her second stay in England. Jarrett-Macauley records that "On 3 March 1941 ... Una was appointed full-time programme assistant on the Empire Service". She stayed until she was taken back to Jamaica in April 1946.[^1] Busby describes her as "a radio broadcaster with the BBC World Service" from 1938, and credits her with starting the programme [*Caribbean Voices*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0012.md).[^2] Her first BBC work, in 1939, was freelance work for the television magazine *Picture Page* at Alexandra Palace.[^3] Jarrett-Macauley notes that, although [Broadcasting House](Atlas/Places/PLC-0046.md) in Portland Place was the BBC's headquarters, "Una did not work there". As a programme assistant she had a small office in the old Bedford College in Regent's Park, then the temporary home of the Empire Talks Department.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **Broadcasting House.** The first edition of this atlas stated that Marson worked at Broadcasting House. Jarrett-Macauley states that she did not, and this edition follows her.[^4]
- **Start of her BBC work.** Busby dates Marson's broadcasting from her return to England in 1938. Jarrett-Macauley dates her full-time appointment to March 1941.[^1][^2]
