*Mary Prince lived with Thomas and Susan Pringle in Solly Terrace, Pentonville, as their domestic servant from 1829.*

After leaving the Woods, [Mary Prince](Atlas/People/PER-0008.md) entered the service of Thomas Pringle, secretary of the [Anti-Slavery Society](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0013.md), and his wife.[^1] Vigne writes that she "in 1829 moved in with the Pringles in Pentonville as their domestic servant". He places their home in [Solly Terrace](Atlas/Places/PLC-0024.md), where they lived until March 1833.[^2] In this household Pringle's lodger Susanna Strickland took down her life story, published in 1831 as [*The History of Mary Prince*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0006.md).[^2]

## Evidence notes

- **Address.** The street name comes from Vigne. The *History* itself does not give the Pringles' address.[^2][^1]
