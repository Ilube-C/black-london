[Constance Cummings-John](Atlas/People/PER-0015.md) became involved in the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) while in London. Adi and Sherwood write that on her return to London she "became involved in the much more radical Black organisation, the International African Service Bureau (IASB), led by GEORGE PADMORE".[^1] They add that she and I. T. A. Wallace-Johnson "had been fellow members of the IASB in London".[^2] Her *Guardian* obituary links her involvement to "the vicious racism she encountered" in the United States.[^3]

## Evidence notes

- **Date.** Adi and Sherwood place her involvement after her return to London in 1936. Høgsbjerg dates the Bureau's launch to May 1937 and Fryer to March 1937.[^1][^4][^5]
