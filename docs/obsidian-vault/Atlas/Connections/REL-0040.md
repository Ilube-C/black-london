In *Darkwater* (1920), [W. E. B. Du Bois](Atlas/People/PER-0001.md) wrote: "It is now nineteen years since I first saw Coleridge-Taylor." He describes a London hall full of people drawn chiefly to the World's Fair at Paris, "and then a few slipping over to London to meet Pan-Africa".[^1] He continues: "Several times we met in public gatherings and then they bade me to their home".[^1] This is Du Bois's own account of meeting [Samuel Coleridge-Taylor](Atlas/People/PER-0010.md) in person.

## Significance

The admiration ran both ways. Du Bois wrote of him at length in the chapter of *Darkwater* titled "The Immortal Child".[^2] In January 1904 Coleridge-Taylor thanked the man who had sent him Du Bois's *The Souls of Black Folk*, calling it "about the finest book I have ever read by a coloured man".[^3] Both men took part in the [Pan-African Conference](Atlas/Events/EVT-0001.md) of 1900.[^4]

## Evidence notes

- **Date.** Du Bois gives no year. His description fits the [Pan-African Conference](Atlas/Events/EVT-0001.md) of July 1900, which Richards notes was timed to precede the Paris Exhibition, but Du Bois does not name the conference.[^1][^5]
