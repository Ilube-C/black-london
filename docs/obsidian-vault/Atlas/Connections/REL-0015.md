*Henry Sylvester Williams conceived and organised the First Pan-African Conference, held at Westminster Town Hall in July 1900.*

[Henry Sylvester Williams](Atlas/People/PER-0003.md), secretary of the [African Association](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0001.md), conceived the idea of a world conference before the Association was formed.[^1] In March 1898 the Association agreed to issue a circular, signed by Williams and its president, announcing a conference two years ahead. By April 1900 Williams was general secretary of the conference committee.[^1] Clarke calls him the [conference](Atlas/Events/EVT-0001.md)'s "principal organiser", with "his extensive list of contacts in all corners of the Black Atlantic".[^2] The University of Edinburgh's timeline likewise names him as its organiser.[^3] The conference met at [Westminster Town Hall](Atlas/Places/PLC-0001.md) from 23 to 25 July 1900.[^2]
