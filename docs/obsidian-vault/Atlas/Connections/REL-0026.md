[George Padmore](Atlas/People/PER-0005.md) and [C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) were boyhood friends from Trinidad. Høgsbjerg writes that in May 1937 James "with his compatriot and boyhood friend, George Padmore, launched the International African Service Bureau" in London.[^1] Padmore was its chair and James its editorial director.[^2] Two years earlier both had sat on the executive of the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md), which James chaired.[^3] In 1937 Padmore passed to James the commission that became [*A History of Negro Revolt*](Atlas/Works/WRK-0016.md).[^4]

## Significance

Høgsbjerg writes that James developed his Pan-Africanism in Britain through his reading on the Haitian Revolution, "discussions with the likes of Padmore", and organising alongside African activists.[^5] Their partnership also linked James to [Kwame Nkrumah](Atlas/People/PER-0016.md): Adi and Sherwood write that Nkrumah came to Padmore in 1945 "recommended to Padmore from the USA by C.L.R. James".[^6]
