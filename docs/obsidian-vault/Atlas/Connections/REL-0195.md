*Jomo Kenyatta met Ladipo Solanke on arriving in London in 1929, when Solanke's West African Students' Union gave him hospitality; Adi describes them as friends.*

When [Jomo Kenyatta](Atlas/People/PER-0017.md) arrived in London on 8 March 1929, he carried the name of a West African organisation that had promised his Kikuyu Central Association hospitality. Murray-Brown writes that he went straight to its address. "Their leader was a Nigerian called Ladipo Solanke, then living at 57 Castletown Road, W14."[^1] Murray-Brown dates their meeting to 1929.[^1] A Colonial Office minute noted that "the West African Students, Mr. Solanke, help him over here".[^2] Adi writes of "Solanke's friendship with Jomo Kenyatta" and later calls Kenyatta one of "Solanke's old friends".[^3]

## Significance

The first contact did not go smoothly. A Colonial Office minute of the time recorded that the West African students were "dissatisfied and disappointed in him".[^4] Murray-Brown later wrote that Kenyatta "had been cautious towards other Africans ever since the days of Ladipo Solanke when he first arrived in England".[^5]

## Evidence notes

- **Where Kenyatta lodged.** Adi cites a Special Branch report of June 1929 which stated that both men were living in Marcus Garvey's house. That is the report's claim; the other sources consulted do not confirm it.[^6]
