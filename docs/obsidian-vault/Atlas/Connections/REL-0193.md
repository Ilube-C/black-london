*C. L. R. James and Amy Ashwood Garvey founded the International African Friends of Abyssinia together in 1935 and were both officers of the International African Service Bureau.*

[C. L. R. James](Atlas/People/PER-0014.md) met [Amy Ashwood Garvey](Atlas/People/PER-0004.md) through the [League of Coloured Peoples](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0019.md). As Italy prepared for war on Ethiopia, James remembered that both of them "felt that there ought to be an opposition" in Britain. Together they revived an earlier committee, which became the [International African Friends of Abyssinia](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0008.md) in 1935.[^1] James was its chairman and Ashwood Garvey its honorary treasurer.[^2] In the [International African Service Bureau](Atlas/Organisations/ORG-0009.md) of 1937, James was editorial director and Ashwood Garvey a vice-president.[^3] Høgsbjerg quotes James's memory that she was "a wonderful cook".[^4]
