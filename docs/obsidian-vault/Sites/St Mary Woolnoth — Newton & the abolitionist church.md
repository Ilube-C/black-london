# St Mary Woolnoth — Newton & the abolitionist church
> John Newton — former slave-ship captain turned abolitionist and author of 'Amazing Grace' — was rector here from 1780 to 1807, publishing his 'Thoughts upon the African Slave Trade' (1788) and mentoring William Wilberforce.
St Mary Woolnoth, a Nicholas Hawksmoor church at the junction of Lombard Street and King William Street, became one of the unlikeliest pulpits of the British abolitionist movement. John Newton (1725–1807) — a former captain of slave-trading voyages whose conversion produced the hymn *Amazing Grace* (1779) — was rector here from 1780 until his death.

In 1788 Newton published *Thoughts upon the African Slave Trade*, an unsparing first-hand account of the trade's atrocities written from this parish; it was distributed to every Member of Parliament. In 1785–86 he met privately with William Wilberforce, then a young MP wrestling with whether to leave politics; Newton's counsel kept Wilberforce in Parliament and pointed him at slavery.

Newton and his wife Mary were originally buried in the crypt; their bodies were reinterred at Olney in 1893 when the Bank Underground station was built beneath the church. The church remains a working parish and a quiet anchor of the City walk.
