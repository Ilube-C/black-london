# Francis Barber at Dr Johnson's House
> From 1752 Francis Barber lived in Samuel Johnson's household at 17 Gough Square — and was made the lexicographer's principal heir.
Francis Barber was born enslaved in Jamaica around 1742 and brought to England as a boy. He entered Samuel Johnson's household at 17 Gough Square in 1752, the year Johnson's wife Tetty died. Johnson treated him with extraordinary affection: he paid for his schooling at Bishop's Stortford Grammar, freed him in his will, and made him principal beneficiary of his estate.

Dr Johnson's House survives — a rare 17th-century townhouse in the City — and is open to the public. The Barber relationship is a quiet centrepiece of any honest tour.
