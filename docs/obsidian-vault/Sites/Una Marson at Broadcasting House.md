# Una Marson at Broadcasting House
> From 1941 Una Marson produced 'Calling the West Indies' and 'Caribbean Voices' from Broadcasting House — the BBC's first Black woman programme-maker.
Jamaican poet, journalist and broadcaster Una Marson joined the BBC in 1941 as a programme assistant on 'Calling the West Indies', a service for Caribbean troops serving in Britain. By 1943 she was producing 'Caribbean Voices', which over the next decade gave national air-time to V.S. Naipaul, Sam Selvon, Derek Walcott, George Lamming and many others — effectively launching post-war Caribbean literature in English.

She broke under the strain in 1946 and returned to Jamaica. Broadcasting House at Portland Place still stands.
