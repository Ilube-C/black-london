# Ignatius Sancho's grocery
> From 1774 the writer, composer and abolitionist Ignatius Sancho ran a grocery here — and from this address became the first Black Briton known to vote in a parliamentary election.
Charles Ignatius Sancho was born in 1729, almost certainly aboard a slave ship in the Atlantic, and brought to England as a small child. After years in service to the Duke and Duchess of Montagu — and an extraordinary self-education — he and his wife Anne opened a small grocery and tobacconist's at 19 Charles Street, Westminster, in 1774.

As a male householder paying parish rates, Sancho qualified for the parliamentary franchise, and in 1774 and again in 1780 he cast his vote — the earliest documented Black voter in British political history. His correspondence, posthumously published as *Letters of the Late Ignatius Sancho, an African* (1782), was a publishing sensation and a quiet, careful argument against the slave trade.

He died from gout at the shop on 14 December 1780; his obituary in the *Gentleman's Magazine* was the first known to be given to a Black Briton in the British press.
